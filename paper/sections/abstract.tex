\begin{abstract}
\noindent Exoplanets and constructed worlds with nitrogen--oxygen air, water as the principal condensable, and
broadly terrestrial surface pressures and temperatures that permit liquid water can differ substantially from
Earth in gravity, size, day or starlight. Even though these \emph{Gaean} worlds share many features with Earth,
Earth-tuned models and their descendants can still produce errors that go unnoticed until a world departs far
enough from Earth for them to grow large. Moreover, the models joined in an ensemble for a world's climate,
storms and seas were built separately and tuned differently from one another, so they have to be adapted to one
another before they describe one world. Constructed worlds can depart from Earth further than natural ones: by
one design's own models, a shield in space that removes the ultraviolet heating the upper air would keep
nitrogen--oxygen air under a gravity too weak to hold it naturally. As a start toward that ensemble,
ExoPlaSim~3.4.2, an Earth model extended to other worlds, and three codes built for Earth, CM1 release~22 with
WRF-ELEC's electrification, SWAN~41.51 and SWASH~12.01, were adapted to that design, the Open Moon, which departs
from Earth at once in gravity, column mass, day and scale height over radius. Their Earth values take four forms:
constants, units of time, geometry, and fits and simplifications used beyond their range; on the paths the lunar
runs take, the released codes also contain five defects, and by ExoPlaSim's own arithmetic three of them, though
they produce little or no error at Earth's values, bias its runs that meet their conditions, runs of slowly
rotating and tidally locked planets among them.\claim{H23}\claim{W06} Where a value was repaired, constants and
units of time mostly took general repairs built to hold on other worlds within the limits of their laws, the
radiative geometry took a method whose table is computed from the world's own column, and the radiation's gas and
scattering fits took calibrations to an in-house line-by-line model and its correlated-$k$ emulation, each holding
near the columns it was fitted on. With the repairs in place, identity, reproduction, arithmetic,
gravity-similarity and Earth-benchmark checks test the code: patched, SWAN reproduces a sixfold gravity scaling of
an Earth sea to 0.3\% in wave height, where the released code misses it by up to 5.4\%.\claim{V04} Applied in the
lunar runs, the two general repairs, measured singly, moved the global-mean surface temperature by 1.6--2.5~K,
while the first calibration of the radiation's Earth fits moved it by at least 20~K and the cloud's Earth-tuned
settings, varied in tests, by up to about 6~K.\claim{G01}\claim{G14}\claim{G05} Those two Earth fits are the next
to revisit. A radiation scheme built from the in-house model's line-by-line spectroscopy would replace the larger
of them for nitrogen--oxygen air with water and modest carbon dioxide, under the starlight it would be built for
and over the pressures and temperatures where that spectroscopy agrees with an independent code, a comparison
planned on one column of the test world. Water cloud, in turn, would be calibrated for each world against
cloud-resolving runs whose own microphysics is fitted on Earth, a fit still to be made on the test world.
\end{abstract}
