\begin{abstract}
\noindent Many of the general circulation models used for exoplanets were developed for Earth or for solar-system
planets, and on a world whose air and water resemble Earth's while its gravity, size, day or starlight differ, a
run that keeps a value set for Earth can complete with a plausible result. A constructed world can depart further:
by one design's own models, a shield in space that removes the ultraviolet heating the upper air would keep
Earth-like air under a gravity too weak to hold it naturally. ExoPlaSim~3.4.2, an Earth model extended to other
worlds, and three codes built for Earth, CM1 release~22 with WRF-ELEC's electrification, SWAN~41.51 and
SWASH~12.01, were adapted to such a world, a breathable Moon, which departs from Earth at once in gravity, column
mass, day and scale height over radius. Their Earth values take four forms: constants, units of time, geometry,
and fits and simplifications used beyond their range; the released codes also hold five defects, and by
ExoPlaSim's own arithmetic three of them, with little or no error at Earth's values, bias its runs that meet their
conditions, slowly rotating and tidally locked planets among them.\claim{H23}\claim{W06} Where a value was
repaired, constants and units of time mostly took general repairs built to hold on other worlds within the limits
of their laws, the radiative geometry took a method whose table is computed from the world's own column, and the
radiation's gas and scattering fits took calibrations to an in-house line-by-line model and its correlated-$k$
emulation, each holding near the columns it was fitted on. Identity, reproduction, arithmetic, gravity-similarity
and Earth-benchmark checks test the code: patched, SWAN reproduces a sixfold gravity scaling of an Earth sea to
0.3\% in wave height, where the released code misses it by up to 5.4\%.\claim{V04} The test world, whose climate
is unobserved, is the only world the repaired codes have run on away from Earth's values. There the two general
repairs measured singly moved the global-mean surface temperature by 1.6--2.5~K, while the first calibration of
the radiation's Earth fits moved it by at least 20~K and the cloud's Earth-tuned settings, varied in tests, by up
to about 6~K.\claim{G01}\claim{G14}\claim{G05} A radiation scheme built from the in-house model's line-by-line
spectroscopy would replace the largest of these fits for nitrogen--oxygen air with water and modest carbon
dioxide, under the starlight it is built for and over the pressures and temperatures where that spectroscopy
agrees with an independent code, a comparison planned on one column of the test world. Cloud would be a
calibration for each world, fitted to cloud-resolving runs, a fit still to be made on the test world.
\end{abstract}
