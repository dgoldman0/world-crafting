\begin{abstract}
\noindent Exoplanets and constructed worlds with nitrogen--oxygen air, water as the principal condensable, and
broadly terrestrial surface pressures and temperatures that permit liquid water can differ substantially from
Earth in gravity, size, day or starlight. Even though these \emph{Gaean} worlds share many features with Earth,
Earth-tuned models and their descendants can still produce errors that go unnoticed until a world departs far
enough from Earth for them to grow large. Moreover, the models joined in an ensemble for a world's climate,
storms and seas were built separately and tuned differently from one another, so they have to be adapted to one
another before they describe one world. Constructed worlds can depart from Earth further than natural ones: in
one design, the Open Moon, a shield in space that blocks the ultraviolet heating the upper air would let the Moon
keep breathable air, according to calculations of atmospheric escape made for that design.\claim{S15} That world
departs from Earth in gravity, column mass, day and scale height over radius at once, which makes it a test of
the Earth values that depend on them. As a start toward such an ensemble, ExoPlaSim, an Earth model extended to
other worlds, was adapted to the Open Moon with CM1 and WRF-ELEC's electrification for its storms and lightning,
and with SWAN and SWASH for its waves from the open sea to the shore. On the paths the lunar runs take, the released codes contain five defects; three of them produce little or no
error at Earth's values, yet by ExoPlaSim's own arithmetic they bias its runs that meet their conditions, runs of
slowly rotating and tidally locked planets among them, while one of the other two, in CM1's boundary layer, errs in
Earth's own air.\claim{H23}\claim{W06}\claim{H34}
Beyond the defects, the constants, units of time and geometry that the codes set for Earth were mostly given repairs
that hold on other worlds within the limits of their laws, or tables recomputed from each world's own column. The
codes' empirical fits, by contrast, hold near the columns they were fitted on, and in the lunar runs their effects on the climate were the largest, of the order of the spreads measured among exoplanet GCMs. Calibrating the radiation's
fits changed the global-mean surface temperature by at least 20~K, and varying the cloud's settings changed it by
up to about 6~K, against 0.7--2.5~K for the general repairs measured singly.\claim{G14}\claim{G05}\claim{G01}\claim{G19}
Between the models the rain and the upper air still differ: forced through the whole column with the GCM's ascent,
the cloud-resolving runs rain 75--84\% of the GCM's rain, part of it at other hours of the day, and runs that make
their own ascent rain more than the GCM at a land site and twice its rain at sea, so at those sites the two ways of
forcing them bracket the GCM's rain, and no run passes the agreement test set for
them.\claim{G21}\claim{G26}\claim{G28}\claim{G29} With the boxes' ascent settled and the GCM's convection closed against such runs, a
radiation scheme reduced with existing tools from line-by-line spectroscopy and cloud calibrated against
cloud-resolving runs of each world would replace the codes' fits, the spectroscopy first checked against an
independent code. A modeller running the ensemble could then follow the consequences of a world's gravity, day and starlight from its global climate into
the rain in its basins and the waves on its shores, whether that world was found around another star or designed.
With radiation and cloud built for other gases and condensables, the same could be done beyond the Gaean class.
\end{abstract}
