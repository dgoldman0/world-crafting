\begin{abstract}
\noindent An ensemble for \emph{terracompatible} worlds would connect global climate to storms and seas on
exoplanets and constructed worlds with nitrogen--oxygen air, water as the principal condensable, and broadly
terrestrial surface pressures and temperatures permitting liquid water. Their gravity, size, day or starlight
can differ substantially from Earth's, while a model that keeps a value set for Earth can complete with a
plausible result. A constructed world can depart further:
by one design's own models, a shield in space that removes the ultraviolet heating the upper air would keep
nitrogen--oxygen air under a gravity too weak to hold it naturally. As a start toward that ensemble,
ExoPlaSim~3.4.2, an Earth model extended to other
worlds, and three codes built for Earth, CM1 release~22 with WRF-ELEC's electrification, SWAN~41.51 and
SWASH~12.01, were adapted to such a world, a breathable Moon, which departs from Earth at once in gravity, column
mass, day and scale height over radius. Their Earth values take four forms: constants, units of time, geometry, and
fits and simplifications used beyond their range; on the paths the lunar runs take, the released codes also contain
five defects, and by ExoPlaSim's own arithmetic three of them, with little or no error at Earth's values, bias its
runs that meet their conditions, runs of slowly rotating and tidally locked planets among
them.\claim{H23}\claim{W06} Where a value was repaired, constants and units of time mostly took general repairs
built to hold on other worlds within the limits of their laws, the radiative geometry took a method whose table is
computed from the world's own column, and the radiation's gas and scattering fits took calibrations to an in-house
line-by-line model and its correlated-$k$ emulation, each holding near the columns it was fitted on. Identity,
reproduction, arithmetic, gravity-similarity and Earth-benchmark checks test the code: patched, SWAN reproduces a
sixfold gravity scaling of an Earth sea to 0.3\% in wave height, where the released code misses it by up to
5.4\%.\claim{V04} The test world, whose climate is unobserved, is the only world the repaired codes have simulated
away from Earth's values, numerical controls apart. There the two general repairs measured singly moved the
global-mean surface temperature by 1.6--2.5~K, while the first calibration of the radiation's Earth fits moved it
by at least 20~K and the cloud's Earth-tuned settings, varied in tests, by up to about
6~K.\claim{G01}\claim{G14}\claim{G05} A radiation scheme built from the in-house model's line-by-line spectroscopy
would replace the largest of these fits for nitrogen--oxygen air with water and modest carbon dioxide, under the
starlight it would be built for and over the pressures and temperatures where that spectroscopy agrees with an
independent code, a comparison planned on one column of the test world. Water cloud would be calibrated for each
world against cloud-resolving runs whose own microphysics is fitted on Earth, a fit still to be made on the test
world.
\end{abstract}
