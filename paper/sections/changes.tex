\section{Changes from the first version}

The first version (\code{paper-v1}, 19 October 2026) reported the ensemble as first adapted to the test world.
Since then the two atmospheric models have been compared budget by budget, and from that comparison this version
understands the ensemble's parts, the tie between them and the order of the work ahead differently. The paragraphs
below summarise those changes for a reader of either version, and Table~\ref{tab:changes} sets the altered
statements side by side.

The largest change is in the tie between the cloud-resolving model and the GCM. The first version held CM1's air
toward the GCM's above a height taken from Earth's practice and read what still separated the models, a lower
atmosphere several kelvin warmer in CM1 and a quarter of the GCM's rain, largely as a measure of the GCM's fits. Most of
that separation came from how CM1 was run and read: on the test world the hold covered most of the column that
convects, CM1's boundary layer carried a defect, its raindrops kept Earth's limits on their size, and its radiative
output, stored at constant volume, had been read as rates at constant
pressure.\claim{H39}\claim{H34}\claim{R18}\claim{V08} This version forces CM1 with the GCM's heating and moistening
through the whole column and judges the pair by an agreement test fixed before the runs.\claim{R14}\claim{G20} So
forced, CM1 rains most of the GCM's rain, and the models part in the timing of the rain over the sea and in the
temperature of the upper air.\claim{G21} The way a box receives its large-scale ascent moves its rain by more than
the models differ, since boxes that make their own ascent rain on the other side of the GCM's rain; the difference
between the models is therefore given as a range between the two couplings, and a change to either model waits on
evidence apart from their comparison.\claim{G26}\claim{G29}

The GCM's Earth values lie in more places than the first version found. That version placed them in PlaSim's
radiation, cloud, geometry and units of time. This one finds more in the physics of its lower air: a convection
scheme driven by the moisture supply, levels laid out for Earth's scale height, mixing lengths and a least wind
fixed in metres and metres per second, and an exchange over water sized for Earth's
gravity.\claim{H37}\claim{H38} The design case accordingly runs on levels laid out for the lunar scale height, with
convection that relaxes toward a moist adiabat, the gust and the similarity theory that CM1 applies over water,
rain evaporated from drop sizes and a cold cloud computed at lunar
gravity.\claim{R19}\claim{R20}\claim{R21}\claim{R22}\claim{R23} The convection's closure remains open and heads the
next steps, and two further Earth values, the damping times of the horizontal diffusion and the depth of the slab
sea, are named with their effects unmeasured.\claim{N05}\claim{H43}\claim{H44} The calibrations are read more
narrowly as well: a longwave correction fitted on one state misplaces the cooling of another, so each state, level
set and convection scheme takes a fit of its own.\claim{R25}

CM1 is now audited as the GCM is. The first version, having given CM1 the test world's gravity, Sun and soil, took
it as the resolved reference for the GCM. This version finds a defect in its simple boundary layer that errs in
Earth's air as well, a gust over water that omits gravity, and rates of rain formation that stay
Earth's;\claim{H34}\claim{H38}\claim{H45} its radiation, by contrast, agrees with the reference model once its
output is read at constant volume.\claim{V08} CM1's boxes become the GCM's target only once they make their own
ascent and their convection holds on finer grids (Conclusion).

The scenario has changed as well. Water added to a world loads its figure and turns it beneath its spin axis, a
known process that the first version left out of the scenario: the design case now runs on a Moon turned about
8\textdegree\ by its seas and lakes, and deriving a world's orientation from its loads has become the first step of
setting one up (Reach).\claim{S17}\claim{W07} The scenario's design temperature is 301~K, where the first version's
was 295~K, and the sunlight that holds it is set with the night's twilight counted once, after a fault of the
adaptation that counted it twice, and with the equinox regressing with the orbit's node.\claim{G27}\claim{H40}

The inventory of defects and Earth values is longer. The released codes hold six defects on the lunar runs' paths,
where the first version counted four: the two added are the fault in CM1's boundary layer and a humidity that
ExoPlaSim's mean output holds from the start of each interval.\claim{H34}\claim{H42} Since several of these faults
left runs that looked plausible on their own, the set-up of a further world closes each model's budgets against its
own surface and against a second model, and examines a design climate after each retune for where its heat goes
(Reach).

The work ahead is ordered differently as a result. The first version's next steps began with PlaSim's longwave
correction at the settled state and with its cloud, to be calibrated against CM1's. This version's begin where the
two models now part: the ascent of the boxes, the closure of the GCM's convection toward the free troposphere such
boxes support, and the temperature of the upper air; cloud and a radiation scheme reduced from the reference
spectroscopy follow, and single runs would size each Earth value whose effect is unmeasured (Next
steps).\claim{N05}\claim{N06} The CM1 results that the first version drew from rings held from 8~km, on a slice's
direction, on the storms and on their lightning, stand under that condition until the rings are rerun, and its
figure of the gap between the models at 45\textdegree~N is withdrawn until then (Results).
