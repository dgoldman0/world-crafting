\section{Changes from the first version}

The first version (\code{paper-v1}, 19 October 2026) reported the ensemble as first adapted to the test world.
Since then CM1 has been forced through the whole column and read against the GCM budget by budget, a comparison
that corrected the first version's account of both models, and the design case has moved to a GCM with refined
physics on a Moon turned by its water. Table~\ref{tab:changes} sets beside each other the statements in which the
two versions differ.

Beyond these corrections, this version adds an agreement test fixed before the boxes ran, boxes that make their own
large-scale ascent, and the orientation that a world's seas and lakes give it (Reach).\claim{G20}\claim{G26}\claim{W07}
Its inventory of Earth values gains PlaSim's convection, levels, mixing lengths, exchange over water, horizontal
diffusion and slab sea, and CM1's gust over water and formation of rain.\claim{H37}\claim{H38}\claim{H43}\claim{H44}\claim{H45}

The first version's results on a slice's direction, on the storms and on their lightning stand, now under the
condition that their runs held CM1's air from 8~km and carried the boundary layer's fault (Results). Its figure of
the gap between the models at 45\textdegree~N is withdrawn until that ring is rerun, and the histories of single
runs that it told in places are kept in the code repository's notes.
