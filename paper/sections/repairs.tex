\section{Repairs}

The repairs follow one rule where they can: a value that held Earth becomes a parameter whose default
reproduces the released code, so that the change can be switched off and compared with the original.
Several changes depart from the rule, and Table~\ref{tab:repairs} marks them. CM1's deeper soil and its
zeroed trace gases and ozone act in every patched build; its 400~ppm default for carbon dioxide replaces the
calendar formula, and the electrified build's gravity defaults to 9.81 where the released NSSL scheme has
9.8; the beta plane's Coriolis force acts on departures from the base-state wind in beta-plane runs; the
electrified build caps the ice sizes its radiation reads; and SWAN's air density is compiled in. SWAN's
wind-input patch reproduces stock output only at standard gravity, 9.80665~m\,s$^{-2}$, set by input, and
the guard added to the water scaling is the identity only for spectra like this one. The value a lunar run
gives each parameter decides the repair's class. A general repair takes its value from physics that holds
on other worlds: a unit, a scaling law, a geometry, a missing process or the removal of a defect. A
calibration takes its value from a fit to the in-house reference model on the lunar design case. Beside the
two classes stand changes of other kinds: settings that carry the scenario's values, capabilities the
released codes lack, a bound where the physics is unsettled, a mitigation, and defects avoided by
configuration.

\textbf{Constants to parameters.} The ExoPlaSim repairs are ten patches to PlaSim's radiation and cloud
code, each adding a namelist switch whose default is the released behaviour, and three settings that carry
the Moon's orbit and calendar: the configured orbit held fixed, a year of 17,160 half-hour steps holding
twelve whole model days, and the synodic Sun clock. In CM1, gravity and the air's carbon dioxide and oxygen
become preprocessor constants that default to 9.81~m\,s$^{-2}$, 400~ppm and Earth's oxygen, and CM1's
twenty spare namelist reals carry the lunar Sun, the forcing and the electrification's settings. SWAN's
wind-input knee moves with gravity and stays at 1~Hz under standard gravity. Terluna's runners insert each
change into the installed source at a marked anchor; the accompanying repository holds them as patch series
against pinned upstream archives, and a script rebuilds from those archives, byte for byte, the deployed
ExoPlaSim copy and the Fortran of the eight lunar CM1 builds behind the current results, while older CM1
builds, the Earth-gravity benchmark builds among them, carry earlier states of the series.\claim{R01}

\textbf{Scaling laws.} Where gravity enters a fitted law, a similarity argument gives the repair. A
particle falling in the regime $V = aD^b$ has a Reynolds number that grows as the Best number to the power
$(b+1)/3$ \autocite{mitchell1996use}, and the Best number of a particle of fixed size is proportional to
$g$, so $V \propto g^{(b+1)/3}$: at lunar gravity cloud droplets fall 6.04 times slower, raindrops 2.94
times and graupel 2.27 times.\claim{R02} The scaling is exact for particles whose mass grows as $D^3$ and
area as $D^2$, drops, graupel and hail, within their fitted regimes; for snow and ice crystals, whose mass
and area follow other powers, the gravity exponent is larger than the patch's. The Morrison and NSSL schemes
carry the scaled speeds through their fall-speed laws and caps into collection and ventilation; in NSSL the
ventilation needed its own scaling at eleven sites.

Other lengths and times scale in the same way. CM1's Rayleigh damping rate and its boundary-layer length
scale as $g$ and $1/g$, as the dynamics stretch, and WRF-ELEC's sub-step lengthens from 0.75 to 6.8~s, which
keeps graupel settling through about 1\% of a layer per sub-step in the lunar charging zone's 2~km layers at
0.44 of Earth's fall speed; the boxes, rounding to whole sub-steps, run one 8~s sub-step per model
step.\claim{R03} The breakdown field already scales with the air's density, and lifting its
180~kV\,m$^{-1}$ cap lets that scaling act wherever lunar flashes start. SWAN's knee becomes a function of
$f\,g_\mathrm{E}/g$, which reproduces stock SWAN at standard gravity and restores the model's gravity
similarity.\claim{R04} The repairs of units and heights restore the quantity each formula was written for.
Slingo's cover counts rain per 86,400~s; CCM3's cloud water counts heights and precipitable water at Earth's
gravity and scales its mixing ratio by $g/g_\mathrm{E}$, which gives each cloud the water path it would hold
at the same pressures on Earth; and CM1's five soil layers thicken to 5--80~cm, 1.55~m in all, under the
month-long thermal wave.

\textbf{Geometry restored.} The curved geometry enters both models through an energy ledger of the design
column, computed once and offline against the same air in flat layers. The ledger traces sunlight from
360~nm to 5~\textmu m through 100 spherical shells and through the same layers taken flat, at solar
elevations from $-90$ to 90\textdegree, in 10,697 spectral channels. Each channel absorbs a direct beam
within 1\% of its sunlight as its members do and, beyond 1,250~nm, keeps the beam's loss above every layer;
below 500~nm each band is one channel. The 282 channels below 1,250~nm thin enough to scatter take spherical
successive orders of scattering; the rest take a direct beam through the shells with the diffuse light of a
pseudo-spherical two-stream, so their twilight lacks scattered light. The infrared escape is traced
through the same shells by impact parameter. The ledger's terms split the curved geometry into the widening
of the shells above each square metre of ground, worth $-6.5$~W\,m$^{-2}$ net, and the curved light and
escape, the twilight and the low Sun's side light, worth $+0.7$~W\,m$^{-2}$.\claim{R05} Tabulated at 167
solar elevations from $-40$ to 90\textdegree, the terms add sunlight to each PlaSim layer or CM1 shell and
the ground's share in the column's clear share, below the horizon as well as above, and remove each layer's
extra infrared escape scaled with temperature to the fourth power. PlaSim's slant factor becomes the Moon's,
$(\mu+a)/(\mu^2+b\mu+c)$ fitted to straight rays through the shells, within 0.30\% for water vapour and
0.08\% for well-mixed air (Figure~\ref{fig:slant}).\claim{R06} Part of the daylight term is a difference of
solvers: with the Sun overhead, where the geometry is flat, the spherical solver's ground still takes 1.3\%
less than the two-stream's, so the daytime term also corrects the reference column PlaSim was calibrated
against.\claim{R07}

\textbf{Calibration by height.} The calibrations fit PlaSim to the in-house radiative--convective model
and its correlated-$k$ emulation. The model is clear-sky and line-by-line, built on HITRAN lines of water,
carbon dioxide and oxygen as served in September 2026 (HITRAN2020 or a later edition), a re-implementation
of MT\_CKD~4.3, collision-induced absorption and Rayleigh scattering, with gravity falling as $GM/r^2$ and
a pseudo-spherical direct beam; the emulation reproduces its outgoing longwave within 0.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ at
288--290~K and within 1.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ for a saturated 330~K lunar column.\claim{R08} Each calibration was
forced by the residual of the one before. The first fitted totals, a Rayleigh multiplier and a weaker water
continuum on global-mean columns, which brought the clear-sky albedo within 0.007 and the outgoing longwave
within about 2~W\,m$^{-2}$. When the Sun-clock and orbit faults were found, a check of how each column split
its own arriving sunlight showed the air 7.7--8.2 points short; with the repair, a multiplier on the
near-infrared water absorptivity, refitted with the Rayleigh multiplier on eleven columns, brought it within
0.3 points rms.\claim{R09} By layer the fit still placed sunlight low: PlaSim put almost nothing above
85~km, where the line-by-line column absorbs 4.2~W\,m$^{-2}$. Three carbon dioxide and oxygen bands, each
fitted to the line-by-line beam's loss above every level within 0.13\% of its energy, and a refit of both
multipliers brought the layers within 0.97~W\,m$^{-2}$ rms of the reference, from 1.71
(Figure~\ref{fig:layers}).\claim{R10} The longwave showed why totals fall short. PlaSim's water continuum
had been fitted to the outgoing flux, which a scheme can match while misplacing its cooling by
10~W\,m$^{-2}$ in a layer. An extra escape to space for each layer and the ground, fitted over land and over
sea on about 2,100 cloud-free three-day means and scaled with temperature to the fourth power, removes each
layer's mean error and leaves 1--5~W\,m$^{-2}$ from column to column.\claim{R11}

\textbf{Processes added.} Some changes supply a process that Earth's usual runs can spare. Cloud colder than
$-38$\,\textdegree C takes Ebert and Curry's ice optics \autocite{ebert1992parameterization} through a
delta-scaled two-stream, with an effective radius from Kristjánsson and Mitchell's table
\autocite{kristjansson2000impact}, in place of liquid water's. WRF-ELEC's charge separation, discharge and
screening, which CM1 lacks, arrive by a graft: its NSSL scheme with charge on every hydrometeor; a Poisson
solver by Fourier transform across the periodic domain and a tridiagonal solve on CM1's stretched levels;
its cylindrical discharge \autocite{ziegler1994observed} and its three-dimensional discharge adapted from
\textcite{macgorman2001lightning}, on a domain that wraps round; and its screening layers, available and off
in the lunar runs.\claim{R12} To these the lunar runs add leakage of charge through a computed lunar
conductivity column and a leader's crossing to the ground at an internal field of 1~kV\,m$^{-1}$ at
sea-level density, scaled with the air's density, which places the ground strikes between the bounds set by
WRF-ELEC's stopping field and its $-7$\,\textdegree C rule; point discharge from the ground, added as an
option, ran in one test window and stays off.\claim{R13} PlaSim gains a downward infrared flux at the model
top, by day and by night, which carries any source above the air, a host planet's glow for a moon among
them, and CM1's radiation reads ice sizes capped at RRTMG's 140~\textmu m, with the excess logged.

\textbf{Forcing a slice of the GCM.} CM1 runs on rings along latitude circles and tilted great circles and
in boxes, each ring column with its own Sun (a box takes one) and, on tilted rings, its own Coriolis
parameter. It is nudged toward the GCM only above 8--16~km, so its boundary layer and convection stay free,
and the GCM's mean vertical wind enters CM1's own large-scale advection.\claim{R14} A heating and moistening
by local time and height supplies the day--night circulation a box lacks and, as a single local-time bin,
the transport across the circle a ring lacks; over terrain each column is nudged and forced at its own
height above sea level. SWAN takes the GCM's surface stress through Wu's drag law inverted to an equivalent
wind, so its friction velocity is the GCM's to within a few per cent, on spherical coordinates at the lunar
radius and with its frequency band scaled by $g/g_\mathrm{E}$; SWASH, unmodified, takes lunar gravity as an
input and SWAN's spectra at its boundary.\claim{R15}
