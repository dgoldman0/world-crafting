\section{Repairs}

Most repairs follow one rule: an Earth value or a defect becomes a parameter whose default is written to
reproduce the released code, so that a repair can be switched on for the world that needs it and its effect
measured against the original code. The changes that depart from the rule act in every build that includes
them or reproduce the released code only under a stated condition, and Table~\ref{tab:repairs} gives each
one's condition.

\textbf{Switches and builds.} The patch series in the code
repository record the changes behind the ten-level states and the CM1 runs forced from them, each against a pinned
upstream archive with its switch or condition, its default and the checks made on it; the changes of the refined
physics, and the builds of the boxes forced from it, are recorded with the runs. In PlaSim, eleven patches change the radiation, cloud and Sun code, each adding a namelist switch written
to reproduce the released code at its default; runs confirm that identity for five of them
(Table~\ref{tab:verification}), while the near-infrared water scaling reproduces the released code at its default
only for spectra like the design case's. In CM1 the switches have two other forms: gravity and the air's carbon
dioxide and oxygen become preprocessor constants that default to 9.81~m\,s$^{-2}$, 400~ppm and Earth's oxygen, and
CM1's twenty spare namelist reals hold the settings of the lunar Sun, the forcing, the electrification and the
curved-air terms.\claim{R01}

Whatever their switches, most repairs belong to three classes, which differ in what was fitted to the test
world. A general repair contains no coefficient fitted to the test world or chosen for it, since its values
follow from a unit, a scaling law, a parameterization fitted over a stated range or the code's own intent, and
its limits are therefore those of its laws (Table~\ref{tab:reach}). One step nearer the world, a general method
computes a quantity of the world's own column offline, and the patched codes apply it as computed, as they apply the curved-air terms, differences between spherical and flat solutions of a clear column of the test world's air. In a calibration, by contrast, a code's
own coefficients are adjusted until its output matches a reference on chosen columns or states of the test world:
the reference model for PlaSim's radiation, and CM1's boxes, air lifted at both gravities and a particle model's
snowflakes for its rain and cold cloud.
The gas-band change has a part of each of these last two kinds: its coefficients were fitted to the reference
beam's loss above every level of the 295~K column on which the curved-air table was built, a quantity computed for that column, and the Rayleigh and
water multipliers were then refitted with them until PlaSim's layers matched the reference, so the tables list it as a calibration. A second convention separates a repair from its setting: the code change that turns an
Earth value into a parameter is counted as the repair and the lunar value entered in it as a setting, so CM1's
gravity, Sun, carbon dioxide and oxygen are general repairs with lunar settings, while CM1's zeroed trace gases
and ozone and SWAN's air density, entered as fixed values in the code, are settings alone. Beyond the three
classes and the settings, the remaining kinds of change are a capability, a process the released codes lack; a
coupling, the use of one model's fields to force another; an avoidance, a configuration of a released code
chosen to keep the runs clear of a defect or an Earth default; a bound, a value chosen between two rules that
bracket an unsettled process; and a mitigation, a cap on what one scheme passes to another.

\textbf{Defects and Earth defaults removed or avoided.} Two of ExoPlaSim's four defects are removed in the code: the
patched PlaSim turns the Sun at the synodic rate continuously from the first step, a mode that assumes a
steady, non-synchronous rotation, and converts convective rain per 86,400~s. A third is avoided with
ExoPlaSim's own \code{fixedorbit} switch, whose documentation describes only the freezing of Milankovitch
variations; set, it makes PlaSim use the configured orbit. These three ExoPlaSim repairs are built for other
configurations of the released code as well, the synodic clock for any steady, non-synchronous rotation, the
unit for any model day, a synchronous one included, and the switch for any configured orbit, and the Sun clock
has been reported to ExoPlaSim's developers \autocite{goldman2026solang}.\claim{N02} The calendar's slip is avoided by
configuration in the same way: with a year of 17,160 half-hour steps, twelve whole model days, the ten output
means of each model day are written at the same places in every year, although the synodic Sun still shifts
3.2\textdegree\ a model day against those means, so analyses sort the output by the Sun's position. SWAN's
defect and its Earth default are likewise avoided by configuration, the uninitialised read of its breaker
parameter by keeping depth-induced breaking on in every run, and its default initial state, whose 2~s peak
period has Earth's dimensions, by starting from rest or from hotstart files. CM1's density fault, by contrast, is removed in the builds of
the boxes on the refined-physics state, whose simple boundary layer divides its tendencies by the air's density
before the solver applies them, a repair that holds in every run with $\code{ipbl} = 2$.\claim{R17} ExoPlaSim's
fourth defect, the humidity held in its mean output, stays in the model, and the analyses correct for it as they
read the output: each interval's mean humidity is taken as the average of a record and the next, and the first and
last records of each year's file are left out.\claim{R27} Two faults of uncertain cause stay open as well, the
parting of CM1's threaded runs and PlaSim's cold lowest level, so CM1 runs that must repeat use one thread, and analyses of the
lowest level screen the cold windows.

\textbf{Scaling laws.} Because the fall-speed laws depend on gravity through the balance of weight and drag, their
repair follows from a similarity argument. A particle falling in the regime $V = aD^b$ has a Reynolds number
that grows as the Best number to the power $(b+1)/3$ \autocite{mitchell1996use}, and the Best number of a
particle of fixed size is proportional to $g$, so $V \propto g^{(b+1)/3}$: at lunar gravity cloud droplets
fall 6.04 times slower, raindrops 2.94 times and graupel 2.27 times.\claim{R02} The scaling is exact for
particles whose mass grows as $D^3$ and area as $D^2$, as for drops, graupel and hail, within their fitted
regimes, whereas snow and ice crystals, whose mass and area follow other powers, have a larger gravity exponent than the patch gives them. The Morrison and NSSL schemes pass the scaled speeds through their
fall-speed laws and caps into collection and ventilation, although in NSSL the ventilation had to be scaled separately at eleven sites. Rain's own limits in Morrison's
scheme follow from a balance of surface tension against weight: the largest drop that survives its fall scales with
the capillary length \autocite{loftus2021physics}, so the patched scheme stretches the breakup lengths and the cap on rain's mean diameter by
$(9.81/g)^{1/2}$ and shifts the cap on its fall speed by $(g/9.81)^{1/4}$, with a refinement for the air's density
computed for gravities from the Moon's to Earth's; in a lunar box the scaled limits
nearly doubled the rain at the ground and cut the share of falling rain that evaporated from 86 to
48\%.\claim{R18}\claim{N03} Beyond the microphysics, CM1's Rayleigh damping rate and its boundary-layer length
are scaled with the sixfold stretch of the dry dynamics, by $g/g_\mathrm{E}$ and $g_\mathrm{E}/g$, and
WRF-ELEC's sub-step lengthens from 0.75 to 6.8~s, so that graupel settles through about 1\% of a layer per
sub-step in the 2~km layers of the lunar charging zone, at 0.44 of Earth's fall speed, though, with the graft's driver rounding each model step to whole sub-steps, the boxes run one 8~s sub-step per model step.\claim{R03} SWAN's repair is a similarity
scaling as well: the reduction above the knee takes $f\,g_\mathrm{E}/g$ in place of $f$, which moves the knee
to $g/g_\mathrm{E}$~Hz, reproduces stock SWAN at standard gravity and restores the model's gravity
similarity.\claim{R04}

Other general repairs remove an Earth value directly. CM1's gravity becomes one build constant for its
dynamics, its radiation, its CAPE diagnostic and both microphysics schemes, and its Sun is computed from a solar day, a flux and a starting hour angle read from spare namelist reals, with each column of a ring at its own longitude;
to match that Sun, CM1's five soil layers are thickened to 5--80~cm, 1.55~m in all, so that they reach below
the thermal wave of any solar day from a few Earth days to the Moon's. PlaSim's orbit regresses the equinox with the
orbit's node, a turn in 6,798 days counted from the run's first step, so that the Sun's distance keeps its year
while the tilt's seasons follow the eclipse year.\claim{R26} In the electrification grafted into CM1,
two limits set at Earth's values are removed: lifting WRF-ELEC's 180~kV\,m$^{-1}$ cap lets the breakdown field's
scaling with the air's density act wherever flashes start, however dense the air there, and in the patched
graft the flash log records each channel's reach from the charge the flash adds to the ions, where the
released code recorded it only above a charge threshold set for Earth's storms. Two more concern PlaSim's cloud.
The patched CCM3 formula counts heights and precipitable water at Earth's gravity and scales the mixing ratio by
$g/g_\mathrm{E}$, which gives each cloud the water path it would hold at the same pressures on
Earth,\claim{R16} while cloud colder than $-38$\,\textdegree C is given Ebert and Curry's parameterized ice
optics \autocite{ebert1992parameterization} through a delta-scaled two-stream, with an effective radius from
Kristjánsson and Mitchell's table \autocite{kristjansson2000impact}, in place of liquid water's. The
parameterization was, however, fitted to crystals of 10~\textmu m and larger, sizes the table gives only above
190~K, so in the coldest lunar cloud the optics are extrapolated in size.\claim{R12} Both codes take the convective
gust in Beljaars's form \autocite{beljaars1995parametrization}, $w_* = (g\,h\,B/\theta_v)^{1/3}$, from the boundary
layer's depth $h$ and the surface's buoyancy flux $B$ at the run's gravity: CM1's surface layer over water, as it
already computes it over land, and PlaSim's surface transfer over every surface, in place of the free-convection
term over water and the least wind of 1~m\,s$^{-1}$, with CM1's least wind of 0.1~m\,s$^{-1}$; the 1~m\,s$^{-1}$
stays as the least wind shear of PlaSim's vertical diffusion.
Over that water PlaSim also takes the similarity theory of CM1's surface layer, carried to the height of its lowest
level, two of whose constants stay those of Earth's air: the viscosity in the roughness of smooth flow and a cap of
2.85~mm on the roughness.\claim{R19}

\textbf{Curved air.} Both atmospheric models, PlaSim and CM1, take the curved geometry from the general method,
a table of curved-air terms computed for one clear column of the test world's air. For the 295~K column on which
the method was built, 1.2~atm of air with 205~kg\,m$^{-2}$ of water over a 294.9~K surface, the radiation of the
same air was traced once, offline, through 100 spherical shells
and through the same layers taken flat, at solar elevations from $-90$ to 90\textdegree, with the differences
tabulated. Sunlight from 360~nm to 5~\textmu m was traced in 10,697 spectral channels, whose construction the
appendix gives, the 282 weakly absorbing channels by spherical successive orders of scattering from an in-house
sky solver, and the rest by the pseudo-spherical approximation \autocite{dahlback1991new,kylling1995reliable}, a
direct beam through the shells with the diffuse light of a two-stream, which the code runs only with the Sun above
the horizon, so that their twilight lacks scattered light. The infrared escape, for its part, was traced through
the same shells by impact parameter. The differences are summed in two terms: the widening of the shells above
each square metre of ground, $-6.5$~W\,m$^{-2}$ net, and the curved light and escape, the twilight and the low
Sun's side light, $+0.7$~W\,m$^{-2}$.\claim{R05} Tabulated at 167 solar elevations from $-40$ to 90\textdegree,
the terms are applied in the patched codes to every column and hour, below the horizon as well as above: each
PlaSim layer and each CM1 level gains its tabulated sunlight, the ground gains its share scaled by the column's
clear-sky fraction, and each layer loses to space its extra infrared escape, scaled with temperature to the
fourth power. Fitted to straight rays through the same shells, PlaSim's slant factor becomes the Moon's,
$(\mu+a)/(\mu^2+b\mu+c)$, within 0.30\% for water vapour and 0.08\% for well-mixed air
(Figure~\ref{fig:slant}).\claim{R06} The daytime terms, however, include a difference of solvers as well as of
geometry: with the Sun overhead, where curvature leaves the beam's path unchanged, the spherical solver's ground
still receives 1.3\% less sunlight than the two-stream's, so by day the terms also shift PlaSim's sunlight from the two-stream reference that PlaSim was calibrated against toward the successive-orders solver's.\claim{R07} The design case takes the terms the same method computes on a 301~K column with 331~kg\,m$^{-2}$ of
water, in 11,559 channels, where they sum to a net loss of 8.0~W\,m$^{-2}$, the shells' widening losing 8.7 and the
curved light gaining 0.7; it keeps the 295~K column's slant factors, against which the shortwave calibration was
fitted, and applies each layer's terms in the share of its column clear of cloud above it, a refinement that cools
the climate by about 1~K, while the ground's twilight is taken at a ground albedo of 0.1, whatever the ground's own, 11\% too strong
over the turned run's land and 3\% too weak over its sea, and 1.5--4.5 times too strong over snow or sea ice, which
that run never holds.\claim{R24} Since the table
is computed for one column, for another world it is recomputed from that world's own column and starlight, and how close to
the horizon a flat treatment holds there depends on the height of the scattering layer against the radius: the
plane-parallel codes that separate surface and aerosol in remote sensing hold typically to about
80\textdegree\ from the zenith on Mars, and to about 65\textdegree\ on Titan, whose haze has a scale height of
about 80~km, 3\% of its radius \autocite{vincendon2010spherical}. Even on Earth, a spherical atmosphere intercepts
2.5~W\,m$^{-2}$ more sunlight than flat layers in the global mean and absorbs 1.5~W\,m$^{-2}$ more of it
\autocite{prather2019round}.

\textbf{Calibration against the reference.} PlaSim was calibrated against the reference model and its
correlated-$k$ emulation \autocite{lacis1991description}. The reference is clear-sky and line-by-line, built on
HITRAN lines of water, carbon dioxide and oxygen, a re-implementation of MT\_CKD \autocite{mlawer2023inclusion} at version~4.3,
HITRAN's collision-induced absorption \autocite{karman2019update}, in its files of 2018--2024, and Rayleigh
scattering,
with gravity falling as $GM/r^2$ and a pseudo-spherical direct beam; its emulation reproduces the outgoing
longwave within 0.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ at 288--290~K and within 1.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ for a saturated 330~K lunar
column.\claim{R08} Each calibration was fitted to the residual of the one before. The first fitted a Rayleigh
multiplier and a weaker water continuum to two totals on global-mean columns, bringing the clear-sky albedo
within 0.007 and the outgoing longwave within about 2~W\,m$^{-2}$. The second refitted the Rayleigh multiplier with a multiplier on the near-infrared water
absorptivity on eleven columns and brought the share of arriving sunlight absorbed in the air from 7.7--8.2
percentage points short to within 0.3 points rms.\claim{R09} By layer the fit still placed sunlight low: on the
295~K column, PlaSim put almost nothing above 85~km, where the reference absorbs
4.2~W\,m$^{-2}$. In a third step, three carbon dioxide and oxygen bands, each fitted to the reference beam's loss
above every level within 0.13\% of its energy, and a refit of both multipliers brought the layers within
0.97~W\,m$^{-2}$ rms of the reference, from 1.71 (Figure~\ref{fig:layers}).\claim{R10} In the longwave, the
first calibration had fitted PlaSim's water continuum to the outgoing flux, which a scheme can match while
misplacing its cooling by 10~W\,m$^{-2}$ in a layer. To place that cooling by layer, an extra escape to space
for each layer and for the ground, fitted over land and over sea on about 2,100 cloud-free three-day means of
the corrected-curved-air state and scaled with temperature to the fourth power, removes each layer's mean error on that state
and leaves 1--5~W\,m$^{-2}$ from column to column.\claim{R11} A correction fitted on one state errs on another,
by up to 6.8~W\,m$^{-2}$ in a layer when the 295~K fit is run at 301~K, so the correction is refitted for each state, level
set and convection scheme.\claim{R25}

\textbf{Levels, convection and rain in the GCM.} The design case runs PlaSim on fifteen $\sigma$ levels laid out for
the test world's scale height, which put the lowest level near 0.3~km and the next near 1.2~km, where the released
ten levels give the lowest layer the first 1.73~km.\claim{R20} On these levels a simplified Betts--Miller scheme
\autocite{frierson2007dynamics} replaces the Kuo-type one: it relaxes a convecting column toward a moist adiabat, and its humidity toward 0.7 of
saturation, over two hours, and treats convection spanning more than 200~hPa as deep, a depth test set in pressure
that spans 16\% of the lunar surface pressure and would need scaling for a world at another pressure; the same
200~hPa above a shallow cloud's base caps its top in place of the released 700~hPa. The two hours are the control
value of Frierson's Earth aquaplanet; stretched sixfold with the dry dynamics, as CM1's damping time and mixing
length are, they become 12~h, and the effect of the relaxation time alone is unmeasured.\claim{H46} The scheme and
the closures tried with it remain under test, since at every box the GCM's rain departs from CM1's beyond the
tolerances of the test set out under Results.\claim{R21} Falling rain evaporates from drop sizes at the world's fall speeds in place of
PlaSim's constant: its drops start at mean diameters fitted to the lunar CM1 boxes and to lunar snow, 0.44~mm for
rain formed in warm cloud and 2.95~mm for rain melted from snow, grow by self-collection, break up at a size scaled
with the capillary length and fall by laws computed at the world's gravity and air, so that the scheme's structure
is general and its starting sizes a calibration.\claim{R22} Two further calibrations fit PlaSim's cold cloud to the
lunar column: factors on the water and crystal size of cloud colder than $-38$\,\textdegree C in its top three
layers, computed from slabs of air lifted through those layers at lunar and at Earth gravity, and a sublimation of
falling snow taken from the snowflakes that McSnow, a particle model of ice growth \autocite{brdar2018mcsnow},
grows at lunar gravity, fitted on two CM1 boxes.\claim{R23}

\textbf{Processes and capabilities added.} WRF-ELEC's charge separation, discharge and screening, which CM1
lacks, were grafted into it: the NSSL scheme with charge on every hydrometeor; a Poisson solver by Fourier
transform across the periodic domain and a tridiagonal solve on CM1's stretched levels; WRF-ELEC's cylindrical
discharge \autocite{ziegler1994observed} and its three-dimensional discharge adapted from
\textcite{macgorman2001lightning}, on a domain that wraps round; and its screening layers, available and off in
the lunar runs.\claim{R12} For runs that last weeks, the graft was given leakage of charge through a
conductivity column computed for the lunar air. Ground strikes had to be bounded, because WRF-ELEC's two Earth
rules bracket them widely on the Moon: under its stopping field every lunar channel stops 15--25~km up, while
its $-7$\,\textdegree C rule alone counts every channel that reaches that level. Between them, the bound counts a
downward channel that reaches the $-7$\,\textdegree C level as a strike only if its potential can drive a leader the rest of the way down, at an internal field of 1~kV\,m$^{-1}$ at sea-level density, scaled with the
air's density. Point discharge from the ground was added to the graft as well, as an option that stays off in the lunar runs.\claim{R13} For the graft's ice a mitigation was added: where the ice outgrows RRTMG's optics, CM1's radiation reads sizes capped at 140~\textmu m and logs the excess. PlaSim, too, is given a
capability, a broadband downward infrared flux at the model top, by day and by night, for a source above the
air, such as a giant host planet's glow on a moon.

\textbf{Forcing from the GCM.} Running CM1 inside the GCM's climate adds structural limits to the Earth values
in the code: a ring of columns along a latitude circle lacks the transport across the circle, and a box lacks
any day--night circulation larger than itself; stock CM1 also lacks a lunar Sun, forcing above its boundary
layer and nudging that follows terrain; a box forced with the advection along the rings alone rained in the mornings and
stayed clear and hot in the afternoons.\claim{H32} CM1 receives what the slice lacks from the GCM's fields, which the GCM computed with the Earth values that remain in it. For the two structural absences, a
heating and moistening by local time and height, taken from the GCM, imposes the effect of the day--night
circulation a box lacks and, as a single local-time bin, of the transport across the circle a ring lacks. The
absences of stock CM1 are filled in turn: each ring column has its own Sun, a box one Sun for all its columns,
and on tilted rings each column has its own Coriolis parameter, acting on departures from the base-state wind;
CM1's own large-scale advection uses the GCM's mean vertical wind; and over terrain each column is
nudged and forced at its own height above sea level. In the boxes forced from the refined-physics state, the heating and
moistening are the GCM's own, the storage of its column less its physics, taken through the whole column, and
CM1's temperature and vapour are held to the GCM's only above the top of the GCM's convection, the lowest level
above which it forms less than 1\% of its convective rain: that top stands near 73~km at the boxes' sites, and the
hold begins near 94~km and is full near 131~km, so that CM1's temperature, moisture and convection develop freely
through the column in which the GCM convects. Their wind is pulled toward the GCM's mean vector by local time over two hours from
the GCM's lowest level, near 0.3~km, up, the GCM's air is drawn in where the mean wind converges, and a sea box's
surface is held at the site's mean temperature.\claim{R14} Beyond the storms, the GCM forces the seas as well: SWAN is driven by the GCM's surface stress, converted through Wu's drag law inverted to an
equivalent wind, so that its friction velocity is within $-2.5$ to $+1.2$\% of the GCM's, on spherical
coordinates at the lunar radius and with its frequency band scaled by $g/g_\mathrm{E}$, and SWASH, run
unmodified, takes lunar gravity as an input and SWAN's spectra at its boundary.\claim{R15} Over Titan's polar
lakes, a Titan GCM's winds likewise force a wave model, PlanetWaves \autocite{detelich2026modeling}, adapted from an
Earth model to other planets' gravity, air and liquids \autocite{schneck2026modeling}. Since the methods that force CM1
impose only the GCM's fields and the slice's geometry, they are built for a slice of any world's GCM whose
convection top is known.
