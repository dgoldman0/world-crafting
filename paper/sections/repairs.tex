\section{Repairs}

Most repairs follow one rule: an Earth value or a defect becomes a parameter whose default is written to
reproduce the released code, so that a repair can be switched on for the world that needs it and its effect
measured against the original code. The changes that depart from the rule act in every build that includes
them or reproduce the released code only under a stated condition, and Table~\ref{tab:repairs} gives each
one's condition. The source changes are recorded as patch series against pinned upstream archives, each with
its switch or condition, its default and the checks made on it, and the settings of the released codes on
which the runs depend are listed with them.

The three classes of repair differ in what was fitted to the test world. A general repair contains no
coefficient fitted to the test world or chosen for it: its values follow from a unit, a scaling law, a
parameterization fitted over a stated range or the code's own intent, and its limits are those of its laws
(Table~\ref{tab:reach}). A general method computes a quantity of the world's own column offline and enters it
as computed, as the curved-air terms enter as differences between spherical and flat solutions of a design
column. In a calibration, PlaSim's own coefficients are adjusted until its output matches the reference model
on chosen columns of the test world. The gas-band change has a part of each of the last two kinds: its
coefficients were fitted to the reference beam's loss above every level of the design column, a quantity
computed for that column, and the Rayleigh and water multipliers were then refitted with them until PlaSim's
layers matched the reference, so the tables class it as a calibration. The code change that turns an Earth
value into a parameter is counted as the repair and the lunar value entered in it as a setting, so CM1's
gravity, Sun, carbon dioxide and oxygen are general repairs with lunar settings, while CM1's zeroed trace
gases and ozone and SWAN's air density, entered as fixed values in the code, are settings alone. The remaining
kinds are a capability, a process the released codes lack; a coupling, the use of one model's fields to force
another; an avoidance, a configuration of a released code chosen to keep the runs clear of a defect or an
Earth default; a bound, a value chosen between two rules that bracket an unsettled process; and a mitigation,
a cap on what one scheme passes to another.

\textbf{Defects and Earth defaults removed or avoided.} Two of ExoPlaSim's defects are removed in the code: the
patched PlaSim turns the Sun at the synodic rate continuously from the first step, a mode that assumes a
steady, non-synchronous rotation, and converts convective rain per 86,400~s. The third is avoided with
ExoPlaSim's own \code{fixedorbit} switch, whose documentation describes only the freezing of Milankovitch
variations; set, it makes PlaSim use the configured orbit. With a year of 17,160 half-hour steps, twelve whole
model days, the ten output means of each model day fall at the same places in every year; the synodic Sun
shifts 3.2\textdegree\ a model day against those means, so analyses sort the output by the Sun's position.
SWAN's uninitialised read of its breaker parameter is avoided by keeping depth-induced breaking on in every
run, and its default initial state, whose 2~s peak period has Earth's dimensions, by starting from rest or from
hotstart files. The thread race and PlaSim's cold lowest level stay open: CM1 runs that must repeat use one
thread, and analyses of the lowest level screen the cold windows. The three ExoPlaSim repairs are built for
other configurations of the released code as well, the synodic clock for any steady, non-synchronous rotation,
the unit for any model day, a synchronous one included, and the switch for any configured orbit; the Sun clock
has been reported to ExoPlaSim's developers \autocite{goldman2026solang}.

\textbf{Switches and builds.} The lunar runs were made with runners, scripts that configure, compile and run
each model and insert each change into the installed source at a marked anchor. Ten patches change PlaSim's
radiation, cloud and Sun code, each adding a namelist switch written to reproduce the released code at its
default; runs confirm that identity for five of them (Table~\ref{tab:verification}), and the near-infrared
water scaling reproduces the released code at its default only for spectra like the design case's. In CM1,
gravity and the air's carbon dioxide and oxygen become preprocessor constants that default to
9.81~m\,s$^{-2}$, 400~ppm and Earth's oxygen, and CM1's twenty spare namelist reals hold the lunar Sun, the
forcing and the electrification's settings. The patch series in the code repository were generated from the
runners' edit lists.\claim{R01}

\textbf{Scaling laws.} In the fall-speed laws gravity enters through the balance of weight and drag, and the
repair follows from a similarity argument. A particle falling in the regime $V = aD^b$ has a Reynolds number
that grows as the Best number to the power $(b+1)/3$ \autocite{mitchell1996use}, and the Best number of a
particle of fixed size is proportional to $g$, so $V \propto g^{(b+1)/3}$: at lunar gravity cloud droplets
fall 6.04 times slower, raindrops 2.94 times and graupel 2.27 times.\claim{R02} The scaling is exact for
particles whose mass grows as $D^3$ and area as $D^2$, as for drops, graupel and hail, within their fitted
regimes; snow and ice crystals, whose mass and area follow other powers, take a larger gravity exponent than
the patch gives them. The Morrison and NSSL schemes pass the scaled speeds through their fall-speed laws and
caps into collection and ventilation, and in NSSL the ventilation needed its own scaling at eleven sites. With
the sixfold stretch of the dry dynamics, CM1's Rayleigh damping rate and its boundary-layer length are scaled
by $g/g_\mathrm{E}$ and $g_\mathrm{E}/g$, and WRF-ELEC's sub-step lengthens from 0.75 to 6.8~s, so that
graupel settles through about 1\% of a layer per sub-step in the 2~km layers of the lunar charging zone, at
0.44 of Earth's fall speed; the boxes, rounding to whole sub-steps, run one 8~s sub-step per model
step.\claim{R03} In SWAN the reduction above the knee takes $f\,g_\mathrm{E}/g$ in place of $f$, which moves the
knee to $g/g_\mathrm{E}$~Hz, reproduces stock SWAN at standard gravity and restores the model's gravity
similarity.\claim{R04}

Other general repairs remove an Earth value directly. CM1's gravity becomes one build constant for its
dynamics, its radiation, its CAPE diagnostic and both microphysics schemes, and its Sun follows a solar day, a
flux and a starting hour angle read from spare namelist reals, with each column of a ring at its own
longitude. Lifting WRF-ELEC's 180~kV\,m$^{-1}$ cap lets the breakdown field's scaling with the air's density act
wherever flashes start, however dense the air there. The patched CCM3 formula counts heights and precipitable
water at Earth's gravity and scales the mixing ratio by $g/g_\mathrm{E}$, which gives each cloud the water path
it would hold at the same pressures on Earth. CM1's five soil layers are thickened to 5--80~cm, 1.55~m in all,
so that they reach below the thermal wave of any solar day from a few Earth days to the Moon's. In the patched
graft the flash log records each channel's reach from the charge the flash hands to the ions, where the
released code recorded it only above a charge threshold set for Earth's storms.\claim{R16} Cloud colder than
$-38$\,\textdegree C is given Ebert and Curry's parameterized ice optics \autocite{ebert1992parameterization}
through a delta-scaled two-stream, with an effective radius from Kristjánsson and Mitchell's table
\autocite{kristjansson2000impact}, in place of liquid water's; the parameterization was fitted to crystals of
10~\textmu m and larger, sizes the table gives only above 190~K, so in the coldest lunar cloud the optics are
extrapolated in size.\claim{R12}

\textbf{Curved air.} Both models read the curved geometry from a table of curved-air terms computed for one clear
design column, 1.2~atm of air with 205~kg\,m$^{-2}$ of water over a 294.9~K surface: the radiation of the same air
traced once, offline, through 100 spherical shells and through the same layers taken flat, at solar elevations from
$-90$ to 90\textdegree, with the differences tabulated. Sunlight from 360~nm to 5~\textmu m was traced in 10,697
spectral channels, the 282 thin enough to scatter by spherical successive orders of scattering from an in-house sky
solver, and the rest by a direct beam through the shells with the diffuse light of a pseudo-spherical two-stream,
so that their twilight lacks scattered light; the infrared escape was traced through the same shells by impact
parameter, and the appendix gives the channels' construction. The differences are summed in two terms: the widening
of the shells above each square metre of ground, $-6.5$~W\,m$^{-2}$ net, and the curved light and escape, the
twilight and the low Sun's side light, $+0.7$~W\,m$^{-2}$.\claim{R05} Tabulated at 167 solar elevations from $-40$
to 90\textdegree, the terms are applied in the patched codes to every column and hour, below the horizon as well as
above: each PlaSim layer and each CM1 level gains its tabulated sunlight, the ground gains its share scaled by the
column's clear-sky fraction, and each layer loses to space its extra infrared escape, scaled with temperature to
the fourth power. PlaSim's slant factor becomes the Moon's, $(\mu+a)/(\mu^2+b\mu+c)$ fitted to straight rays
through the shells, within 0.30\% for water vapour and 0.08\% for well-mixed air
(Figure~\ref{fig:slant}).\claim{R06} With the Sun overhead, where curvature leaves the beam's path unchanged, the
spherical solver's ground still receives 1.3\% less sunlight than the two-stream's, so by day the terms also move
PlaSim from the two-stream reference it was calibrated against toward the successive-orders solver.\claim{R07} For
another world the table is recomputed from that world's own column. How close to the horizon a flat treatment holds
depends on the height of the scattering layer against the radius: the plane-parallel codes that separate surface
and aerosol in remote sensing hold typically to about 80\textdegree\ from the zenith on Mars, and to about
65\textdegree\ on Titan, whose haze has a scale height of about 80~km, 3\% of its radius
\autocite{vincendon2010spherical}.

\textbf{Calibration against the reference.} PlaSim was calibrated against the reference model and its
correlated-$k$ emulation. The reference is clear-sky and line-by-line, built on HITRAN lines of water, carbon
dioxide and oxygen, a re-implementation of MT\_CKD~4.3, collision-induced absorption and Rayleigh scattering,
with gravity falling as $GM/r^2$ and a pseudo-spherical direct beam; its emulation reproduces the outgoing
longwave within 0.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ at 288--290~K and within 1.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ for a saturated 330~K lunar
column.\claim{R08} Each calibration was fitted to the residual of the one before. The first fitted a Rayleigh
multiplier and a weaker water continuum to two totals on global-mean columns, bringing the clear-sky albedo
within 0.007 and the outgoing longwave within about 2~W\,m$^{-2}$. The second, made with the removal of the
Sun-clock and orbit defects, refitted the Rayleigh multiplier with a multiplier on the near-infrared water
absorptivity on eleven columns and brought the share of arriving sunlight absorbed in the air from 7.7--8.2
percentage points short to within 0.3 points rms.\claim{R09} By layer the fit still placed sunlight low: on the
design column of the curved-air table, PlaSim put almost nothing above 85~km, where the reference absorbs
4.2~W\,m$^{-2}$. Three carbon dioxide and oxygen bands, each fitted to the reference beam's loss above every
level within 0.13\% of its energy, and a refit of both multipliers brought the layers within
0.97~W\,m$^{-2}$ rms of the reference, from 1.71 (Figure~\ref{fig:layers}).\claim{R10} In the longwave, the first
calibration had fitted PlaSim's water continuum to the outgoing flux, which a scheme can match while
misplacing its cooling by 10~W\,m$^{-2}$ in a layer. An extra escape to space for each layer and for the
ground, fitted over land and over sea on about 2,100 cloud-free three-day means of the design state and scaled
with temperature to the fourth power, removes each layer's mean error on that state and leaves
1--5~W\,m$^{-2}$ from column to column; it runs only in the longwave test.\claim{R11}

\textbf{Processes and capabilities added.} WRF-ELEC's charge separation, discharge and screening, which CM1
lacks, were grafted into it: the NSSL scheme with charge on every hydrometeor; a Poisson solver by Fourier
transform across the periodic domain and a tridiagonal solve on CM1's stretched levels; WRF-ELEC's cylindrical
discharge \autocite{ziegler1994observed} and its three-dimensional discharge adapted from
\textcite{macgorman2001lightning}, on a domain that wraps round; and its screening layers, available and off in
the lunar runs.\claim{R12} For runs that last weeks, the graft was given leakage of charge through a
conductivity column computed for the lunar air, and a condition for ground strikes: a downward channel that
reaches the $-7$\,\textdegree C level counts as a strike only if its potential can carry a leader the rest of
the way down, at an internal field of 1~kV\,m$^{-1}$ at sea-level density, scaled with the air's density. The
condition places the ground strikes between WRF-ELEC's two Earth rules, its stopping field, under which every
lunar channel stops 15--25~km up, and its $-7$\,\textdegree C rule alone; point discharge from the ground,
added as an option, ran in one test window and stays off.\claim{R13} Where the graft's ice outgrows RRTMG's
optics, CM1's radiation reads sizes capped at 140~\textmu m and logs the excess. PlaSim is given a broadband
downward infrared flux at the model top, by day and by night, for a source above the air, such as a giant host
planet's glow on a moon.

\textbf{Forcing from the GCM.} Running CM1 inside the GCM's climate adds structural limits to the Earth values
in the code: a ring of columns along a latitude circle lacks the transport across the circle, and a box lacks
any day--night circulation larger than itself; stock CM1 also lacks a lunar Sun, forcing above its boundary
layer and nudging that follows terrain. Two of these absences showed in the runs. The first box, forced with
the advection along the rings alone, rained in the mornings and stayed clear and hot in the afternoons until
it was stopped at day 47, and at 45\textdegree\,N the missing transport across the circle accounts for about a
third of CM1's warm offset from the GCM.\claim{H32} CM1 receives what the slice lacks from the GCM's fields,
and with those fields the Earth values that remain in the GCM. Each ring column has its own Sun, a box one Sun
for all its columns, and on tilted rings each column has its own Coriolis parameter, acting on departures from
the base-state wind; CM1 is nudged toward the GCM only above 8--16~km, so that its boundary layer and
convection develop freely, and the GCM's mean vertical wind enters CM1's own large-scale
advection.\claim{R14} A heating and moistening by local time and height, taken from the GCM, imposes the
effect of the day--night circulation a box lacks and, as a single local-time bin, of the transport across the
circle a ring lacks; over terrain each column is nudged and forced at its own height above sea level. SWAN is
forced by the GCM's surface stress, converted through Wu's drag law inverted to an equivalent wind, so that
its friction velocity lies within $-2.5$ to $+1.2$\% of the GCM's, on spherical coordinates at the lunar
radius and with its frequency band scaled by $g/g_\mathrm{E}$; SWASH runs unmodified, with lunar gravity as an
input and SWAN's spectra at its boundary.\claim{R15} The methods impose only the GCM's fields and the slice's
geometry, and are built for a slice of any world's GCM.
