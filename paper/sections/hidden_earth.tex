\section{Where Earth hides in the models}

A model carries Earth wherever one of its values or forms agrees with Earth because Earth set it, and
such a part, run on the Moon, returns Earth's answer to a lunar question. Table~\ref{tab:inventory}
lists every instance found in the four models with its form and its state at Terluna commit 186b294; the
defect log in the accompanying repository gives each one's discovery, evidence, repair and measured
effect. The inventory separates two kinds. In a limitation the code does what it was written to do, on
the world it was written for. In a defect the code is wrong on its own terms, on every world, and Earth's
values or the configurations usual on Earth kept the error too small or too rare to see. Four of the five
forms are limitations; the fifth holds the released codes' defects.

\textbf{Constants written into the code.} A dimensional constant carries the scale of the world it was
set on, and gravity is the commonest. CM1 release 22 sets it to 9.81~m\,s$^{-2}$ in four places: its
dynamics, the conversion of pressure to layer mass in its RRTMG radiation \autocite{iacono2008radiative}, its CAPE diagnostic and the Morrison microphysics,
whose fall-speed laws $V = aD^b$ and their caps hold Earth's gravity in their coefficients.\claim{H01}
WRF-ELEC's NSSL scheme sets 9.8 twice and ties the ventilation of evaporating rain and depositing ice to
Earth's fall-speed coefficients.\claim{H02} Earth's air sets three thresholds in WRF-ELEC's lightning. A breakdown field scaled with the air's density \autocite{dwyer2003fundamental} and capped at 180~kV\,m$^{-1}$ acts on Earth only below about 4.5~km, beneath where flashes
start, and in lunar air below about 36~km, where it lowers the field by 44\% at the ground. A ground strike
is declared when a channel reaches air warmer than $-7$\,\textdegree C, about 5~km above Earth's ground and
27--34~km above the Moon's. A 0.75~s sub-step is sized for 500~m layers and Earth's fall
speeds.\claim{H03} Earth's seas set SWAN's constants. It reduces its linear wind input above a knee fixed at
1~Hz, a value its source comments call largely arbitrary, and the fixed knee breaks the model's gravity
similarity: stock SWAN misses a sixfold similarity test by 5.44\% in significant wave height.\claim{H04} It
also fixes the air's density at 1.28~kg\,m$^{-3}$, where the air over the lunar seas holds about
1.40.\claim{H05} Earth's composition and rotation complete the set: RRTMG takes carbon dioxide from the
calendar year and carries Earth's methane, nitrous oxide, halocarbons and ozone, and CM1's beta plane
derives its gradient from Earth's rotation and a 6,370~km radius.\claim{H06} Each constant is right for
Earth, and becomes right for the Moon once it is a parameter or follows a scaling law.

\textbf{Units of time.} A formula written per day, and a process that matters only over a long run, both
hold Earth's calendar. Slingo's convective cover in PlaSim, $0.245 + 0.125\ln P$ in the form PlaSim's manual gives it \autocite{slingo1991response,lunkeit2012plasim}, with $P$ the convective
rain in millimetres per day, converts the model's rain to millimetres per day with the model's own solar day. ExoPlaSim sets that
day from the planet's rotation and orbit, 29.8 Earth days for the Moon, so the formula reads lunar rain
about 30 times too heavy and adds $0.125\ln 29.8 = 0.42$ to the cover, up to its cap of 0.8; on any world
the bias is $0.125\ln(\text{day}/1\,\text{d})$.\claim{H07} ExoPlaSim's year of 360 days in 17,280
half-hour steps holds 12.08 lunar model days of 1,430 steps, so output kept by model time slips 120 steps
a year against the Sun.\claim{H08} CM1's Sun follows Earth's 24-hour day, Earth's calendar and an obliquity
of 23.5\textdegree, and its five soil layers, 1--16~cm thick, resolve Earth's diurnal thermal wave; under
the month-long day that wave reaches about 0.6~m.\claim{H09} WRF-ELEC leaves the conduction of charge
through the air off by default, a choice that costs little in a storm of a few hours. In a month-long
lunar run the charge that evaporating cloud handed to the small ions persisted for weeks and held the field
at the ground near 20~kV\,m$^{-1}$ across the whole domain.\claim{H10}

\textbf{Geometry.} PlaSim and RRTMG treat each column as a stack of flat layers, lit only while the Sun
stands above that column's horizon. In the Moon's tall air the shells widen with
height, sunlight crosses the air beyond each column's horizon and the infrared escapes through a
wider top. Against flat columns of the same air the curved geometry takes in 10.3~W\,m$^{-2}$ more sunlight
and lets out 16.2~W\,m$^{-2}$ more infrared, a net loss of 5.8~W\,m$^{-2}$, nearly all of it from the
shells' widening, and its twilight puts 1.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ on the ground while the Sun is below the
horizon.\claim{H11} PlaSim stretches the low Sun's water path by Earth's horizon magnification,
$35/\sqrt{1+1224\mu^2}$ with $\mu$ the cosine of the solar zenith angle, where the Moon's factor is 13.5 for
water vapour and 7.1 for well-mixed air (Figure~\ref{fig:slant}).\claim{H12} PlaSim's dynamics treat the
air as a thin shell and CM1's as a flat slab, both with gravity constant with height, while the in-house
reference models take $g = GM/r^2$; lunar gravity falls 5.4\% by the air's mass-mean height and 15.3\% by
CM1's 150~km top.\claim{H13}

\textbf{Fits beyond their range.} The radiation schemes rest on fits to Earth's columns and Earth's Sun,
and the lunar columns lie far outside them. PlaSim's near-infrared water absorptivity, Lacis and Hansen's fit to Earth's water paths \autocite{lacis1974parameterization}, levels off toward half the sunlight, and over the Moon's longer paths it left
the air absorbing 7.7--8.2 points too little of the arriving sunlight on eleven columns.\claim{H14}
ExoPlaSim normalises its Rayleigh coefficient to the ultraviolet of a 5772~K blackbody, which for the
shield-filtered Sun gives a coefficient too low, and PlaSim's water continuum over-absorbs at lunar paths;
uncalibrated, the design case passed 310~K and was still warming at 315.5~K in its twelfth
year.\claim{H15} PlaSim's two shortwave bands omit carbon dioxide and oxygen, whose lines take sunlight
high in a column 7.25 times Earth's; the model gave that sunlight to the water vapour lower
down.\claim{H16} Sasamori's broadband longwave \autocite{sasamori1968radiative} has a continuum fitted to the outgoing flux, which leaves the
cooling by height free: against the correlated-$k$ reference it cools the lowest two layers by
9.7--11.9~W\,m$^{-2}$ too much and the air aloft by up to 12~W\,m$^{-2}$ too little.\claim{H17} PlaSim
diagnoses its clouds' water with CCM3's profile \autocite{kiehl1996description}, which falls off with geometric height on a scale of
$700\ln(1+\mathrm{PW})$~m written for Earth's gravity; with every pressure level six times higher, clouds
above about 20~km, most of the lunar cover, were left nearly dry.\claim{H18} It gives every cloud the
optics of liquid water and reckons saturation over liquid at every temperature, so its high cloud, at
180--204~K in the top layers, forms at 136--149\% of ice saturation.\claim{H19} In CM1, RRTMG's reference
pressures begin at 1,053.63~hPa, and the lowest 7.5~km of air over the lunar sea, at up to 1,216~hPa, lie
beyond them and are extrapolated in $\ln p$. Its ice optics end at 140~\textmu m, a size that slowly
falling lunar ice outgrows: the electrified box stopped at day 6.45 when its microphysics passed
157~\textmu m to the radiation.\claim{H20}

The microphysical and surface laws carry Earth in the same way. Morrison \autocite{morrison2005new} bounds the mean diameter of rain at 2.8~mm and begins breakup in rain's self-collection above a mean of 0.3~mm, both values taken from Earth's rain, while the
largest drop that survives its own fall scales with the capillary length $\sqrt{\sigma/\rho_w g}$, 2.46
times longer at lunar gravity.\claim{H21} The laws of sea and storm are Earth's calibrations: Wu's drag \autocite{wu1982wind},
Komen's growth and whitecapping \autocite{komen1984existence}, the AGROW coefficient \autocite{cavaleri1981wind}, a
breaker index of 0.73 \autocite{battjes1978energy}, SWASH's breaking thresholds of 0.6 and 0.3, and WRF-ELEC's
laboratory charging laws \autocite{saunders1998laboratory,takahashi1978riming}, between which the lunar storms'
polarity turns over.\claim{H22}

\textbf{Defects that Earth hid.} Five defects sat in the released codes. In a non-synchronous run on its
default solar geometry, ExoPlaSim's solar-position routine turns the Sun at the planet's rotation rate and
restarts its clock every model day, so each day
ends with the Sun skipped back by the excess of the model day over the rotation period: about 1\textdegree\
on Earth and 32.5\textdegree\ on the Moon, where sunlight along the equator then ran 8.3\% short from
270\textdegree\ to 57\textdegree~E and up to 17\% over near 164\textdegree~E
(Figure~\ref{fig:sunclock}).\claim{H23} Its default orbit setting silently replaces the configured
obliquity with Earth's orbit for the model's starting year from Berger's series \autocite{berger1978long},
23.7\textdegree, which on a Moon tilted
1.54\textdegree\ put 149~W\,m$^{-2}$ of sunlight on 86\textdegree\ of latitude against the Moon's 29 and
carried Earth's seasons and summer rain to 60\textdegree; on Earth the substitution is
invisible.\claim{H24} SWAN reads its breaker parameter uninitialised whenever depth-induced breaking is off
and divides by its square root in the fraction that sets the spectrum's limiter; repeated Earth runs then
differed by about 2.5\% in wave height, and SWAN's usual configuration, with breaking on, hides
it.\claim{H25} CM1 on several threads parts from itself after a few thousand seconds, first in the domain's
total vertical momentum, in the plain Morrison build as in the electrified one; the race remains
unlocated.\claim{H26} PlaSim's lowest model level reads up to 20~K too cold in 0.8\% of three-day windows,
most likely from the spectral transform over steep terrain.\claim{H27} Earth's parameters keep the first
two small and SWAN's usual configuration hides the third; the Moon's slow rotation and small tilt made the
first two large.

\textbf{Defects of the adaptation.} The adaptation made defects of its own, and checks of the kinds set
out under Verification found them. The GCM runner first extended the solar spectrum past its 2.73~\textmu m
end at its last value, an invented infrared tail that put 86\% of the sunlight in the near infrared and
warmed the first run past 320~K.\claim{H28} The first electrified build discharged once per 6~s step,
after all the step's charging, so the field stood a median 3.2 times breakdown at a discharge, which
removed a median 150~C. Its fall-speed scaling multiplied memory the scheme had left unset, and an OpenMP
directive made private a variable the threads shared, both caught when results changed with the thread
count; and the first lunar gravity patch scaled the NSSL fall speeds and missed the ventilation tied to
them, so lunar rain evaporated 1.3--1.5 times too fast for its size until a code review found
it.\claim{H29} The curved-air ledger's first channels averaged optical depth over windows and opaque lines
and absorbed 58\% too much sunlight above 500~nm with the Sun overhead.\claim{H30} The first terrain build
placed its forcing by a corner column standing 2.9~km up. CM1's starting soundings held the GCM's top-level
vapour constant above the GCM's 130~km top, 6--15 times saturated over ice at 140--150~km, and the
45\textdegree\,N twilight pair ran its two halves on builds compiled at different optimisation
levels.\claim{H31} The last two remain in the runs made before they were found.

\textbf{What a slice of the GCM lacks.} Running CM1 inside the GCM's climate adds a structural limit to
the inherited ones. A ring of columns along a latitude circle misses the transport across the circle, a
box misses any day--night circulation larger than itself, and stock CM1 needs three things added: a lunar
Sun, forcing above its boundary layer and nudging that follows terrain. Each absence showed in a run. The first box, forced with
the advection along the rings alone, rained in the mornings and stayed clear and hot in the afternoons
until it was stopped at day 47, and at 45\textdegree\,N the missing transport across the circle accounts
for about a third of CM1's warm offset from the GCM.\claim{H32} The forcing methods that answer these
limits borrow from the GCM whatever the slice lacks, and so inherit the GCM's remaining Earth.
