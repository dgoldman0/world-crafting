\section{Next steps}

The longwave correction and PlaSim's cloud, the two Earth fits whose tests moved the test world's climate most,
come first, the larger first; a check of the reference to which the longwave correction and every calibration
were fitted comes third; and a test of the general repairs on worlds other studies model, a few hours of
computing on which Table~\ref{tab:reach} depends, comes fourth. The later steps are unordered, since their
effects on the climate are unmeasured.

\textbf{The longwave at the settled state.} The largest single sensitivity comes from a correction fitted at
295~K and used near 307~K with twice the water vapour. Refitting the correction on the settled state's
cloud-free columns, and iterating to a consistent pair of fit and climate, decides whether the warm state holds:
if the consistent state returns near 295~K, the design's dimming stands, and if it stays near 307~K, the dimming
that holds the design temperature must grow, and the rain and cloud change with it. A correction with humidity
among its predictors would extend the fit; a reduced correlated-$k$ scheme built from the reference model's own
spectroscopy in place of Sasamori's and Lacis and Hansen's fits would replace it, and would hold on any column
whose gases and pressures that spectroscopy covers, on another world as on the test world.

\textbf{Cloud from the cloud-resolving model.} PlaSim's cloud amount and cloud water move the test world by
several kelvin and remain uncalibrated for it. Forcing CM1 from the consistent state of the first step, with the
transport across each ring imposed, tests whether the corrected longwave closes the 3.7~K by which CM1's lowest
1.8~km at 45\textdegree\,N stays warmer than the GCM's, and the cloud cover and water by pressure of those runs
become the target to which PlaSim's humidity thresholds and its cloud-water profile are fitted. Refitted cloud
could move the test world as far as the cloud settings do, up to about 6~K, and the design's dimming with it.
Cloud fitted this way remains a calibration, refitted for each other world from cloud-resolving runs of that
world.

\textbf{The reference against an independent code.} Every calibration was fitted to the line-by-line model, whose
carbon dioxide, oxygen, collision-induced absorption and sunlight have yet to be compared with an independent
code on the lunar column. Running the design column through LBLRTM \autocite{clough2005atmospheric} for the
longwave and a solver built on DISORT \autocite{stamnes1988numerically} for the sunlight, with every absorber on
identical layers, tests the reference itself. Layer heating or a split of sunlight departing from the reference by
more than the calibration residuals, 0.97~W\,m$^{-2}$ rms by layer or 0.6 points in the split, would require the
multipliers and the gas bands to be refitted and the dimming reset.

\textbf{A test of the reach.} Away from Earth's values, the repaired models have run on the test world alone.
Released and repaired ExoPlaSim on Earth-air planets rotating once in 16 and 64 days, two of the sidereal periods
of the ROCKE-3D runs of \textcite{way2018climates}, with the released radiation, which suits their Earth-sized
columns, would measure the three defects' effect on worlds that other studies model; the repaired runs' sunlight by
longitude and convective cover against rain would test the arithmetic of the Sun-clock and convective-cover repairs
off the test world; and setting the repaired climate beside ROCKE-3D's would separate the defects' effects from the
two models' own physics. A general repair whose arithmetic fails off the test world would be reclassed, and the
reach of Table~\ref{tab:reach} would narrow with it.

\textbf{An independent GCM.} ROCKE-3D or the LMD generic model on the test world's scenario
\autocite{way2017resolving,wordsworth2011gliese} would show which of the two present models departs near the
ground: an independent GCM's air as warm as CM1's would make PlaSim's boundary layer and longwave the next
repairs, and air as cool as PlaSim's would place the error in the slices' forcing and CM1's microphysics.

\textbf{The tall air's dynamics.} Both dynamical cores hold gravity constant, PlaSim's in a thin shell and CM1's in
a flat slab, while lunar gravity falls 5\% by the air's mass-mean height and the air in spherical shells holds
11.5\% more over each square metre of ground than $p_s/g_s$. The consistent deep-atmosphere equations
\autocite{white2005consistent} and the deep forms of global models \autocite{mayne2014unified} include both
effects; a first step corrects the GCM's column mass and heights, and a test of the circulation's sensitivity
shows whether the full equations are needed. A change in the GCM's poleward heat transport larger than the
design case's spread from year to year would make a deep core a requirement for air as tall against its world's
radius.

\textbf{Radiation in CM1.} RRTMG extrapolates its tables over the lowest 7.5~km of air over the sea, takes the
Sun's unfiltered spectrum, and cools the lowest 1.8~km 4.5~W\,m$^{-2}$ more than the reference. Extending its
tables past 1,053.63~hPa, with every other input held, would separate the table's edge from the other causes: if
the extended tables remove most of the 4.5~W\,m$^{-2}$, the extrapolation was the cause, and CM1's lowest air
warms by about a kelvin, so that, with PlaSim's longwave correction warming the GCM as well, the near-ground range
that remains measures the two models' boundary layers; if they remove little, the cause lies in RRTMG's other
approximations, and replacing RRTMG with the in-house correlated-$k$ scheme becomes the step. Giving RRTMG the
shield's spectrum, in a run of its own, would correct the difference of about 5~W\,m$^{-2}$ in the air's absorbed
sunlight that the Sun's unfiltered spectrum brings.

\textbf{Microphysics at lunar gravity.} Morrison's limits on the size of rain, its breakup and the size of ice
crystals stay Earth's; snow falls with the drops' gravity exponent where its own mass and area laws give a larger
one; and in the boxes 85\% of the falling water evaporates, in the rings 57--73\%. A gravity-similarity pair
under prescribed radiative cooling would measure how far the scaled microphysics departs from similarity, and
limits scaled with the capillary length would show how much of the evaporation the small drops cause.\claim{N03}
If larger drops bring CM1's surface rain toward the GCM's, the rain gap closes on the GCM's side and the rain-fed
lakes of the design keep their source; if CM1's rain stays near a quarter of the forcing GCM's, the lakes' water
budget has to be drawn from the cloud-resolving rain.

\textbf{The sign of the lunar lightning.} The sign of the lunar lightning changes with the laboratory charging
law chosen, and the laws disagree in the lunar storms' little cloud water. The sign of the charge rimed ice takes
at 4--8~m\,s$^{-1}$ in 0.02--0.2~g\,m$^{-3}$ of cloud water at $-5$ to $-16$\,\textdegree C, conditions Earth's
laboratories can reach, would decide whether the lunar ground strikes are negative, as under Saunders and Peck's
law, or positive, as under Takahashi's, and so the polarity that protection on the ground and in the air must
meet.\claim{N01} A leader model of the long crossing would replace the bound on ground strikes, now between none
and a fifth of flashes, with a rate, and with it the number of strikes, each lowering a median 149~C, that the
ground receives.

\textbf{The seas' Earth laws.} Wind input and breaking checked against theory at the lunar ratio of air to water
density, and the spread between source-term packages, Komen's against Janssen's and ST6, would decide whether the
similarity image of an Earth sea holds on the test world; a wide spread would make the lunar wave heights a
range. The air's density as a field and a wider frequency band for the small seas that fail the band-edge test
are numerical needs, and swell decay over the long fetches, where swell carries a third to two thirds of the wave
energy, a physical one that ST6 includes.\claim{N04}
