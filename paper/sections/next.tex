\section{Next work}

The next work follows from where the uncertainty now sits, and the order below follows how much of it each
step would remove. Each step names the question it settles, the comparison that settles it and the result
that would change the lunar design.

\textbf{The longwave at the settled state.} The largest single sensitivity rests on a correction fitted at
295~K and used near 307~K with twice the water vapour. Refitting the correction on the settled state's
cloud-free columns and iterating to a consistent pair of fit and climate decides whether the warm state
survives; a correction carrying humidity as well as temperature, and then a reduced correlated-$k$ scheme
built from the reference model's own spectroscopy in place of Sasamori's, would remove the fit altogether.
If the consistent state returns near 295~K, the design's dimming stands; if it stays near 307~K, the
dimming that holds the design temperature must grow, and the rain and cloud would change with it.

\textbf{Cloud from the cloud-resolving model.} PlaSim's cloud amount and cloud water move the Moon by
several kelvin and lack any lunar calibration. Forcing CM1 from the corrected GCM, with the transport across
each ring imposed, tests first whether the near-ground gap closes, and then gives cloud cover and water by
pressure against which PlaSim's humidity thresholds and its cloud-water profile can be fitted. Refitted cloud
could move the Moon as far as the cloud settings do, up to about 6~K, and the design's dimming with it. An
independent GCM on the same scenario, ROCKE-3D or the LMD generic model
\autocite{way2017resolving,wordsworth2011gliese}, is the comparison that would show which of the two present
models carries the disagreement in warmth and rain.

\textbf{The tall air's dynamics.} Both dynamical cores hold gravity constant in a thin shell, where the
lunar air loses 5\% of its gravity by its mass-mean height and holds 11.5\% more air over each square metre
of ground than a flat column of constant gravity. The consistent deep-atmosphere equations
\autocite{white2005consistent} and the deep forms of global models \autocite{mayne2014unified} carry both
effects; a first step corrects the GCM's column mass and heights, and a test of the circulation's
sensitivity says whether the full equations are needed. A change in the heat carried poleward larger than
the spread between the present models would make a deep core a requirement for the design case.

\textbf{Radiation in CM1.} RRTMG extrapolates its tables over the lowest 7.5~km of air over the sea, takes
the Sun's unfiltered spectrum and over-cools the lower air by 4.5--10~W\,m$^{-2}$ against the reference.
Extending its tables past 1,053.63~hPa, or replacing it with the in-house correlated-$k$ scheme, and giving
it the shield's spectrum would separate the table's edge from the other causes of that over-cooling, and
would move the near-ground temperatures that the range between the models reports.

\textbf{Microphysics at lunar gravity.} Morrison's limits on the size of rain, its breakup and the size of
ice crystals stay Earth's; snow falls with the drops' gravity exponent where its own mass and area laws give
a larger one; and in the boxes 85\% of the falling water evaporates, in the rings 57--73\%. A
gravity-similarity pair under prescribed radiative cooling would measure how far the scaled microphysics
departs from similarity, and limits scaled with the capillary length would show how much of the evaporation
the small drops cause; larger drops reaching the ground would narrow the rain gap between CM1 and the
GCM.\claim{N03}

\textbf{Electricity and seas.} The lightning's sign rests on laboratory charging laws that disagree in the
lunar storms' little cloud water. The sign of the charge rimed ice takes at 4--8~m\,s$^{-1}$ in
0.02--0.2~g\,m$^{-3}$ of cloud water at $-5$ to $-16$\,\textdegree C, conditions Earth's laboratories can
reach, would settle which way round the lunar storms are charged.\claim{N01} A finer box over a stormy window
would separate the grid from the physics in the flash rate, and a leader model would replace the bound on
ground strikes. The seas need the air's density field in place of one value, swell decay, a test of
convergence in frequency, and wind input and breaking checked against theory at the lunar ratio of air to
water density.

\textbf{Upstream and the record.} The Sun clock is reported to ExoPlaSim's maintainers and awaits a
reply;\claim{N02} the orbit default and SWAN's uninitialised breaker parameter await reports that would serve
every user of those codes, CM1's thread race, placed by elimination in its own loops, awaits locating and a
report, and CM1 would gain from one gravity parameter honoured by every scheme. The record itself needs the
twilight pair's control rerun on one build; runtime identity runs for the Sun clock, the water scaling and
the curved-air, gas-band and longwave patches at their off settings; and forcing for the CM1 cases set up
before the Sun and the sounding were corrected.
