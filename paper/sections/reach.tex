\section{Worlds within reach}

The two classes of repair reach differently. A general repair carries to other worlds as it stands, within
the limits Table~\ref{tab:reach} sets: the Sun clock, the fixed orbit and whole model days, convective cover
per Earth day, cloud water by pressure, gravity throughout CM1 with the fall-speed scaling, a Sun for any
day, leakage, the lifted breakdown cap and the scaled sub-step, SWAN's knee, and the methods that force a
slice of a GCM. A calibration carries only near the case it was fitted on: the Rayleigh and water
multipliers, the water continuum, the longwave correction and the gas-band fits. The curved-air terms sit
between the two, a general method whose tables belong to one column, and the settings carry the lunar
scenario's own values. Another world therefore takes the general repairs unchanged and needs its
calibrations, its ledger and its settings made again, with tools that come with the repairs: the reference
model, the ledger and the offline copies of PlaSim's radiation.\claim{W01}

\textbf{Where Earth's assumptions break.} Figure~\ref{fig:regimes} places found and crafted worlds against
the ratios the hidden Earth depends on. Along the geometry and the column, PlaSim's horizon factor of 35 is
exact only at Earth's scale height over radius; an isothermal Chapman factor gives 22 for present Mars, 24.5
for Venus, 14 for Titan and 7.2 for the Moon, so the slant path is wrong for each and most for the tallest
air. PlaSim's broadband fits were made near Earth's column, which Titan's exceeds tenfold, Venus's a
hundredfold and the Moon's sevenfold. Along the day and gravity, PlaSim's per-day formulas are exact only for
a one-day solar day: Slingo's cover gains $0.125\ln(\text{day}/1\,\text{d})$, 0.35 for Titan's 16-day solar
day and 0.60 for Venus's 117-day one, and loses cover for days shorter than Earth's. Its cloud water, written
in metres, errs at any gravity other than Earth's, too dry aloft under weaker gravity and too wet under
stronger. Its Sun clock, in a non-synchronous run, skips back $1/(N-1)$ of a turn each model day, where $N$
counts the planet's rotations in a 360-day year, rounded to a multiple of 12 and floored at 12: about
1\textdegree\ near Earth's rotation, 2\textdegree\ at a two-day rotation, 10\textdegree\ at ten days and
33\textdegree\ at any rotation slower than 20 days, exact only for a one-day rotation.\claim{W02} Released
CM1 breaks at any gravity but Earth's, under any day but Earth's 24 hours, and in any air whose carbon
dioxide differs from Earth's calendar-year value; in the configuration used, SWAN breaks gravity similarity
through one constant.

Table~\ref{tab:reach} sets out, property by property, where the released models break, what the
adaptations now reach, and what stays beyond them. Read by world, it gives three groups.

\textbf{Within reach as adapted.} The Open Moon under other dimmings runs in all four models with the
repairs as they stand. At 1.0~atm, a case yet to be run, or behind another shield that blocks the
ultraviolet, it needs its calibrations refitted against the reference and its cloud settings checked; a
shield that lets the ultraviolet through needs ozone, which the CM1 build sets to zero. Slowly rotating
planets with Earth's gravity and air, a common subject of exoplanet GCMs
\autocite{yang2014strong,way2018climates}, should need only ExoPlaSim's general repairs, though none has
been run: in a non-synchronous run the released Sun clock, convective cover and output calendar would
mislead, and the released radiation stays near the column it was fitted on while the climate stays near
Earth's warmth. For rotations slower than 20 days, ExoPlaSim's model day departs from the solar day unless
the year holds about twelve rotations, so output kept in whole model days drifts against the Sun, as
Terluna's analyses already allow for by sorting on the Sun's position.\claim{W03}

\textbf{Within reach with a new column.} A crafted world with breathable air on a small body, such as Mars
held at 1~bar, has a scale height over radius five times Earth's, 2.6 times Earth's air over each square
metre and 0.38 of Earth's gravity; it needs the general repairs, a ledger of its own column and calibrations
refitted to it, and at its 1~bar level it stays inside RRTMG's pressure range, though ground standing below
that level takes RRTMG past its tables as the lunar seas do. Earth-like air on a larger planet reaches the
same way, with the cloud water's error running the other way.\claim{W04}

\textbf{Beyond reach.} Titan's nitrogen--methane air at 94~K, present Mars's thin carbon dioxide and
Venus's 92~bar lie outside what PlaSim's and RRTMG's radiation carry and what Morrison's water-only
microphysics condenses; their general repairs would hold, and their radiation, condensation and clouds
would have to be built.\claim{W05} Four limits bound most of the reach of the adapted models: compositions
beyond nitrogen, oxygen, argon, water and modest carbon dioxide; surface pressures past RRTMG's tables,
already extrapolated over the lowest 7.5~km at 1.2~atm; scale heights past a few hundredths of the radius,
where thin-shell, constant-gravity dynamics lose the 5--15\% fall of gravity across the air; and empirical
laws of cloud, sea and charge used far from the Earth conditions they were fitted on. Table~\ref{tab:reach}
adds the narrower ones.
