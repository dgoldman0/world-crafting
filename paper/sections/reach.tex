\section{Reach to other worlds}

The Open Moon is the first world besides Earth that the repaired codes have simulated; for other worlds,
Table~\ref{tab:reach} gives, property by property, the range each repair was built for. To extend the ensemble to
another world, a modeller applies the general repairs unchanged within those ranges, recomputes the curved-air
table from that world's own column and starlight, refits the Rayleigh and water multipliers, the water continuum, the longwave
correction and the gas-band fits against the reference model, and sets the scenario's values, while leakage and
the slice forcing read that world's conductivity column and GCM fields, and the condition for ground strikes stays
a bound between two of WRF-ELEC's rules. The reference model, the code that computes the curved-air table and
offline copies of PlaSim's radiation, all in the code repository, compute the new table and the refits.\claim{W01}

\textbf{The defects in other configurations.} The three ExoPlaSim defects, two removed by general repairs and the
third avoided with the fixed-orbit switch, remain in the released code, and by the code's arithmetic they bias any
run that meets their conditions. The Sun clock acts in any non-synchronous run on the default solar geometry whose
rotation differs from one day. The convective-cover unit, by contrast, biases synchronous runs as well, since
ExoPlaSim's synchronous mode, even as it fixes the Sun in the sky, keeps the model day its rotation period sets, so
the cover rises by about $+0.23$ for TRAPPIST-1e's rotation of 6.1 days
\autocite{yang2023cloud} and $+0.31$ for Proxima Centauri~b's 11.2 days \autocite{boutle2017exploring}. The third
defect, the orbit default, gives Earth's seasons to any run that sets an obliquity or an eccentricity without
fixing the orbit.\claim{W06}

\textbf{How the Earth values err.} The Earth values that depend on a ratio of Figure~\ref{fig:regimes} err as a
world departs from Earth along it. PlaSim's horizon factor of 35 is right only at Earth's scale height over radius:
an isothermal Chapman factor gives 22 for present Mars, 24.5 for Venus, 14 for Titan and 7.2 for the Moon, so the
slant path errs for each and most for the tallest air. Along the column, PlaSim's broadband fits, made near Earth's
column, would be applied to columns ten times Earth's on Titan, a hundred times on Venus and seven times on the
Moon, and along gravity its cloud water, written in metres, errs at any gravity other than Earth's, too dry aloft
under weaker gravity and too wet under stronger; beside these ratios, Slingo's cover, even with its unit repaired,
remains a fit made on Earth's convection.\claim{W02} Among the other codes, CM1, once adapted, reads gravity as a
parameter on its Morrison and NSSL paths and has run storms at
the Moon's gravity and at Earth's; its drop limits and breakup, however, remain Earth's, with their effect at other
gravities unmeasured. Along the day, its Sun and soil are built for
days from a few Earth days to the Moon's, while along the column RRTMG's tables end at 1.05~bar. At sea, with
SWAN's knee patched, the two wave models, in the configuration used, are built to run water seas at any gravity as
the scaled image of an Earth sea, and on the code paths the similarity tests ran they have been tested at a sixth
of Earth's gravity, with Earth's empirical laws, whereas SWAN's ST6 drag, exact quadruplets and bottom friction,
and SWASH's rotation, all outside that configuration, keep Earth's constants. ExoPlaSim, for its part, holds near
the columns its radiation and cloud were fitted on: Earth-air columns near Earth's warmth with the released
radiation, and the test world's design columns with the calibrations. Of the natural worlds in
Figure~\ref{fig:regimes}, then, Earth alone lies near the fits in the adapted models.

\textbf{Worlds near the existing fits.} With the repairs as they stand, the four models are built to simulate the
Open Moon under other dimmings while its columns stay near the fitted ones: the split of sunlight, in the version
fitted before the gas bands, fell 0.7 points short on a 303~K column with 338~kg\,m$^{-2}$ of vapour, whereas the
refit with the gas bands is known through its fit residuals and the longwave correction's accuracy near 307~K is
unchecked. For slowly rotating planets with Earth's gravity and air around a Sun-like star near Earth's insolation,
such as those of the cooler runs that \textcite{yang2014strong} and \textcite{way2018climates} made with CAM3 and
ROCKE-3D, ExoPlaSim would need its general repairs, its fixed-orbit setting and its released radiation, which was
fitted on columns and a spectrum like theirs, while its cloud keeps Slingo's fit to Earth's convection and PlaSim's
Earth-tuned thresholds, Earth fits of a process through which those studies found the climate to change with
rotation. Their warmer runs, however, at up to 2.9 times Earth's insolation, lie beyond the radiation's fits. At
rotations slower than 20 days, moreover, ExoPlaSim's model day departs from the solar day unless the year holds
about twelve rotations, 69.8 against 77.6 days for a 64-day rotation and 265 against 726 days for a 243-day one, so
output kept in whole model days drifts against the Sun, though analyses that sort output by the Sun's position
avoid the drift.\claim{W03}

\textbf{Worlds that need refitted calibrations.} Synchronous planets of M dwarfs, such as TRAPPIST-1e and Proxima
Centauri~b, receive a spectrum outside the near-infrared water fit, so their shortwave needs refitting against the
reference with the star's spectrum. Even the Open Moon needs its calibrations refitted and its cloud settings
checked at 1.0~atm, a case yet to be run, or behind another shield that blocks the ultraviolet, while under a shield
that passes the ultraviolet its air would form ozone, which every patched CM1 build sets to zero. Beyond the Moon, a
constructed world with breathable air on a small body, such as Mars held at 1~bar, has a scale height over radius
five times Earth's, 2.6 times Earth's air over each square metre and 0.38 of Earth's gravity, and so needs the
general repairs, a curved-air table of its own column and calibrations refitted to it. For CM1's radiation, its
1~bar level stays inside RRTMG's pressure range, while the pressure on ground below that level lies past RRTMG's
tables, as it does over the lunar seas. On a larger planet, nitrogen--oxygen air needs the same steps, a slant
factor from its own column among them, though there the released cloud water errs toward too much water, the
reverse of the test world's error, and the same general repair corrects it. Around a giant planet, a breathable
moon needs, besides its column, the downward infrared that PlaSim now takes at its top for the host's
glow.\claim{W04}

\textbf{Worlds that need new physics.} Titan's nitrogen--methane air at 94~K, present Mars's thin carbon dioxide
and Venus's 92~bar lie outside what PlaSim's and RRTMG's radiation and Morrison's water-only microphysics
represent, so although the general repairs of units, clocks and gravity would apply to them unchanged, their
radiation, condensation and clouds would have to be built.\claim{W05} Within the Gaean class itself, four
restrictions on the current implementation limit its use: compositions of nitrogen, oxygen, argon, water and
modest carbon dioxide; surface pressures within RRTMG's tables, already exceeded over the lowest 7.5~km at
1.2~atm; scale heights up to a few hundredths of the radius, the test world's, where thin-shell, constant-gravity
dynamics already omit the 5--15\% fall of gravity across the air; and empirical laws of cloud, sea and charge used
near the Earth conditions they were fitted on, with narrower limits in Table~\ref{tab:reach}.
