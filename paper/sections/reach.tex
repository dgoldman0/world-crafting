\section{Worlds within reach}

The two classes of repair reach differently. A general repair carries to any world as it stands: the Sun
clock, the fixed orbit and whole model days, convective cover per Earth day, cloud water by pressure,
gravity throughout CM1 with the fall-speed scaling, the lunar Sun and deep soil, leakage, the lifted
breakdown cap and the scaled sub-step, SWAN's knee, and the methods that force a slice of a GCM. A
calibration carries only near the column it was fitted on: the Rayleigh and water multipliers, the water
continuum, the longwave correction and the gas-band fits. The curved-air terms sit between the two, a
general method whose tables belong to one column. Another world therefore takes the general repairs
unchanged and needs its calibrations and its ledger computed again, with tools that come with the repairs:
the reference model, the ledger and the offline copies of PlaSim's radiation.\claim{W01}

\textbf{Where Earth's assumptions break.} Figure~\ref{fig:regimes} places found and crafted worlds against
the ratios the hidden Earth depends on. PlaSim's horizon factor of 35 is exact only at Earth's scale height
over radius: an isothermal Chapman factor gives 22 for present Mars, 24.5 for Venus, 14 for Titan and 7.2
for the Moon, so the slant path is wrong for each of them, and most for the tallest air. Its broadband fits
hold near Earth's absorber paths, which Titan exceeds tenfold, Venus a hundredfold and the Moon
sevenfold. Its per-day formulas are exact only for a one-day solar day; Slingo's cover gains
$0.125\ln(\text{day}/1\,\text{d})$, which is 0.35 for Titan's 16-day solar day and 0.60 for Venus's
117-day one, and turns negative for days shorter than Earth's. Its cloud water, written in metres, is wrong
at any gravity other than Earth's, too dry aloft under weaker gravity and too wet under stronger. Its Sun
clock skips back $1/(N-1)$ of a turn each model day for a planet that turns $N$ times in its 360-day year:
1\textdegree\ near Earth's rotation, 2\textdegree\ at a two-day rotation, 10\textdegree\ at ten days and
33\textdegree\ at any rotation of about 21--60 days, and only a one-day rotation is exact.\claim{W02}
Released CM1 breaks at any gravity but Earth's, under any day but Earth's 24 hours, and in any air whose
carbon dioxide differs from Earth's calendar-year value; SWAN breaks gravity similarity through one
constant.

Table~\ref{tab:reach} sets out, property by property, where the released models break, what the
adaptations now reach, and what stays beyond them. Read by world, it gives three groups.

\textbf{Within reach as adapted.} The Open Moon at 1.0 to 1.2~atm, under other shields and dimmings, runs
in all four models with the repairs as they stand; a change of pressure or sunlight calls for refitting the
calibrations against the reference and for checking the cloud settings.
Slowly rotating planets with Earth's gravity and air, a common subject of exoplanet GCMs
\autocite{yang2014strong,way2018climates}, need only the general repairs to ExoPlaSim: in a
non-synchronous run the released Sun clock, convective cover and output calendar would mislead, and with
the column at Earth's mass the released radiation stays near the paths it was fitted on while the climate
stays near Earth's warmth.\claim{W03}

\textbf{Within reach with a new column.} A crafted world with breathable air on a small body, such as Mars
held at 1~bar, has a scale height over radius five times Earth's, 2.6 times Earth's air over each square
metre and 0.38 of Earth's gravity; it needs the general repairs, a ledger of its own column and calibrations
refitted to it, and it stays inside RRTMG's pressure range. Earth-like air on a larger planet reaches the
same way, with the cloud water's error running the other way.\claim{W04}

\textbf{Beyond reach.} Titan's nitrogen--methane air at 94~K, present Mars's thin carbon dioxide and
Venus's 92~bar lie outside what PlaSim's and RRTMG's radiation carry and what Morrison's water-only
microphysics condenses; their general repairs would hold, and their radiation, condensation and clouds
would have to be built.\claim{W05} Four limits bound the reach of the adapted models: compositions beyond
nitrogen, oxygen, argon, water and modest carbon dioxide; surface pressures past RRTMG's tables, already
extrapolated over the lowest 7.5~km at 1.2~atm; scale heights past a few hundredths of the radius, where
thin-shell, constant-gravity dynamics lose the 5--15\% fall of gravity across the air; and empirical laws
of cloud, sea and charge used far from the Earth conditions they were fitted on.
