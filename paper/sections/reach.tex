\section{Reach to other worlds}

The reach of a model is here the set of worlds on which its repaired code holds. The largest changes to the
test world's climate came from parts fitted to one world's columns: the first calibration moved the global mean
by at least 20~K, PlaSim's Earth-tuned cloud settings by up to about 6~K, and the longwave correction, switched
on with the ice optics, by 11.5~K, a joint effect in which the ice optics' share is unmeasured. Measured one at a
time, the general repairs moved it by a few kelvin at most, and the Sun clock and the Moon's tilt, applied with
the split of sunlight recalibrated, moved where sunlight and rain fall. For another world a modeller applies the
general repairs unchanged within the limits of Table~\ref{tab:reach}, recomputes the curved-air table from that
world's own column, refits the Rayleigh and water multipliers, the water continuum, the longwave correction and
the gas-band fits against the reference model, and sets the scenario's values; leakage and the slice forcing read
that world's conductivity column and GCM fields, and the condition for ground strikes stays a bound between two
of WRF-ELEC's rules. The reference model, the code that computes the curved-air table and offline copies of
PlaSim's radiation, all in the code repository, compute the new table and the refits.\claim{W01}

\textbf{The defects in other configurations.} The three ExoPlaSim defects that the general repairs remove remain in
the released code, and by the code's arithmetic they bias any run that meets their conditions. The Sun clock skips
back in any non-synchronous run on the default solar geometry whose rotation differs from one day, by about
33\textdegree\ a model day for every rotation slower than 20 days. The convective-cover unit adds
$0.125\ln(\text{day}/1\,\text{d})$ to the cover in any run whose model day differs from one Earth day, synchronous
runs included, since ExoPlaSim's synchronous mode fixes the Sun in the sky and keeps the model day its rotation
period sets: about $+0.23$ for TRAPPIST-1e's rotation of 6.1 days \autocite{yang2023cloud} and $+0.31$ for Proxima
Centauri~b's 11.2 days \autocite{boutle2017exploring}. The orbit default gives Earth's seasons to any run that sets
an obliquity or an eccentricity without fixing the orbit.\claim{W06}

\textbf{How the Earth values err.} The Earth values that depend on a ratio of Figure~\ref{fig:regimes} err as a
world departs from Earth along it. PlaSim's horizon factor of 35 is right only at Earth's scale height over radius;
an isothermal Chapman factor gives 22 for present Mars, 24.5 for Venus, 14 for Titan and 7.2 for the Moon, so the
slant path errs for each and most for the tallest air, and PlaSim's broadband fits, made near Earth's column, would
be applied to columns ten times Earth's on Titan, a hundred times on Venus and seven times on the Moon. Slingo's
cover, with its unit repaired, remains a fit made on Earth's convection, and PlaSim's cloud water, written in
metres, errs at any gravity other than Earth's, too dry aloft under weaker gravity and too wet under
stronger.\claim{W02} Released CM1 computes with Earth's gravity of 9.81~m\,s$^{-2}$, Earth's 24-hour day and the
calendar year's carbon dioxide, trace gases and ozone, and SWAN, in the configuration used, loses gravity
similarity through its wind-input knee, fixed at 1~Hz. The adapted CM1 reads gravity as a parameter on its Morrison
and NSSL paths and has run storms at the Moon's gravity and at Earth's; its drop limits and breakup remain Earth's,
and their effect at other gravities is unmeasured, since the similarity pair failed to convect. Its Sun and soil
are built for days from a few Earth days to the Moon's, and RRTMG's tables end at 1.05~bar. SWAN and SWASH run
water seas at any gravity as the scaled image of an Earth sea, tested at a sixth of Earth's gravity, with Earth's
empirical laws. ExoPlaSim holds near the columns its radiation and cloud were fitted on: Earth-air columns near
Earth's warmth with the released radiation, and the test world's design columns with the calibrations. Of the
natural worlds in Figure~\ref{fig:regimes}, Earth alone lies near the fits in the adapted models, and every run of
those models away from Earth's values is of the test world.

\textbf{Worlds near the existing fits.} With the repairs as they stand, the four models are built to simulate the
breathable Moon under other dimmings while its columns stay near the fitted ones: the split of sunlight, in the
version fitted before the gas bands, fell 0.7 points short on a 303~K column with 338~kg\,m$^{-2}$ of vapour; the
refit with the gas bands is known through its fit residuals, and the longwave correction's accuracy near 307~K is
unchecked. For slowly rotating planets with Earth's gravity and air around a Sun-like star near Earth's insolation,
such as the cooler runs that \textcite{yang2014strong} and \textcite{way2018climates} made with CAM3 and ROCKE-3D,
ExoPlaSim needs its general repairs, its fixed-orbit setting and its released radiation, which was fitted on
columns and a spectrum like theirs, while its cloud keeps Slingo's fit to Earth's convection and PlaSim's
Earth-tuned thresholds, and cloud is among the processes through which those studies found the climate to change
with rotation; their warmer runs, at up to 2.9 times Earth's insolation, lie beyond the radiation's fits, and the
runs have yet to be made. At rotations slower than 20 days, ExoPlaSim's model day departs from the solar day unless
the year holds about twelve rotations, 69.8 against 77.6 days for a 64-day rotation and 265 against 726 days for a
243-day one, so output kept in whole model days drifts against the Sun, and analyses that sort output by the Sun's
position avoid the drift.\claim{W03}

\textbf{Worlds that need refitted calibrations.} Synchronous planets of M dwarfs, such as TRAPPIST-1e and
Proxima Centauri~b, add a spectrum outside the near-infrared water fit, and their shortwave needs refitting
against the reference with the star's spectrum. The breathable Moon at 1.0~atm, a case yet to be run, or behind
another shield that blocks the ultraviolet needs its calibrations refitted and its cloud settings checked; under
a shield that passes the ultraviolet the air would form ozone, which every patched CM1 build sets to zero. A
constructed world with breathable air on a small body, such as Mars held at 1~bar, has a scale height over
radius five times Earth's, 2.6 times Earth's air over each square metre and 0.38 of Earth's gravity; it needs the
general repairs, a curved-air table of its own column and calibrations refitted to it, and at its 1~bar level it
stays inside RRTMG's pressure range, while the pressure on ground below that level lies past RRTMG's tables, as
it does over the lunar seas. Earth-like air on a larger planet needs the same steps, a slant factor from its own
column among them; there the released cloud water errs toward too much water, the reverse of the test world's
error, and the same general repair corrects it. A breathable moon of a giant planet needs, besides its column,
the downward infrared that PlaSim now takes at its top for the host's glow.\claim{W04}

\textbf{Worlds that need new physics.} Titan's nitrogen--methane air at 94~K, present Mars's thin carbon dioxide
and Venus's 92~bar lie outside what PlaSim's and RRTMG's radiation and Morrison's water-only microphysics
represent; the general repairs of units, clocks and gravity would apply to them unchanged, while their
radiation, condensation and clouds would have to be built.\claim{W05} The adapted models hold within four
limits: compositions of nitrogen, oxygen, argon, water and modest carbon dioxide; surface pressures within
RRTMG's tables, already exceeded over the lowest 7.5~km at 1.2~atm; scale heights up to a few hundredths of the
radius, the test world's, where thin-shell, constant-gravity dynamics already omit the 5--15\% fall of gravity
across the air; and empirical laws of cloud, sea and charge used near the Earth conditions they were fitted on,
with narrower limits in Table~\ref{tab:reach}.
