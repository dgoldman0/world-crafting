\section{Discussion and conclusions}

Earth values in four forms and five defects were found in four Earth-built codes adapted to a world that departs
from Earth along four ratios at once, and where a value was repaired, the kind of repair followed largely from its
form: units and constants took general repairs with no coefficient fitted to the test world, geometry a method
computed from the world's own column, and the radiation's fits calibrations to a line-by-line model. Three of the
defects, all in ExoPlaSim, produce little or no error at Earth's values and an error that grows as a world's
rotation slows or its day or tilt departs from Earth's, and by the code's own arithmetic they bias ExoPlaSim's runs
of natural planets: the convective-cover unit adds about 0.23 to the convective cover of a synchronous run of
TRAPPIST-1e, and the Sun clock sets the Sun back about 33\textdegree\ each model day in a non-synchronous run, on
the default solar geometry, of any planet rotating more slowly than once in 20 days.

Two of the three keep the global mean of the sunlight arriving at the top of the air and move that sunlight between
places. At any instant the sunlight a latitude circle receives depends on the Sun's declination and distance alone,
so the Sun clock, which errs in the Sun's longitude, keeps every zonal mean of the arriving sunlight while the test
world's equator received up to 17\% more than its mean in one sector and 8\% less in another; the orbit default,
which put Earth's tilt in place of the configured one, brought Earth's seasons and summer rain to 60\textdegree.
The moved sunlight changed the clouds, and through them the global means: in a two-year test of the Sun and tilt
alone, the clouds' effect in sunlight weakened by 2.4~W\,m$^{-2}$, the test world ran 0.9~K warmer while still
gaining heat, and the share of land under half a millimetre of rain a day rose from 9 to 36\%. An error of this
kind leaves a check of the arriving sunlight's global mean unchanged, and it shows in maps of sunlight by longitude
and of rain by season. Models can agree in their global means and still disagree widely in where cloud lies: on two
tidally locked planets, the quasi-global cloud-permitting runs of \textcite{yang2023cloud} covered 27 and 41\% of
the sky with cloud and two GCMs whose cover follows relative humidity, stability and large-scale ascent covered
82--97\%, while the three models' global-mean surface temperatures and albedos came out broadly alike.

By the global-mean temperature, changes to the parts fitted to Earth's atmosphere moved the test world furthest:
calibrating the radiation's Earth fits moved it by at least 20~K, and PlaSim's Earth-tuned cloud settings, pushed
to the ends of their tests, by up to about 6~K, against 1.6--2.5~K for the two general repairs measured alone. The
parts differ in what a modeller of a new world would need. The geometry of tall air is computed from the world's
own column, as the curved-air table was for the test world, whose air in curved shells loses a net 5.8~W\,m$^{-2}$
against the same clear column taken flat; Titan's haze, with a scale height of about 80~km, stands a few hundredths
of the radius tall, as the test world's air does, and plane-parallel aerosol codes for Titan's surface hold to
about 65\textdegree\ from the zenith \autocite{vincendon2010spherical}. A scheme built from line-by-line
spectroscopy would replace the radiation's gas fits, the largest of them, on any column whose gases, pressures and
temperatures that spectroscopy covers, on a natural world as on a constructed one, and the cloud optics would
remain fits. Cloud amount stays a calibration for each world, fitted to cloud-resolving runs of that world, and
those runs compute with Earth-fitted microphysics of their own.

Some of those Earth-fitted laws can be tested apart from any one world, in laboratories on Earth or in runs at
other gravities. In two windows of an earlier electrified run, made before the ventilation repair and with leakage
off, the test world's storms separated as much charge under Takahashi's charging law as under Saunders and Peck's,
within the spread of the Saunders and Peck runs, with the opposite sign, since the two laws disagree on the sign
that rimed ice takes in little cloud water; a measurement of that sign at 4--8~m\,s$^{-1}$ in 0.02--0.2~g\,m$^{-3}$
of cloud water between $-5$ and $-16$\,\textdegree C, conditions laboratories reach, would decide the polarity of
the lightning. The flash-rate laws used for tidally locked planets were fitted to Earth's storms and set the flash
rate from the cloud-top height or from the graupel and ice aloft
\autocite{braam2022lightning,sergeev2025lightning}. At a sixth of Earth's gravity the freezing levels of six
Morrison runs stood five to six times their heights in Earth's tropics, and the main electrified run, forced from
the Earth-orbit state, flashed at about a hundredth of Earth's average density, part of the difference possibly
from its grid; electrified storms resolved at other gravities, computed with the same laboratory charging laws,
could test those flash-rate laws, a route \textcite{sergeev2025lightning} also propose.

Gravity stays fixed in a laboratory on Earth, and a model's dependence on it is tested by similarity runs. Patched,
SWAN reproduces a sixfold gravity scaling of an Earth sea to 0.3\% in wave height, where the released code missed
it by 5.4\%, so in a sea under another gravity, the ancient seas of Mars among them, SWAN's gravity follows that
scaling, as tested at a sixth of Earth's gravity on the code paths those tests ran, and what stays uncertain is
Earth's empirical laws of drag, wind input and breaking at that sea's ratio of air to water density. CM1's
gravity-scaled microphysics remains untested this way, since the pair of runs designed for it failed to convect.
Its drop-size limits are Earth's, while the largest raindrop that survives its fall scales with the capillary
length, 2.46 times longer at a sixth of Earth's gravity; 85\% of the water falling from the clouds of a box forced
from the Earth-orbit state evaporated before it reached the ground, against 57--73\% in the rings, and a run with
limits scaled to the capillary length would measure how much of that evaporation the small drops cause.

By one design's own models, a shield in space that removes the ultraviolet heating the upper air would keep
Earth-like air on the Moon for tens of billions of years, on a body whose escape velocity let its natural
atmosphere go, so that gravity, column and the air's height against the radius depart from Earth's together, and
the Moon's tidal lock adds a 29.53-day day. On such a world the Earth values that depend on those ratios err
together. For its designers the calibrations hold near the columns they were fitted on, so a lower pressure or
another shield would need them refitted. Away from Earth's values every change made for the test world has run on
it alone. Released and repaired ExoPlaSim on Earth-air planets rotating once in 16 and 64 days, two of the sidereal
periods at which \textcite{way2018climates} ran ROCKE-3D, with the released radiation, would measure how far the
three defects move ExoPlaSim's climate of worlds that other studies model and would test its Sun-clock and
convective-cover repairs there. For any new world, the geometry and the gas absorption can be computed from the
world's own column, and cloud remains the part to be calibrated for that world, against cloud-resolving runs whose
microphysics laboratories and similarity runs can test.
