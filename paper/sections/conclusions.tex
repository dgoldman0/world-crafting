\section{Discussion and conclusions}

Where an Earth value in the four codes was repaired, the kind of repair followed largely from the value's form:
units and constants took general repairs with no coefficient fitted to the test world, geometry took a method whose
table is computed from the world's own column, and the radiation's fits took calibrations to a line-by-line model
and its correlated-$k$ emulation. Two of the general repairs remove, and a switch avoids, three of the five
defects, all in ExoPlaSim, which produce little or no error at Earth's values and an error that grows as a world's
rotation slows or its day, tilt or eccentricity departs from Earth's. By the code's own arithmetic, the
convective-cover unit adds about 0.23, up to the cover's cap of 0.8, to the convective cover of a synchronous run
of TRAPPIST-1e, and the Sun clock sets the Sun back about 33\textdegree\ each model day in a non-synchronous run of
any planet rotating more slowly than once in 20 days, on the default solar geometry.

On the test world two of the three kept the global mean of the sunlight arriving at the top of the air and moved
that sunlight between places. The Sun clock, which errs in the Sun's longitude alone, keeps every zonal mean of the
arriving sunlight, while the test world's equator received up to 17\% more than its mean in one sector and 8\% less
in another; the orbit default, which put Earth's tilt in place of the configured one, brought Earth's seasons and
summer rain to 60\textdegree, and in a two-year test of the corrected Sun clock and tilt alone the share of land
under half a millimetre of rain a day rose from 9 to 36\%. The moved sunlight changed the clouds, and through them
the global means: in the same test the clouds' effect in sunlight weakened by 2.4~W\,m$^{-2}$, and the test world
ran 0.9~K warmer while still gaining heat. A modeller finds such an error in maps of sunlight by longitude and of
rain by season.

Where two of the defects moved sunlight between places, changes to the parts fitted to Earth's atmosphere moved the
test world's global-mean surface temperature furthest: the first calibration of the radiation's Earth fits, a
Rayleigh multiplier and a weaker water continuum, moved the first design runs by at least 20~K; the longwave
correction and the ice optics, switched on together, warmed the design case by 11.5~K over years 10--19, with a
correction fitted at 295~K; and PlaSim's Earth-tuned cloud settings, pushed to the ends of their tests, moved the
global mean by up to about 6~K as the runs settled, against 1.6--2.5~K for the two general repairs measured alone.
What a modeller of a new world needs for each part depends on how far the world's own column determines it. The
radiative geometry of tall air is computed from that column, as the curved-air table was for the test world; on
Titan, whose haze has a scale height of about 80~km, three hundredths of its radius as for the test world's air,
the plane-parallel codes that separate the surface from the haze hold to about 65\textdegree\ from the zenith
\autocite{vincendon2010spherical}, although the adapted models lack Titan's nitrogen--methane radiation. The
dynamical cores keep a thin shell in the GCM and a flat slab in CM1, both at constant gravity, an approximation
whose effect on the test world's circulation is still to be measured. A scheme built from the reference's
line-by-line spectroscopy would replace the radiation's gas and scattering fits for air of nitrogen and oxygen with
water and modest carbon dioxide, under the starlight the scheme is built for and at the pressures and temperatures
where that spectroscopy agrees with an independent code, while the cloud optics would remain fits. The reference
has been compared with independent codes for water-only longwave at constant gravity and for Rayleigh scattering to
an optical depth of 1.5, and a comparison of its carbon dioxide, oxygen, collision-induced absorption and sunlight
on the design column is planned. Cloud amount would be a calibration for each world, fitted to cloud-resolving runs
of that world. On two tidally locked planets in the comparison of \textcite{yang2023cloud}, cloud covered 82--97\%
of the sky in two GCMs whose cover follows relative humidity, stability and large-scale ascent, and 27 and 41\% in
quasi-global cloud-permitting runs that count cloud by a condensate threshold, over sea surfaces fixed from a
coarser run of the same model, while the planetary albedos lay 2 to 11 points apart. On the test world the cloud
fit is still to be made, and CM1's runs compute with Earth-fitted microphysics of their own.

The charging laws grafted into CM1 were fitted to laboratory measurements. Two of them, Takahashi's and Saunders
and Peck's, disagree on the sign that rimed ice takes in little cloud water, so that in two windows of an earlier
electrified run, made before the ventilation repair and with leakage off, the test world's storms held their charge
in opposite arrangements under the two; a measurement of that sign at hail's impact speeds of 4--8~m\,s$^{-1}$, in
0.02--0.2~g\,m$^{-3}$ of cloud water at $-5$ to $-16$\,\textdegree C, would decide which arrangement the model's
lunar storms take. The flash-rate laws with which \textcite{braam2022lightning} and \textcite{sergeev2025lightning}
estimated lightning on tidally locked planets were fitted to Earth's storms and set the rate from the upward flux of
graupel and the column's ice water path or from the cloud-top height. At a sixth of Earth's gravity the cloud tops
of six runs with Morrison's microphysics rose to 55--80~km, far above the storms the cloud-top law was fitted on,
while the main electrified run, with the NSSL microphysics, forced from the Earth-orbit state and computing its
flashes from resolved charge, flashed at about a hundredth of Earth's average flash density, part of the difference
possibly from its grid. Tuning such laws on electrified storms resolved at other gravities, an application of the
route Sergeev et al. propose for different planetary atmospheres where observations are lacking, would fit them to
flashes computed from the same laboratory charging laws and from discharge thresholds set for Earth's storms, on
grids fine enough to converge the flash count: a comparison between models at each gravity.

Whether gravity enters a model consistently is a property of the code, tested by similarity runs. Patched, SWAN
passes such a test, reproducing a sixfold gravity scaling of an Earth sea to 0.3\% in wave height where the released
code misses it by up to 5.4\%; the patch, built to keep the scaling at any gravity, has been tested at a sixth of
Earth's on the code paths those tests ran, while Earth's empirical laws of drag, wind input and breaking keep
coefficients fitted at Earth's gravity and ratio of air to water density. For CM1 the test is still to be made: the
pair of runs designed to measure how far its convection, with gravity-scaled microphysics, departs from Earth's
convection stretched sixfold failed to convect. Morrison's drop-size limits in CM1 stay Earth's, while the largest
raindrop that survives its fall scales with the capillary length, 2.46 times longer at a sixth of Earth's gravity:
experiments on the largest stable drops of other media falling through other fluids agree with that capillary-length
form within about 20\%, and on present-day Earth collisions between hydrometeors, in place of the breakup of single
drops, set the largest raindrops \autocite{loftus2021physics}. Runs with Morrison's limits scaled by the capillary
length would therefore bound how much of the lunar rain's evaporation its small drops cause, a step still to run.

Part of the test world's design was set with the model, and so with the Earth fits that remain in it: the shield's
dimming was chosen so that the calibrated GCM settles at the design's 295~K, and holding that temperature on the
corrected curved-air terms and the gas bands took easing it from 5.0 to 3.0\%. With a longwave correction refitted
at the settled state, or cloud fitted to cloud-resolving runs, the dimming that holds 295~K could change again, and
under a lower pressure or another shield the columns would differ from those the calibrations were fitted on. The
changes that remove or avoid the three defects contain no coefficient fitted to the test world, and away from
Earth's values the repaired codes have run on the test world alone, so the defects' biases on other worlds are known
so far from the code's arithmetic. Runs of released and repaired ExoPlaSim on ozone-free Earth-air planets at
Earth's insolation and zero obliquity and eccentricity, rotating once in 16 and 64 days, two of the sidereal periods
of the ROCKE-3D runs of \textcite{way2018climates}, would measure their joint effect. The radiation and cloud fits,
which moved the test world's climate furthest, would be refitted or replaced on another world from evidence beyond
the GCM: the gas and scattering fits from line-by-line spectroscopy checked against an independent code, and the
cloud from cloud-resolving runs of that world, a second model whose own drop and charging laws would in turn need
laboratory tests at the conditions of its storms.
