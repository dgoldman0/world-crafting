\section{Conclusions}

Each Earth assumption in the four models was exact on Earth, which kept it out of sight in Earth's own
runs: a horizon factor of 35 is right at Earth's scale height, a formula counted per day is right for a
one-day solar day, and a Sun clock that restarts each day skips only a degree. A breathable Moon moves the
scale height, the column, the gravity and the day together, more than seven times Earth's air standing tall
under a sixth of its gravity through a month-long day, and so exposed them all at once: the clock's skip grew
to 32.5\textdegree, the default orbit handed the Moon Earth's seasons, and formulas fitted to Earth's
columns ran far past their range.

The repairs that removed this Earth are mostly general. Each turns a hidden value into a parameter whose
default reproduces the released code, and each takes its lunar value from a unit, a scaling law, a geometry
or a missing process, so it carries unchanged to slowly rotating planets, to small bodies with breathable
air and to worlds of other gravity. The calibrations are fewer and narrower: they tie PlaSim's radiation to
the in-house reference on one design column, and, with PlaSim's cloud settings, they hold the lunar climate
to that column and to cloud formulas fitted on Earth. Identity, reproduction, similarity and benchmark
checks show the code doing what it states; the calibrations show agreement with a reference model; the lunar
climate itself awaits a test, and the strongest available is agreement among independent models.

The adapted models have located the uncertainty that remains. It lies where the models stay Earth's: in
cloud amount, whose Earth-tuned settings move the Moon by up to 5~K, in the height at which the longwave
cools the air, whose correction moves it by 11.5~K, and in the boundary layer, where the cloud-resolving
model and the GCM still disagree by several kelvin near the ground. The next work is the work that loosens the tie to one column
and one cloud scheme: a longwave fit at the settled state, cloud constrained by cloud-resolving runs, and an
independent GCM.
