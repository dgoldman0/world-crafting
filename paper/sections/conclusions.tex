\section{Discussion and conclusions}

Where an Earth value in the four codes was repaired, the kind of repair followed largely from the value's form:
units and constants took general repairs with no coefficient fitted to the test world, geometry took a method whose
table is computed from the world's own column, and the radiation's fits took calibrations to a line-by-line model
and its correlated-$k$ emulation. Of the five defects, three, all in ExoPlaSim, produce little or no error at
Earth's values and an error that grows as a world's rotation slows or its day, tilt or eccentricity departs from
Earth's. By the code's own arithmetic, the convective-cover unit would add about 0.23, up to the cover's cap of
0.8, to the convective cover of a synchronous run of TRAPPIST-1e, and the Sun clock sets the Sun back about
33\textdegree\ each model day in a non-synchronous run, on the default solar geometry, of any planet rotating more
slowly than once in 20 days.

On the test world two of the three kept the global mean of the sunlight arriving at the top of the air and moved
that sunlight between places. The Sun clock, which errs in the Sun's longitude alone, keeps every zonal mean of the
arriving sunlight, while the test world's equator received up to 17\% more than its mean in one sector and 8\% less
in another; the orbit default, which put Earth's tilt in place of the configured one, brought Earth's seasons and
summer rain to 60\textdegree, and in a two-year test of the Sun and tilt alone the share of land under half a
millimetre of rain a day rose from 9 to 36\%. The moved sunlight changed the clouds, and through them the global
means: in the same test the clouds' effect in sunlight weakened by 2.4~W\,m$^{-2}$, and the test world ran 0.9~K
warmer while still gaining heat. A modeller finds such an error in maps of sunlight by longitude and of rain by
season, since global means can differ far less than cloud cover does. In the comparison of
\textcite{yang2023cloud} on two tidally locked planets, cloud covered 82--97\% of the sky in two GCMs whose cover
follows relative humidity, stability and large-scale ascent, and 27 and 41\% in quasi-global cloud-permitting runs
that count cloud by a condensate threshold, over sea surfaces fixed from a coarser run of the same model, while the
planetary albedos lay 2 to 11 points apart.

Where two of the defects moved sunlight between places, changes to the parts fitted to Earth's atmosphere moved the
test world's global-mean surface temperature furthest: the first calibration of the radiation's Earth fits, a
Rayleigh multiplier and a weaker water continuum, moved the first design runs by at least 20~K; the longwave
correction and the ice optics, switched on together, warmed the design case by 11.5~K over years 10--19, with a
correction fitted at 295~K; and PlaSim's Earth-tuned cloud settings, pushed to the ends of their tests, moved the
global mean by up to about 6~K as the runs settled, against 1.6--2.5~K for the two general repairs measured alone.
What a modeller of a new world needs for each part depends on how far the world's own column determines it. The
radiative geometry of tall air is computed from that column, as the curved-air table was for the test world, while
the GCM's dynamics keep a thin shell and CM1's a flat slab, both at constant gravity. On Titan, whose haze has a
scale height of about 80~km, three hundredths of its radius as for the test world's air, the plane-parallel codes
that separate the surface from the haze hold to about 65\textdegree\ from the zenith
\autocite{vincendon2010spherical}, although the adapted models lack Titan's nitrogen--methane radiation. A scheme
built from the reference's line-by-line spectroscopy would replace the radiation's gas fits, the largest of the Earth
fits, for air of nitrogen and oxygen with water and modest carbon dioxide at the pressures and temperatures over
which an independent code has checked that spectroscopy, a check so far planned on the design column alone, and the
cloud optics would remain fits. Cloud amount would be a calibration for each world, fitted to cloud-resolving runs
of that world; on the test world that fit is still to be made, and those runs compute with Earth-fitted
microphysics of their own.

The storm models' charging laws were fitted to laboratory measurements. Two of them, Takahashi's and Saunders and
Peck's, disagree on the sign that rimed ice takes in little cloud water, so that in two windows of an earlier
electrified run, made before the ventilation repair and with leakage off, the test world's storms took opposite
polarities under the two; a measurement of that sign at the impact speeds, cloud water and temperatures of the
lunar storms would decide which polarity the model's lunar lightning takes. The flash-rate laws with which
\textcite{braam2022lightning} and \textcite{sergeev2025lightning} estimated lightning on tidally locked planets
were fitted to Earth's storms and set the rate from the upward flux of graupel and the column's ice water path or
from the cloud-top height. At a sixth of Earth's gravity the cloud tops of six runs with Morrison's microphysics
rose to 55--80~km, far above the storms the cloud-top law was fitted on, while the main electrified run, with the
NSSL microphysics, forced from the Earth-orbit state and computing its flashes from resolved charge, flashed at
about a hundredth of Earth's average flash density, part of the difference possibly from its grid. Electrified
storms resolved at other gravities, the route Sergeev et al. propose for tuning such laws where observations are
lacking, would set those laws beside flashes computed from the same laboratory charging laws and from discharge
thresholds set for Earth's storms, on grids fine enough to converge the flash count: a comparison between models at
each gravity.

Whether gravity enters a model consistently is a property of the code, tested by similarity runs. Patched, SWAN
reproduces a sixfold gravity scaling of an Earth sea to 0.3\% in wave height, where the released code misses it by
up to 5.4\%; the patch, built to keep the scaling at any gravity, has been tested at a sixth of Earth's on the code
paths those tests ran, while Earth's empirical laws of drag, wind input and breaking keep coefficients fitted at
Earth's gravity and ratio of air to water density. For CM1 the test is still to be made: the pair of runs designed
to measure how far its convection, with gravity-scaled microphysics, departs from Earth's convection stretched
sixfold failed to convect. Its drop-size limits stay Earth's, while the largest raindrop that survives its fall
scales with the capillary length, 2.46 times longer at a sixth of Earth's gravity; experiments on the largest stable
drops of other media falling through other fluids agree with that capillary-length form within about 20\%
\autocite{loftus2021physics}. On Earth, collisions set the largest raindrops, so runs with Morrison's limits scaled
by the capillary length would bound a sensitivity, a step still to run.

Part of the test world's design was set with the model, and so with the Earth fits that remain in it: the shield's
dimming was chosen so that the calibrated GCM settles at the design's 295~K, and holding that temperature on the
corrected curved-air terms and the gas bands took easing it from 5.0 to 3.0\%. With a longwave correction refitted
at the settled state, or cloud fitted to cloud-resolving runs, the dimming that holds 295~K could change again, and
under a lower pressure or another shield the columns would differ from those the calibrations were fitted on. The
changes that remove or avoid the three defects contain no coefficient fitted to the test world, and away from
Earth's values the repaired codes have run on the test world alone, so the defects' biases on other worlds are known
so far from the code's arithmetic. Released and repaired ExoPlaSim on ozone-free Earth-air planets at Earth's
insolation and zero obliquity and eccentricity, rotating once in 16 and 64 days, two of the sidereal periods of the
ROCKE-3D runs of \textcite{way2018climates}, would measure their joint effect. The fits, which moved the test
world's climate furthest, would be refitted or replaced on another world from evidence outside the adapted codes:
the gas fits from line-by-line spectroscopy checked against an independent code, the cloud from cloud-resolving runs
of that world, and the drop and charging laws inside those runs from laboratory tests.
