\section{Conclusions}

On a breathable Moon, where scale height, column, gravity and day all lie far from Earth's in air close to
Earth's own, the Earth values left in four models erred by amounts large enough to measure, and three of the
five defects found beside them produce little or no error at Earth's values: a Sun clock exact at a one-day
rotation, a rain unit exact at a one-day model day, and an orbit default that moves Earth's tilt by a fraction
of a degree. All three act in other users' runs as well, the clock in any non-synchronous run whose rotation
differs from a day, the rain unit in synchronous runs too, and the orbit default in any run that sets a tilt
without fixing the orbit.

Removing the Earth values sorted the models' parts twice, by how far each moved the Moon and by how far each
travels, and the two orders run against each other. The general repairs, which another world takes unchanged,
moved the Moon's global mean by a few kelvin at most and moved where its sunlight and rain fall, cutting the
rain by nearly a quarter and raising the share of land under half a millimetre a day from a tenth to two
fifths. The parts another world must remake moved it most: the first calibration by at least 20~K, PlaSim's
Earth-tuned cloud by up to about 6~K, and its longwave cooling by height, corrected with ice optics, by about
11.5~K in a run outside the correction's fitted state. Where CM1 forms its own cloud and rain at lunar gravity,
the freezing level stands five to six times higher than Earth's, most of the falling water evaporates before it
reaches the ground, and the lightning is rarer and larger than Earth's, with a sign set by a laboratory
charging law; SWAN, by similarity, raises waves four to five times higher than Earth's under the same wind.

The work that would narrow the Moon's remaining uncertainty is therefore the work that would extend the
models to other Earth-like worlds. A radiation scheme built from the reference's own spectroscopy in place of
fitted coefficients, cloud constrained by cloud-resolving runs in place of Earth's settings, and a reference
checked against an independent code would each turn a part that holds near one column into one that holds
wherever its physics does, and runs of the repaired ExoPlaSim on Earth-air slow rotators would show whether
the parts already classed as general hold off the Moon.
