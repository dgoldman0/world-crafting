\section{Conclusion}

A modeller who takes a model of Earth to another world inherits the values set in it for Earth. In four codes that
began as Earth models, coupled so that a planet's climate forces both its electrified storms and the waves that run
up its shore, most repairs of those values, and of the defects found beside them, were built as switches whose
default is written to reproduce the released code. On a world whose air resembles Earth's while its gravity, day,
column of air and scale height all depart from Earth's, the Earth values can then be taken out one at a time and
Earth's share of a result measured.

Measured that way on the test world, Earth's share was largest in the fits, the formulas that read as physics while
their coefficients come from Earth's columns, clouds and storms: the first calibration of the radiation's fits
against the line-by-line model moved the global-mean surface temperature by at least 20~K, and PlaSim's Earth-tuned
cloud settings, pushed to the ends of their tests, moved it by up to about 6~K, where the repairs of units,
constants and geometry, measured one at a time, moved it by 2.5~K or less.

Small as their share of the global mean was, repairs of those kinds put the world's own gravity and geometry into
the codes, and with them the codes computed the rain, storms and sky by which the test world, in its models,
departs from Earth. Raindrops fall about three times slower, and in a storm-resolving box, with drop sizes still
set for Earth, 85\% of the water leaving the clouds evaporated on the way down, against 64\% when the same drops
fell at Earth's speeds. The same gravity, in both models, stretched the storms upward, and in the electrified run
their charge took Earth's arrangement at five to six times its height. Overhead, the air is so tall that a full
Earth day after sunset about half of it still lies above the Moon's geometric shadow, in sunlight that the
curved-air terms add to each layer and that flat columns, lit only while the Sun stands above their own horizon,
leave out. The seas needed only the gravity that SWAN already reads: under the same wind, fetch and duration as on
Earth, with Wu's drag at both gravities, they build waves four to five times higher, with five times the period and
Earth's steepness, and once SWAN's wind-input knee moves with gravity, the lunar sea is an Earth sea scaled.

Computed for a world that exists only as a design, these features can be checked against other models and against
the codes themselves, and the checks of a code need nothing from the world it models. Five of ExoPlaSim's patches
at their neutral settings repeat a model year's restart of the builds before them byte for byte, so that the change
each makes when switched on is its own; run at a sixth of Earth's gravity, with its lengths and times stretched
sixfold, the patched SWAN matches the Earth sea it scales to 0.3\% in wave height, where the released code misses
by up to 5.4\%. A similarity run of that kind tests whether gravity enters a code consistently, a property of the
code on any world, and CM1's gravity-scaled microphysics, behind the slow rain, still lacks that test, since the
pair of runs designed for it failed to convect. The fits need evidence from outside the codes.

For the radiation's gas and scattering fits that evidence is spectroscopy: a scheme built from the reference
model's line-by-line spectroscopy would replace them, once an independent code has checked that spectroscopy, a
comparison planned on the design column, and the scheme has been checked against it layer by layer, and would then
hold on another world's columns within the gases, pressures and temperatures of the checks and under the starlight
it would be built for. For cloud the evidence is runs that resolve it. On two tidally locked planets
\textcite{yang2023cloud} found cloud-permitting runs far from two GCMs in cloud cover and closer in albedo, and
judged the climates broadly similar; on the test world CM1, forced from the GCM's own fields by a method built for
a slice of any world's GCM, computes the cloud against which PlaSim's would be calibrated.

Those runs compute with Earth-fitted laws of their own, and for two of them the evidence would come from
laboratories on Earth. Two of the charging laws grafted into CM1 disagree on the sign of the charge rimed ice takes
in little cloud water, and in two windows of an earlier run, made before the ventilation repair and with leakage
off, the lunar storms held their charge in opposite arrangements under the two; measured at the speeds at which the
storms' hail and graupel strike, that sign would decide which way up the model's storms hold their charge. Gravity
enters the raindrop through the capillary length, so experiments at Earth's gravity can vary the other terms: the
largest stable drops of other liquids, falling through other fluids, follow the capillary scaling within about 20\%
\autocite{loftus2021physics}. If the sizes that collisions allow scale as the largest stable drop does, CM1's
limits on raindrop size, scaled the same way, would show how much of the lunar rain's evaporation its small drops
cause, and whether the design's rain-fed lakes keep their source.

What a modeller must do to carry a part of these codes to another world follows from how the part was made: a
general repair is built to hold there unchanged within the limits of its law, the curved-air method once its table
is recomputed from that world's own column, and a calibration once it is refitted on that world's columns, while a
world whose air or condensates differ from Earth's needs its radiation, condensation and clouds built anew. The
curved-air method matters wherever the scattering layer is tall against the radius, as Titan's haze is, at the same
three hundredths of the radius as the test world's air, and there the plane-parallel codes that separate surface
from haze hold typically to about 65\textdegree\ from the zenith \autocite{vincendon2010spherical}. Away from
Earth's values, numerical controls apart, the repaired codes have run on the test world alone.

The three ExoPlaSim defects that grow as a world departs from Earth's values were found on the test world, whose
month-long day and small tilt made them large, and by the code's own arithmetic they bias the runs of natural
planets that meet their conditions as well: the Sun clock sets the Sun back about 33\textdegree\ each model day in
any non-synchronous run, on the default solar geometry, of a planet turning more slowly than once in 20 days, and
the convective-cover unit adds about 0.23 to the convective cover of a synchronous run of TRAPPIST-1e. On the test
world the Sun clock and the orbit default kept the global mean of the sunlight arriving at the top of the air while
moving that sunlight between places, and correcting them warmed the global mean by 0.9~K over two years, still
gaining heat, while the share of land under half a millimetre of rain a day rose fourfold, a change a modeller
finds in maps of sunlight by longitude and of rain by season. Released and repaired ExoPlaSim, run on two of the
slowly rotating planets of \textcite{way2018climates}, would be the first runs of the repairs off the test world
and would measure the three defects' joint effect on worlds that other studies have modelled.

A constructed world can combine Earth-like air with a gravity, a size and a day that no known natural world
combines with it, and a model run on such a world meets at once the Earth values that a slow rotator meets one at a
time and a smaller world a few at a time. For such a world the model is part of the design. The test world's shield
was dimmed until the calibrated GCM settled at the design's 295~K, and as the curved-air terms and the carbon
dioxide and oxygen bands entered the radiation, holding that temperature took easing the dimming from 5.0 to 3.0\%;
a refitted longwave correction, or cloud calibrated against resolved runs, could move it again. Until such a world
is built its models are the only evidence of its climate, and the Earth values left in them are the design's own
uncertainty: in the air where people and plants would live, at 45\textdegree\,N and with the longwave correction
off, the storm model ran 3.7~K warmer than the GCM that forced it once the transport across the ring was imposed,
and about 1.5~K of that gap is the GCM's own over-cooling through its Earth-fitted longwave.

Many models used for other worlds began as models of Earth, and in four of them, measured one switch at a time on
the test world, Earth's share of the climate came mostly from fits whose replacements would come from evidence
outside the codes: line-by-line spectroscopy for the radiation's gases, runs that resolve the cloud for its cover
and water, and, inside those runs, laboratory measurements of the charge rimed ice takes at the storms' impact
speeds and of the largest stable drops of other liquids. Built and checked, those replacements would let a modeller
set the gravity, day, column and starlight of a world whose air and water resemble Earth's and follow its weather
down the chain from its climate to its shore, with Earth's share of the results measured: for a slowly turning
exoplanet, where its sunlight falls and how its cloud and rain follow its long day; for a world still being
designed, how high its storms build and which way up they hold their charge, how much of its rain reaches the
ground, how its swell runs up a shore and how long its upper air stays sunlit after sunset, all before anyone lives
under its sky.
