\section{Discussion and conclusions}

Brought to a world that departs from Earth along four ratios at once, four Earth-built codes were found to contain
Earth values in four forms and five defects, and where a value was repaired, the kind of repair followed largely
from its form: constants and units of time took general repairs with no coefficient fitted to the test world,
geometry a method computed from the world's own column, and the radiation's fits calibrations that hold near the
columns they were fitted on. Three of the defects produce little or no error at Earth's values and an error that
grows as a world departs from Earth's rotation, day or tilt, and by the code's own arithmetic they bias ExoPlaSim's
runs of slowly rotating and tidally locked planets.

Several of these errors leave the global means nearly intact and change where sunlight and rain fall. ExoPlaSim's
Sun clock keeps every zonal and global mean of sunlight exact, since the share of a latitude circle in sunlight
depends on the Sun's declination alone, and moves the sunlight between longitudes: on the test world it left the
equator 8.3\% below its mean from 270\textdegree\ to 57\textdegree~E and up to 17\% above it near 164\textdegree~E,
and in a non-synchronous run on ExoPlaSim's default solar geometry with any rotation slower than 20 days it sets
the Sun back about 33\textdegree\ each model day. Earth's tilt, put in place of the configured one by the orbit
default, brought Earth's seasons and summer rain to 60\textdegree; with the Sun and the tilt set right, 36\% of the
land received under half a millimetre of rain a day within two years, against 9\% before, while the step in which
both were corrected, with a refit of the split of sunlight, moved the global mean by 0.6~K. A check of the global
energy balance passes with such errors in place; they show in a map of sunlight by longitude or of rain by season.

By size, changes to the parts fitted to Earth's atmosphere moved the test world furthest. Calibrating the
radiation's Earth fits, used beyond the range they were fitted over, moved its global-mean surface temperature by
more than 20~K, and PlaSim's Earth-tuned cloud settings, pushed to the ends of their tests, by up to about 6~K,
while the two general repairs measured singly, of a unit and of a constant, moved it by 1.6--2.5~K. The two
atmospheric models disagree where each computes with Earth fits of its own: with the GCM's transport imposed, CM1's
lowest 1.8~km at 45\textdegree\,N stays 3.7~K warmer than the GCM's, and a box forced from the GCM brings a quarter
of the GCM's rain to the ground. Models of natural planets differ widely in cloud as well: on two tidally locked
planets a global cloud-permitting model covered 27 and 41\% of the sky with cloud, where two GCMs whose cover
follows relative humidity, stability and large-scale ascent covered 82--97\% \autocite{yang2023cloud}. The
radiation's fits can be replaced outright, since a scheme built from line-by-line spectroscopy would hold on any
column whose gases and pressures that spectroscopy covers, on a natural world as on a constructed one. Cloud is
harder to replace: the cloud-resolving runs that could calibrate a GCM's cloud for a new world compute with
Earth-fitted microphysics in turn.

Some of the Earth laws in the cloud-resolving model can be tested in laboratories on Earth. In two windows of the
test world's electrified run, the storms separated as much charge under Takahashi's charging law as under Saunders
and Peck's, with the opposite sign, since the two laws disagree on the sign that rimed ice takes in the little
cloud water of those storms; a measurement of that sign at 4--8~m\,s$^{-1}$ in 0.02--0.2~g\,m$^{-3}$ of cloud water
between $-5$ and $-16$\,\textdegree C, conditions laboratories reach, would decide the polarity of the lightning.
The largest raindrop that survives its own fall scales with the capillary length, 2.46 times longer at a sixth of
Earth's gravity, while Morrison's scheme keeps Earth's limits on drop size and breakup; with 85\% of the water
falling from the clouds of a box forced from the GCM evaporating before it reached the ground, those limits help
set how much rain a low-gravity world receives. Flash rates on tidally locked planets have been estimated with laws
fitted to Earth's storms, from the cloud-top height or from the graupel and ice aloft
\autocite{braam2022lightning,sergeev2025lightning}. In the one electrified box, cloud tops reached 55--80~km, with
the freezing levels five to six times their heights in Earth's tropics, and the flashes came at about a hundredth
of Earth's average density, so a law fitted at Earth's gravity would be applied there far from the storms it was
fitted on, and resolved storms at other gravities can test it.

Once a model scales correctly with gravity, its Earth-fitted laws are what remains uncertain. Patched, SWAN scales
an Earth sea sixfold in gravity within 0.3\% in wave height, where the released code missed by 5.4\%, so for a sea
under another gravity, the ancient seas of Mars among them, the open questions are Earth's empirical laws for drag,
wind input and breaking at that sea's ratio of air to water density. The geometry of tall air can be computed from
the world's own column: against flat columns, the test world's curved air loses a net 5.8~W\,m$^{-2}$, and on
Titan, whose air is a quarter as tall in proportion, a flat treatment already fails beyond about 65\textdegree\
from the zenith \autocite{vincendon2010spherical}.

Constructed worlds join Earth-like air to gravities, sizes and days that no known natural world combines with it.
Behind engineered protection, Earth-like air can persist on a body whose escape velocity would let a natural
atmosphere go, so that gravity, column, day and the air's height against the radius depart from Earth's together,
and the Earth values that depend on them err together. Modelling such a world also tests the tools used for natural
ones: the defects found here bias runs of slowly rotating and tidally locked planets, and each change made for the
test world is recorded with its switch or condition and its class, so that a modeller of another world can see
which changes were built to apply unchanged and which must be refitted there. The general repairs were built to
hold off the test world and have run on it alone. Released and repaired ExoPlaSim on Earth-air planets rotating
once in 16 and 64 days, the periods at which ROCKE-3D has simulated such planets \autocite{way2018climates}, would
measure how far the three defects move the simulated climate of worlds that other studies model, and would test the
general repairs on a world beyond the one they were made on.
