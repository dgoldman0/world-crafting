\section{Discussion and conclusions}

Where an Earth value in the four codes was repaired, the kind of repair followed largely from the value's form:
units and constants took general repairs with no coefficient fitted to the test world, geometry took a method whose
table is computed from the world's own column, and the radiation's fits took calibrations to a line-by-line model
and its correlated-$k$ emulation. Two of the general repairs remove, and a switch avoids, three of the five
defects, all in ExoPlaSim, which produce little or no error at Earth's values and an error that grows the more
slowly a world rotates or the further its day, tilt or eccentricity departs from Earth's. By the code's own
arithmetic, the convective-cover unit adds about 0.23, up to the cover's cap of 0.8, to the convective cover of a
synchronous run of TRAPPIST-1e, and, on the default solar geometry, the Sun clock sets the Sun back about
33\textdegree\ each model day in a non-synchronous run of any planet rotating more slowly than once in 20 days.

On the test world two of the three kept the global mean of the sunlight arriving at the top of the air and moved
that sunlight between places. The Sun clock errs in the Sun's longitude alone and so keeps every zonal mean of the
arriving sunlight, while the test world's equator received up to 17\% more than its mean in one sector and 8\% less
in another; the orbit default, which put Earth's tilt in place of the configured one, brought Earth's seasons and
summer rain to 60\textdegree. In a two-year test of the corrected Sun clock and tilt alone, the share of land under
half a millimetre of rain a day rose from 9 to 36\%, and the moved sunlight changed the clouds and through them the
global means: the clouds' effect in sunlight weakened by 2.4~W\,m$^{-2}$, and the test world ran 0.9~K warmer while
still gaining heat. A modeller finds such an error in maps of sunlight by longitude and of rain by season.

Whereas two of the defects moved sunlight between places, changes to the parts fitted to Earth's atmosphere moved
the test world's global-mean surface temperature furthest: the first calibration of the radiation's Earth fits, a
Rayleigh multiplier and a weaker water continuum, moved the first design runs by at least 20~K; the longwave
correction and the ice optics, switched on together, warmed the design case by 11.5~K over years 10--19, with a
correction fitted at 295~K; and PlaSim's Earth-tuned cloud settings, pushed to the ends of their tests, moved the
global mean by up to about 6~K as the runs settled, against 1.6--2.5~K for the two general repairs measured alone.
What a modeller of a new world needs for each part depends on how far the world's own column determines it. The
radiative geometry of tall air is computed from that column, as the curved-air table was for the test world; on
Titan, whose haze has a scale height of about 80~km, the same three hundredths of the radius as the test world's
air, the plane-parallel codes that separate the surface from the haze hold typically to about 65\textdegree\ from
the zenith \autocite{vincendon2010spherical}, although the adapted models lack Titan's nitrogen--methane radiation.
The dynamical cores approximate the same geometry by a thin shell in the GCM and a flat slab in CM1, both at
constant gravity, with an effect on the test world's circulation still to be measured. A scheme built from the
reference's line-by-line spectroscopy would replace the radiation's gas and scattering fits for air of nitrogen and
oxygen with water and modest carbon dioxide, under the starlight the scheme would be built for and at the pressures
and temperatures where that spectroscopy agrees with an independent code, while the cloud optics would remain fits.
The reference has been compared with an independent code, PyRADS, for water-only longwave at constant gravity and
with an independent method, a Monte Carlo calculation, for Rayleigh scattering to an optical depth of 1.5, and a
comparison of its carbon dioxide, oxygen, collision-induced absorption and sunlight on the design column is
planned. Cloud amount would be calibrated for each world against cloud-resolving runs of that world: on two
tidally locked planets in the comparison of \textcite{yang2023cloud}, cloud covered 82--97\% of the sky in two GCMs
whose cover follows relative humidity, stability and large-scale ascent, and 27 and 41\% in quasi-global
cloud-permitting runs that count cloud by a condensate threshold, over sea surfaces fixed from a coarser run of the
same model, while the planetary albedos lay 2 to 11 points apart. On the test world the cloud fit is still to be
made, and CM1's runs compute with Earth-fitted microphysics of their own.

The charging laws grafted into CM1 were fitted to laboratory measurements. Two of them, Takahashi's and Saunders
and Peck's, disagree on the sign that rimed ice takes in little cloud water, so that in two windows of an earlier
electrified run, made before the ventilation repair and with leakage off, the test world's storms held their charge
in opposite arrangements under the two. Measurements of that sign at the impact speeds of the lunar storms' hail and
graupel, 4--8 and about 1--4~m\,s$^{-1}$, would decide which arrangement the model's lunar storms take, since hail
carried most of the charge under Saunders and Peck's law and graupel under Takahashi's. The flash-rate laws with
which \textcite{braam2022lightning} and \textcite{sergeev2025lightning} estimated lightning on tidally locked planets
were fitted to Earth's storms and set the rate from the cloud-top height or, in Sergeev et al.'s storm-resolving
runs, from the upward flux of graupel and the column's ice water path. At a sixth of Earth's gravity the cloud tops
of six runs with Morrison's microphysics rose to 55--80~km, far above the storms the cloud-top law was fitted on,
while the main electrified run, with the NSSL microphysics, forced from the Earth-orbit state and computing its
flashes from resolved charge, flashed at about a hundredth of Earth's average flash density, part of the difference
possibly from its grid. Tuning such laws on electrified storms resolved at other gravities, an application of the
route Sergeev et al. propose for different planetary atmospheres where observations are lacking, would fit them to
flashes computed from the same laboratory charging laws and from discharge thresholds set for Earth's storms, on
grids fine enough to converge the flash count: a comparison between models at each gravity.

Whether gravity enters a model consistently is a property of the code, tested by similarity runs. Patched, SWAN
passes such a test, reproducing a sixfold gravity scaling of an Earth sea to 0.3\% in wave height where the released
code misses it by up to 5.4\%; the patch, built to keep the scaling at any gravity, has been tested at a sixth of
Earth's on the code paths those tests ran, while Earth's empirical laws of drag, wind input and breaking keep
coefficients fitted at Earth's gravity and ratio of air to water density. For CM1 the pair of runs designed to
measure how far its convection, with gravity-scaled microphysics, departs from Earth's convection stretched sixfold
failed to convect. Morrison's drop-size limits in CM1 stay Earth's: under them, 85\% of the water falling from the
clouds of a box forced from the GCM evaporated on the way down, against 64\% when the same drops fell at Earth's
speeds, and the ground received about a quarter of the GCM's rain. The largest raindrop that survives its fall
scales with the capillary length, 2.46 times longer at a sixth of Earth's gravity: experiments on the largest stable
drops of other media falling through other fluids agree with that capillary-length form within about 20\%, and on
present-day Earth collisions between hydrometeors, in place of the breakup of single drops, set the largest
raindrops \autocite{loftus2021physics}. Runs with Morrison's limits scaled by the capillary length would show, if
the sizes that collisions allow scale as the largest stable drop does, how much of the lunar rain's evaporation its
small drops cause.

Part of the test world's design was set with the model, and so with the Earth fits that remain in it: the shield's
dimming was chosen so that the calibrated GCM settles at the design's 295~K, and holding that temperature on the
corrected curved-air terms and the gas bands took easing it from 5.0 to 3.0\%. With a longwave correction refitted
at the settled state, or cloud fitted to cloud-resolving runs, the dimming that holds 295~K could change again, and
under a lower pressure or another shield the columns would differ from those the calibrations were fitted on.
Although the changes that remove or avoid the three defects contain no coefficient fitted to the test world, away
from Earth's values the repaired codes have run on the test world alone, so the defects' biases on other worlds are
known from the code's arithmetic; runs of released and repaired ExoPlaSim in the configuration of two of the
ROCKE-3D runs of \textcite{way2018climates}, ozone-free planets of nitrogen-dominated air at Earth's insolation and
zero obliquity and eccentricity, rotating once in 16 and 64 days, would measure their joint effect. The radiation and
cloud fits, which moved the test world's climate furthest, would be refitted or replaced on another world of
nitrogen--oxygen air from evidence beyond the GCM: the gas and scattering fits from line-by-line spectroscopy
checked against an independent code, and the cloud from cloud-resolving runs of that world, a second model whose
own drop and ice laws, fitted on Earth, would in turn need testing at that world's gravity.
