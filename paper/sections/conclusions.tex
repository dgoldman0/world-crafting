\section{Discussion and conclusions}

Earth values in four forms and five defects were found in four Earth-built codes adapted to a world that departs
from Earth at once in gravity, column mass, scale height over radius and day, and where a value was repaired, the
kind of repair followed largely from its form: units and constants took general repairs with no coefficient fitted
to the test world, geometry a method computed from the world's own column, and the radiation's fits calibrations to
a line-by-line model. Three of the defects, all in ExoPlaSim, produce little or no error at Earth's values and an
error that grows as a world's rotation slows or its day or tilt departs from Earth's, and by the code's own
arithmetic they bias ExoPlaSim's runs of natural planets that meet their conditions. The convective-cover unit adds
about 0.23 to the convective cover of a synchronous run of TRAPPIST-1e, and the Sun clock sets the Sun back about
33\textdegree\ each model day in a non-synchronous run, on the default solar geometry, of any planet rotating more
slowly than once in 20 days.

On the test world two of the three kept the global mean of the sunlight arriving at the top of the air and moved
that sunlight between places. The Sun clock, which errs in the Sun's longitude alone, keeps every zonal mean of the
arriving sunlight, while the test world's equator received up to 17\% more than its mean in one sector and 8\% less
in another; the orbit default, which put Earth's tilt in place of the configured one, brought Earth's seasons and
summer rain to 60\textdegree, and in a two-year test of the Sun and tilt alone the share of land under half a
millimetre of rain a day rose from 9 to 36\%. The moved sunlight changed the clouds, and through them the global
means: in the same test the clouds' effect in sunlight weakened by 2.4~W\,m$^{-2}$, and the test world ran 0.9~K
warmer while still gaining heat. A modeller finds such an error in maps of sunlight by longitude and of rain by
season, and a comparison of models by their global means can pass it: on two tidally locked planets, the
quasi-global cloud-permitting runs of \textcite{yang2023cloud} covered 27 and 41\% of the sky with cloud and two
GCMs whose cover follows relative humidity, stability and large-scale ascent covered 82--97\%, most of the
difference on the night side and at high latitudes of the day side, while the three models' global-mean surface
temperatures and albedos came out broadly alike.

Changes to the parts fitted to Earth's atmosphere moved the test world's global-mean surface temperature furthest:
the first calibration of the radiation's Earth fits, a Rayleigh multiplier and a weaker water continuum, moved the
first design runs by at least 20~K, and PlaSim's Earth-tuned cloud settings, pushed to the ends of their tests, by
up to about 6~K, against 1.6--2.5~K for the two general repairs measured alone. What a modeller of a new world
needs for each part depends on how far the world's own column determines it. The radiative geometry of tall air is
computed from that column, as the curved-air table was for the test world, whose air in curved shells loses a net
5.8~W\,m$^{-2}$ against the same clear column taken flat with a pseudo-spherical direct beam, while the dynamics
keep a thin shell and constant gravity; Titan's haze, with a scale height of about 80~km, stands a few hundredths
of its radius tall, as the test world's air does, and plane-parallel aerosol codes for Titan's surface hold to
about 65\textdegree\ from the zenith \autocite{vincendon2010spherical}, although Titan's nitrogen--methane
radiation lies outside the adapted models. A scheme built from line-by-line spectroscopy would replace the
radiation's gas fits, the largest of the Earth fits, on any column whose gases, pressures and temperatures that
spectroscopy covers, and the cloud optics would remain fits. Cloud amount would stay a calibration for each world,
to be fitted to cloud-resolving runs of that world; on the test world that fit is a step still to run, and those
runs compute with Earth-fitted microphysics of their own.

Some of those Earth-fitted laws can be tested apart from any one world, in laboratories on Earth or in runs at
other gravities. In two windows of an earlier electrified run, made before the ventilation repair and with leakage
off, the test world's storms took opposite polarities under Takahashi's charging law and Saunders and Peck's, which
disagree on the sign that rimed ice takes in little cloud water; a measurement of that sign at 4--8~m\,s$^{-1}$ in
0.02--0.2~g\,m$^{-3}$ of cloud water between $-5$ and $-16$\,\textdegree C, conditions laboratories reach, would
decide the polarity of the lightning. The flash-rate laws with which \textcite{braam2022lightning} and
\textcite{sergeev2025lightning} estimated lightning on tidally locked planets were fitted to Earth's storms and set
the flash rate from the cloud-top height or, in Sergeev et al.'s storm-resolving runs, from the graupel and ice
aloft. At a sixth of Earth's gravity the cloud tops of six Morrison runs rose to 55--80~km, far above the storms
the cloud-top law was fitted on, while the main electrified run, forced from the Earth-orbit state, flashed at
about a hundredth of Earth's average density, part of the difference possibly from its grid; electrified storms
resolved at other gravities, computed with the same laboratory charging laws, could test those flash-rate laws,
along the route Sergeev et al. propose of explicit charging in cloud-resolving runs.

Gravity stays fixed in a wave tank or a cloud laboratory on Earth, so a model's dependence on it is tested by
similarity runs. Patched, SWAN reproduces a sixfold gravity scaling of an Earth sea to 0.3\% in wave height, where
the released code missed it by 5.4\% and departs from it on any sea under another gravity, the ancient seas of Mars
among them; the patch, built to keep the scaling at any gravity, has been tested at a sixth of Earth's on the code
paths those tests ran, and Earth's empirical laws of drag, wind input and breaking remain uncertain at each such
sea's ratio of air to water density. CM1's gravity-scaled microphysics remains untested this way, since the pair of
runs designed for it failed to convect, and its drop-size limits stay Earth's while the largest raindrop that
survives its fall scales with the capillary length, 2.46 times longer at a sixth of Earth's gravity.

By one design's own models, a shield in space that removes the ultraviolet heating the upper air would hold the
Moon's Earth-like air to losses of 0.6--1.4~kg\,s$^{-1}$ over a solar cycle, rates at which removing it would take
60 to 150 billion years, on a body whose escape velocity let its natural atmosphere go, so that gravity, column and
the air's height against the radius depart from Earth's together, and the Moon's tidal lock adds a 29.53-day day. A
designer who lowers the pressure or changes the shield moves the columns off those the calibrations were fitted on,
and would need the calibrations refitted. On natural planets the three defects' biases are known so far from the
code's arithmetic, since away from Earth's values every change made for the test world has run on it alone;
released and repaired ExoPlaSim on Earth-air planets at Earth's insolation, rotating once in 16 and 64 days, two of
the sidereal periods at which \textcite{way2018climates} ran ROCKE-3D, would measure those biases in the climate of
worlds that other studies model and test the Sun-clock and convective-cover repairs there. For a new world whose
gases, pressures and temperatures the line-by-line spectroscopy covers, and whose air stands within a few
hundredths of its radius, the radiative geometry can be computed from the world's own column and, once a scheme is
built from that spectroscopy, the gas absorption as well, while cloud remains to be calibrated for that world
against cloud-resolving runs whose microphysics can be tested in laboratories and by similarity runs.
