\section{Availability, authorship and review}

\textbf{Code and data.} The patch series for ExoPlaSim, CM1 with WRF-ELEC and SWAN, the Fortran written for
CM1's electrified build, the in-house reference and calibration tools with their tests, the notes on each
component's correctness and limits, the defect log, the verification record, and this manuscript's figure
scripts, data extracts and claims register are held in the world-crafting repository, to be published with
the paper's release commit. The case comes from the Terluna project \autocite{goldman2026terluna}, whose
repository at commit 186b294 holds the scenario, the runners, the case set-up and the analyses; the model runs
themselves are held on the project's research drive, and the products derived from them are committed to
Terluna with the hashes of their inputs. The upstream sources are pinned by checksum: ExoPlaSim 3.4.2 from its
source distribution, CM1 release 22.0, SWAN 41.51, SWASH 12.01 and WRF-ELEC at commit \code{e43041b}. The
patches inherit their upstream licences: the GNU GPL version 3 for ExoPlaSim and version 3 or later for SWAN,
CM1's MIT-style licence, and the WRF public-domain notice for WRF-ELEC.

\textbf{Author and assistance.} Daniel S. Goldman set the scenario and its questions, made the decisions
recorded in Terluna's decisions register and is responsible for the content. The adaptations, analyses and
this manuscript were developed with AI assistance. Claude Opus~5.5 (Anthropic), working under the author's
direction, wrote and ran most of the code, patches and analyses in Terluna, where 374 of the 487 commits to
186b294 name it as co-author,\claim{Z01} assembled the world-crafting repository and drafted this text;
subagents of the same model compiled the defect log and the verification record, checked the bibliography and
gathered the sources, refereed the claims, challenged the whole manuscript and reviewed the prose. In Terluna,
OpenAI's Codex reviewed the solar-shield, atmospheric-electricity and three other research branches on
8 October 2026 and planned their merge, an order the author accepted, and wrote notes outside this paper's
subject; by Terluna's records, the July 2026 knowledge bundle from which the scenario's earlier decisions were
recovered, and the editorial controls behind the paper's writing rules, came from the author's conversations
with ChatGPT. Each claim marker in the source names a register entry that traces the claim to a file in one of
the two repositories, a run on the research drive, a web record or a stated calculation, and the paper's check
script confirms that every marker resolves and every listed source exists; numbers repeated in the conclusions
and the next steps refer to the same entries without markers.

\textbf{Competing interests.} The author declares no competing interests. The work is an unfunded personal
project that brings the author no income, and the author's practice is to make public every part of it that its
licences and sources allow.

\textbf{Review history.} A first draft, written section by section on 17 October 2026, received the drafting
assistant's own silent prose passes; two subagents acting as technical referees checked every claim and table
row against its sources and found 94 statements to correct or qualify; and a third, as whole-manuscript
challenger, raised 5 blocking, 11 major and 21 minor findings. The author then read the draft in a
stream-of-thought review, which the author counts as its cadence review, and rejected its framing and much of
its prose. Claude Opus~5.5, under the author's direction, wrote a second draft in answer to that review and to
the challenge, and five fresh reviewer agents read it in a first editorial round, four by section and one across
the whole paper, raising 172 findings, 20 of them blocking. The author, reading the second draft during that
round, found it still centred on the test world; the third draft, written by the same assistant, reframes the
paper on the field and acts on the round's findings. Each round's findings and dispositions, with the referees'
reports and the challenge, are recorded in the repository's review folder as the round closes. Review by people
other than the author, or by a journal, is still to come.
