\section{Availability, authorship and review}

\textbf{Code and data.} The patch series for ExoPlaSim, CM1 with WRF-ELEC and SWAN, the Fortran written for
CM1's electrified build, the in-house reference and calibration tools with their tests, the notes on each
component's correctness and limits, the defect log, the verification record, and this manuscript's figure
scripts, data extracts and claims register are held in the world-crafting repository, to be published with
the paper's release commit. Each claim marker in the source names a register entry that traces the claim to a
file in one of the two repositories, a run on the research drive, a web record or a stated calculation, and the
paper's check script confirms that every marker resolves and every listed source exists; numbers repeated in the
conclusion and the next steps refer to the same entries without markers. The case comes from the Terluna project
\autocite{goldman2026terluna}, whose repository at commit 186b294 holds the scenario, the runners, the case set-up
and the analyses; the model runs themselves are held on the project's research drive, and the products derived
from them are committed to Terluna with the hashes of their inputs. The patches apply to upstream sources pinned
by checksum: ExoPlaSim 3.4.2 from its source distribution, CM1 release 22.0, SWAN 41.51, SWASH 12.01 and WRF-ELEC
at commit \code{e43041b}. As patches to those sources, they inherit their licences: the GNU GPL version 3 for
ExoPlaSim and version 3 or later for SWAN, CM1's MIT-style licence, and the WRF public-domain notice for WRF-ELEC.

\textbf{Author and assistance.} Daniel S. Goldman set the scenario and its questions, made the decisions
recorded in Terluna's decisions register and is responsible for the content. The adaptations, analyses and
this manuscript were developed with AI assistance. Claude Opus~5.5 (Anthropic), working under the author's
direction, wrote and ran most of the code, patches and analyses in Terluna, where 374 of the 487 commits to
186b294 name it as co-author,\claim{Z01} assembled the world-crafting repository and drafted this text;
subagents of the same model compiled the defect log and the verification record, checked the bibliography and
gathered the sources, refereed the claims, challenged the whole manuscript and reviewed the prose. In Terluna,
OpenAI's Codex reviewed the solar-shield, atmospheric-electricity and three other research branches on
8 October 2026 and planned their merge, an order the author accepted, and wrote notes outside this paper's
subject; by Terluna's records, the July 2026 knowledge bundle from which the scenario's earlier decisions were
recovered, and the editorial controls behind the paper's writing rules, came from the author's conversations
with ChatGPT. On 18 October 2026, Codex wrote a fresh conclusion at the author's request; fresh Codex agents
reviewed its prose and evidence in two rounds, followed by Codex's revisions. The author then rejected its lunar
framing, and Codex rewrote it around the model ensemble for exoplanets and constructed worlds, with a third round
of fresh prose and evidence readings and corresponding revisions to the abstract and introduction's purpose
statements. The author accepted the ensemble-centred conclusion, chose \emph{terracompatible}, since renamed
\emph{Gaean}, for the intended class of worlds and requested a terminology and framing check across the
manuscript, with one sentence in the conclusion on the ensemble's prospective extension beyond that class.

\textbf{Competing interests.} The author declares no competing interests. The work is an unfunded personal
project that brings the author no income, and the author's practice is to make public every part of it that its
licences and sources allow.

\textbf{Review history.} A first draft, written section by section on 17 October 2026, received the drafting
assistant's own silent prose passes; two subagents acting as technical referees checked every claim and table row
against its sources and found 94 statements to correct or qualify; and a third, as whole-manuscript challenger,
raised 5 blocking, 11 major and 21 minor findings. The author then read the draft in a stream-of-thought review,
which the author counts as its cadence review, and rejected its framing and much of its prose. Claude Opus~5.5,
under the author's direction, wrote a second draft in answer to that review and to the challenge, and five fresh
reviewer agents read it in a first editorial round, four by section and one across the whole paper, raising 172
findings, 20 of them blocking. The author, reading the second draft during that round, found it still centred on
the test world, so the third draft, written by the same assistant, reframes the paper on the field and acts on the
round's findings. The author read the third draft, judged the rest of the paper to stand for now and rejected its
conclusions as a summary that implied reach to untested worlds. The closing section was rewritten as discussion and
conclusions and read, with the passages revised beside it, in seven further rounds, each by two fresh reviewer
agents, one for prose and the author's direction and one for evidence and scope, with a revision after each; the
sixth of these found that the next steps gave one charging law positive ground strikes where the runs made negative
ones, an error the earlier rounds had passed, and the seventh found nothing blocking, its remaining findings acted
on in the last revision. When the author read the resulting conclusion on 18 October 2026 and rejected it as a
summary of the work, Claude Opus~5.5 rewrote it, and two fresh reviewer agents read the rewrite in a ninth round
before a tenth checked the evidence of its revision. Reading the revision, the author judged it still a summary
and so asked Codex to write the conclusion afresh. The Codex rewrite received prose and evidence reviews in rounds
11 and 12; both evidence reviews found no blocking issue, while the prose reviews prompted a structural revision
followed by local revisions to the opening, paragraph endings and final prospect. Because the author, reading that
revision, judged it still centred on the Moon, Codex replaced it with one that develops the broader model
ensemble. In round 13, the prose review found the conclusion ready for the author's reading and required
alignment of the introduction, while the evidence review required a local qualification of the cloud-calibration
claim. Both findings were acted on. The author read and accepted the revised conclusion, requesting the
terracompatible terminology and the single prospective sentence; Codex then revised the terminology and framing
across the manuscript. Two fresh agents read the whole manuscript in round 14 for prose and physical scope,
including source checks of the revised composition descriptions; both found the requested revision ready for the
author's reading, with one article correction, which was made. Claude Opus~5.5 then checked the revision against
its build, its records and the body of the paper, and corrected three sentences and this account of the review.
The author then judged the statement of where the repaired codes had run, repeated through the paper, to be
defensive, and it was recast as one statement of fact in the section on reach. At the author's direction, three
fresh agents then read the whole paper for its flow alone in a fifteenth round, and its findings were acted on
section by section. In every round the agents read for cadence silently, and each round's findings and
dispositions, with the referees' reports and the challenge, are recorded in the repository's review folder, with
the page-by-page inspection of the built PDF, identified by its hash. Review by people other than the author, or
by a journal, is still to come.
