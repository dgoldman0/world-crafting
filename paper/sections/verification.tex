\section{Verification}

Six kinds of check were made on the repaired codes, in three steps of distance from the code
(Table~\ref{tab:verification}). In the first step, identity and reproduction checks test the code alone: a patch
at its default repeats, bit for bit along the trajectory tested, the arithmetic of the same build before the patch;
the recorded sources are rebuilt byte for byte from the pinned archives; and a run restarted, or repeated on one
thread, writes the same output. In the second, arithmetic and similarity checks test the code against a formula,
a direct solution or itself, a similarity check comparing in dimensionless form a run at scaled gravity, lengths
and times with the original, to test whether gravity enters the code consistently. In the third, calibrations and
benchmarks compare the code with something outside it: the reference model, on the columns a fit was made on and a
few beyond, and a scheme's published behaviour on Earth. The checks of the first two steps concern properties of
the code that hold on any world, although a similarity test runs Earth's empirical laws at the scaled gravity with
their Earth coefficients, which it leaves untested there. Each check of the third step leaves the lunar climate untested in its own way: the
reference is a clear-sky model built on HITRAN lines and the MT\_CKD continuum, a fit holds near its fitted
state, and the benchmarks were run in Earth's air.

\textbf{Identity and reproduction.} Five ExoPlaSim runs, each a model year from one restart, repeat byte for byte
the restart of the same build before the patch, with the patch at its neutral setting: the Rayleigh and
continuum calibration at the released values, the cloud-water and convective-day patches, infrared from space at
zero, and the ice optics off. The first three link back to the released model in sequence, each run against a
build that holds only the patches checked before it. The infrared and ice-optics runs, by contrast, were compared
against builds that already held the Sun clock and the water scaling, and for the ice optics the curved-air, gas-band
and longwave patches as well; those patches were written to reproduce the released code at their off settings
and have yet to be run that way, so the chain from these two checks to the released model closes once they are,
while for the water scaling at its default the identity already follows from arithmetic on this
spectrum.\claim{V01} The recorded sources were checked next, against the pinned upstream archives: applied to them,
the patch series rebuild byte for byte the two patched sources of the deployed ExoPlaSim copy and the Fortran of
the lunar CM1 builds behind the results, eight rebuilt directly and the 45\textdegree\,N rings' build through
sources identical to one of them, while the Earth-gravity benchmark builds were compiled from earlier states of the
runners' edit lists. The GCM runs are traced to the same series through their source hashes: prefixes of the
ExoPlaSim series reproduce the source hashes of 32 of the 34 GCM runs that recorded one; of the states in
Table~\ref{tab:states}, the uncalibrated and first calibrated runs predate source hashing, and the
convective-cover run compiled a draft of its patch whose committed form repeats its first year byte for
byte.\claim{V03} In the last kind of check the runs themselves are restarted or repeated: a GCM year killed a minute
in and resumed repeats the uninterrupted year's restart and every variable of its output, runs of CM1 repeat bit
for bit on one thread, and the Earth supercell, restarted on four threads and on two, repeats its fields and its 41
flashes. In the lunar boxes, however, which ran on several threads, runs part from one another after
3,000--7,400~s, so each box run is one realisation. Table~\ref{tab:verification} lists the other CM1 and SWAN
identity checks.\claim{V02}\claim{H26}

\textbf{Similarity and arithmetic.} Gravity is the parameter of a sea that stays fixed in a laboratory on Earth:
the wind-tunnel tests made for SWAN's runs on the seas of ancient Mars varied the air's density and the wind
\autocite{banfield2015winds}, so a wave model's gravity is checked numerically, by a similarity test. Stock SWAN, run at a
sixth of Earth's gravity with lengths, depths and durations six times longer and frequencies six times lower,
differs from the Earth run it scales by 5.44\% in significant wave height and 3.91\% in mean period; patched, it
differs by 0.303\% and 0.326\%, against a 1\% criterion.\claim{V04} SWASH, the model of the shore, was tested against theory and by the same
scaling: it reproduces the linear dispersion relation within 1.76\% in amplitude and 0.289\% in phase at lunar, four times lunar and Earth gravity, preserves
a linear waveform to 0.0009\% of its amplitude when gravity quadruples and the clock halves, and, under the same
scaling with breaking and a moving shoreline, changes its 98th-percentile run-up by 1.20\%.\claim{V05} On the
code paths these tests ran, Komen growth and whitecapping under a fixed wind, DIA, the wind-input knee and
depth breaking on Cartesian strips started from rest, a lunar wave run is an Earth run scaled in gravity with
Earth's coefficients, while the lunar runs' spherical grids, nests and GCM forcing were checked separately or left
unchecked. CM1's gravity-scaled microphysics, by contrast, remains untested this way, since the pair of runs designed to
measure how far lunar convection departs from Earth's stretched sixfold failed to convect.\claim{V06} The arithmetic
checks, the other half of this step, compared four implementations with the arithmetic they encode
(Table~\ref{tab:verification}); among them, the uneven sunlight of the Sun-clock defect, rebuilt from PlaSim's
solar-position routine alone, correlates with the run's at 1.0000.\claim{V11}

\textbf{Calibration and the reference.} Of the calibrations, the split of sunlight has been tested beyond its
fit, in the version fitted before the gas bands: on eleven columns outside the fit's set it held within 0.6
percentage points rms, and a 303~K column carrying 338~kg\,m$^{-2}$ of vapour, warmer and wetter than any
fitted, fell 0.7 points short, where the scheme before its water recalibration fell 9.7 short.\claim{V07} The
refit with the gas bands and the longwave correction, by contrast, are known through their fit residuals
(Table~\ref{tab:verification}). Whatever its test, every calibration is clear-sky, so PlaSim's clouds remain
uncalibrated, and the accuracy of each is bounded by the reference's own. That accuracy has been checked in part.
The reference's correlated-$k$ emulation reproduces the line-by-line outgoing longwave within 0.4--1.4~W\,m$^{-2}$,
and the line-by-line model has two recorded checks
against other codes, kept as numbers alone, with their harness outside the repository: PyRADS
\autocite{koll2018earth}, for longwave with water vapour alone at constant gravity, and a Monte Carlo calculation
of Rayleigh scattering up to an optical depth of 1.5, where the lunar column's Rayleigh depth is about 4 at
360~nm. Its carbon dioxide, oxygen, collision-induced absorption and sunlight on the lunar column, however, have
yet to be compared with an independent code.\claim{V16} Against the same reference RRTMG, checked and left uncalibrated,
lies within 2.5 points of the line-by-line shares of reflected, air-absorbed and ground-absorbed sunlight at the
site where the rings cross the equator at 0\textdegree~E, and on 990 cloud-free columns of the
45\textdegree\,N ring it cools the air more than the correlated-$k$ model does, by 4.5~W\,m$^{-2}$ in the lowest
1.8~km, 8.5 at 1.8--6.5~km and 10.2 at 6.5--13.7~km.\claim{V08}

\textbf{Benchmarks on Earth.} In the Weisman--Klemp supercell \autocite{weisman1982dependence} the electrified
graft stays within 6\% of CM1's own NSSL scheme in its largest updrafts, within 3.4~dBZ in reflectivity at every
half hour and within 9\% in condensate, although its hail differs by up to a factor of two, the two copies being
different versions of the scheme.\claim{V09} Set beside published runs of the same charging and lightning schemes
\autocite{macgorman2001lightning,kuhlman2006numerically,sun2023aerosol,salinas2021modeling}, the storm flashes
first at 28.5 minutes, where those runs flash first at 27--40 minutes, and its flash rates and median charge per
flash fall inside their ranges, while with WRF-ELEC's ground rule on, 1.1\% of its flashes strike the ground,
against 0.33\% in the run of \textcite{salinas2021modeling}. The storm does, however, separate charge several times
faster than the published storms, a difference traced to cloud ice reaching $2\times10^8$ crystals per kilogram
where droplets freeze homogeneously in 50~m\,s$^{-1}$ updrafts.\claim{V10} The leakage added for the lunar runs has
an Earth benchmark of its own: the conductivity column through which the code leaks charge comes from an ion chain
that, run for Earth at a geomagnetic cutoff rigidity of 3~GV, reproduces Earth's measured fair-weather positive
conductivity \autocite{nicoll2012measurements} within 2--8\% from 5 to 30~km.\claim{V11}

\textbf{Defects of the adaptation.} The patches and runners written for the test world contained defects of
their own, found by these checks, by code review or in the course of the work. Six were removed before the runs
reported here: the GCM runner's first solar spectrum, whose tail held its last value past 2.73~\textmu m, put
86\% of the sunlight in the near infrared and warmed the first run past 320~K;\claim{H28} the first electrified
build discharged once per step after all the step's charging, multiplied memory the scheme had left unset and
made private a variable its threads shared; the first terrain build placed its forcing by a corner column
2.9~km up; and the sky solver's production grid of 114 channels absorbed 58\% too much sunlight above 500~nm with
the Sun overhead, which had made PlaSim look short of its calibration. Four remain in reported runs, two of them
across whole sets of runs. The first channels of the curved-air table, bisected on the whole column alone, put
near-infrared sunlight too high, 19.0 against 10.7~W\,m$^{-2}$ at 66--85~km, and the curved-air and gas-band runs
of Table~\ref{tab:states} ran on them.\claim{H30} Every CM1 case set up before 2026-10-17, and so every CM1 run
reported here, started from soundings that held the vapour of the GCM's top level, near 130~km, constant above it,
and on the 45\textdegree\,N rings, the only cases examined for it, the air began 6--15 times saturated over ice at
140--150~km and every column kept 1.1~g\,m$^{-2}$ of ice above 107~km through both lunar days. The other two bear on
single comparisons. The first lunar gravity patch scaled the NSSL fall speeds and left the ventilation tied to them
at Earth's values, so that lunar rain evaporated 1.3--1.5 times too fast for its size, and a code review found the
omission after the windows of the storm results that compare charging laws had run, though before the main
electrified run began.\claim{H29} Likewise, the 45\textdegree\,N rings with and without the curved-air terms ran on
builds compiled at different optimisation levels, so their 0.013--0.021~K difference falls below what the pair
resolves.\claim{H31}

\textbf{Checks that fell short.} Five checks fell short, and three of the five shortfalls concern reported
results, two of them along the wave chain from the open sea to the shore. SWAN's early growth depends on the time
step: halving it changes the first hours' wave height by up to 33\%, while the 48-hour endpoints move under 0.3\%,
and in the weather runs small seas fail their 5\% time-step criterion where seas above 1~m pass, so the step's
error lies in the sea states below 1~m. At the shore, SWASH's control with irregular input, the kind of input from
which the run-up was computed, misses its 5\% bound, at 5.22\% in height and 6.28\% in period.\claim{V12} The
third shortfall is CM1's: rebuilt at a higher optimisation, CM1 departs from its first build by
0.28--0.32~\textdegree C rms in the air at 2~m within a model day, inside the spread of last-bit nudges, and since
runs on several threads part from one another as well, two box runs that differ in one setting compare as
realisations.
The other two shortfalls were traced or bounded: SWAN with depth breaking off failed its repeatability test by
about 2.5\%, a failure traced to the uninitialised breaker parameter and avoided in every run by keeping breaking
on, and the curved-air table's scattering orders reached their cap in one channel of 282, whose omitted tail is
about $3\times10^{-4}$ of that channel's diffuse light at the ground.\claim{V13} The checks still open are taken
up with the next steps.
