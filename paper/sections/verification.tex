\section{Verification}

Five kinds of check were made, and they reach progressively further from the code
(Table~\ref{tab:verification}). Identity and reproduction checks reach the code alone: a patch at its
default leaves the released arithmetic unchanged along the trajectory tested, and the recorded code and
results can be rebuilt. A similarity check reaches the consistency of the physics, showing that a run at
scaled gravity, lengths and times reproduces the original in dimensionless form, so gravity enters the code
consistently. Calibrations and benchmarks reach outside the code, to the reference model on the columns a
fit was made on and to a scheme's published behaviour on Earth. Each stops short of a lunar climate: the
reference is itself a clear-sky model resting on its spectroscopy, a fit holds near its fitted state,
similarity leaves Earth's empirical laws untested at lunar gravity, and a benchmark runs on Earth.

\textbf{Identity and reproduction.} Five ExoPlaSim runs, each a model year from one restart, repeat the
released model's restart file byte for byte with a patch at its neutral setting: the Rayleigh and
continuum calibration at the released values, the cloud-water and convective-day patches, infrared from
space at zero and the ice optics off. Two more show that the committed convective-day patch equals the
staged code that ran, and that a year killed a minute in and resumed repeats the uninterrupted year's
restart and every variable of its output, its files differing only in 12 bytes of HDF5 header; all seven
restart hashes were confirmed again on 17 October 2026.\claim{V01} In CM1 the tilted-ring patches and the
local-time advection repeat an existing run's first model day bit for bit, single-thread runs repeat, and
the electrified supercell, restarted on four threads and on two, repeats its fields and its 41 flashes.
Patched and stock SWAN at Earth's gravity write identical output.\claim{V02} Applied to the pinned
upstream archives, the patch series rebuild every deployed source byte for byte, and prefixes of the
ExoPlaSim series reproduce the source hashes of 32 of the 34 GCM runs that recorded one; the other two
compiled drafts of a patch, and the committed form of one draft repeats its run's first year
exactly.\claim{V03}

\textbf{Similarity.} The similarity checks carry the strongest statements about gravity. Stock SWAN, run
at a sixth of Earth's gravity with lengths, depths and durations six times longer and frequencies six
times lower, misses its Earth twin by 5.44\% in significant wave height and 3.91\% in mean period; patched,
by 0.303\% and 0.326\%, against a 1\% criterion, with the largest difference in peak period one spectral
bin.\claim{V04} SWASH reproduces the linear dispersion relation within 1.76\% in amplitude and 0.289\% in
phase at lunar, four times lunar and Earth gravity, preserves a linear waveform to 0.0009\% of its
amplitude when gravity quadruples and the clock halves, and with breaking and a moving shoreline changes
its 98th-percentile run-up by 1.20\%.\claim{V05} These checks make each lunar wave run the gravity-scaled
image of an Earth run with the same Earth-fitted coefficients, and whether those coefficients hold on
lunar seas stays open. CM1's gravity-scaled microphysics lacks such a test: the pair of runs designed for
it failed to convect.\claim{V06}

\textbf{Calibration.} The calibrations hold on the columns they were fitted to and a little beyond. The
clear-sky split of sunlight holds within 0.6 points rms on eleven columns of the corrected run outside
the fit's set, and at 0\textdegree~E the air takes 31.4\% of the arriving sunlight against the line-by-line
model's 31.1\%; a 303~K column carrying 338~kg\,m$^{-2}$ of vapour, outside the fit, falls 0.7 points short
where the uncalibrated scheme fell 9.7 short.\claim{V07} The gas bands fit within 0.13\% of each band's
energy and the layers within 0.97~W\,m$^{-2}$ rms, and the longwave correction leaves 1--5~W\,m$^{-2}$ from
column to column, within 2.5~W\,m$^{-2}$ in every band of latitude. RRTMG, checked and left uncalibrated,
lies within 2.5 points of the line-by-line shares of reflected, air-absorbed and ground-absorbed sunlight at
the equatorial crossing, and on 990 cloud-free columns of the 45\textdegree\,N ring cools the air more than
the correlated-$k$ model, by 4.5~W\,m$^{-2}$ in the lowest 1.8~km, 8.5 at 1.8--6.5~km and 10.2 at
6.5--13.7~km.\claim{V08} Every calibration is clear-sky, and PlaSim's clouds remain uncalibrated.
The reference bounds them all: its correlated-$k$ emulation reproduces the line-by-line outgoing longwave
within 0.4--1.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ and the 15~\textmu m heating of carbon dioxide within about 5\% up to 5~Pa, and
the line-by-line model rests on HITRAN2020 and MT\_CKD.

\textbf{Benchmarks and arithmetic.} The electrified graft was benchmarked on Earth. Against CM1's own NSSL
scheme in the Weisman--Klemp supercell \autocite{weisman1982dependence}, its largest updrafts stay within 6\%, its reflectivity within
3.4~dBZ, its condensate within 9\% and its graupel within 12\%; hail differs by up to a factor of two, the
two copies being different versions of the scheme.\claim{V09} Against published runs of the same charging
and lightning schemes, the first flash comes at 28.5 minutes against a published 27--40, flash rates and
charge per flash fall within the published ranges, and 1.1\% of flashes strike the ground; the charging
runs several times stronger, traced to cloud ice reaching $2\times10^8$ crystals per kilogram where droplets
freeze homogeneously in 50~m\,s$^{-1}$ updrafts.\claim{V10} The conductivity column reproduces Earth's
measured fair-weather conductivity within 2--8\% from 5 to 30~km. Arithmetic checks rebuild the uneven
sunlight of the Sun-clock defect from PlaSim's solar-position arithmetic with correlation 1.0000, place a
tilted ring's Sun within $8\times10^{-7}$ of the spherical formula and its Coriolis parameter within
$3\times10^{-12}$~s$^{-1}$ of $2\Omega\sin\phi$, match the field solver to a direct solve to one part in
$10^5$, and hold the curved-air ledger's channels within 0.9~W\,m$^{-2}$ of the fine spectral grid in every
layer.\claim{V11}

\textbf{Checks that fell short.} SWAN with depth breaking off failed its repeatability test by about 2.5\%,
which exposed the uninitialised breaker parameter. SWAN's early growth depends on the time step: halving it
changes the first hours' wave height by up to 33\%, while the 48-hour endpoints move under 0.3\%, and small
seas in the weather runs fail their 5\% time-step criterion where seas above 1~m pass. SWASH's control with
irregular input misses its 5\% bound, at 5.22\% in height and 6.28\% in period.\claim{V12} CM1 rebuilt at a
higher optimisation departs from its first build by 0.28--0.32~\textdegree C rms in the air at 2~m within a
model day, inside the spread of last-bit nudges, and on several threads it parts from itself. The ledger's
scattering orders reached their cap in one channel of 282, whose omitted tail is about $3\times10^{-4}$ of
that channel's diffuse light at the ground.\claim{V13}

\textbf{Checks still missing.} The missing checks mark where the results stand least supported. CM1 has yet
to be forced from the longwave-corrected GCM, so whether the corrected physics closes the gap between the
two models is untested. The longwave correction, fitted at 295~K, ran in a test that settled
near 307~K with twice the water vapour, and its accuracy there is unchecked. The 45\textdegree\,N twilight
pair lacks a control on the same build and an estimate of its realization noise. The Sun clock and the
water scaling lack a runtime identity run, the second holding analytically for this spectrum. RRTMG's
extrapolation below its tables, CM1's thread race and its gravity-scaled microphysics remain untested on
their own, and an independent GCM has yet to run the scenario.\claim{V14}
