\section{Earth values and defects in the released codes}

ExoPlaSim's default orbit is one of the Earth values and defects met on the paths the lunar runs take through
the four codes (Table~\ref{tab:inventory}). What a repair of an Earth value needs follows from its form: a
dimensional constant needs the world's own value or a law that gives it, a unit of time the world's own unit, a
geometry a method computed for the world's own air, and a fit or simplification a refit or a scheme that holds
over the world's range. Each value is right at Earth's ratios and errs as a world departs from them along the
ratio it depends on, some steadily and some past a threshold, so the inventory reads as well as a list of what
to check on another world. The defect log in the code repository records each entry's discovery and evidence,
with its repair and measured effect where these exist, and adds the entries found off the lunar runs' paths and
the adaptation's own defects.

Gravity, the commonest constant, enters the codes as a number, a length and a frequency. As a number it is
9.81~m\,s$^{-2}$ in four places on the lunar paths of CM1 release 22, its dynamics, the conversion of pressure to
layer mass in RRTMG \autocite{iacono2008radiative}, its CAPE diagnostic and the Morrison microphysics, as well as
in schemes those paths leave unselected, and 9.8, set twice, in WRF-ELEC's NSSL scheme, which also ties the
ventilation of evaporating rain and depositing ice to Earth's fall speeds.\claim{H01}\claim{H02} As a length it
sets the decay of CCM3's profile of cloud water \autocite{kiehl1996description}, from which PlaSim diagnoses its
clouds' water, on a geometric height scale of $700\ln(1+\mathrm{PW})$~m written for Earth's gravity: with every
pressure level six times higher, clouds above about 20~km, most of the lunar cover, were left nearly dry, and under
a gravity stronger than Earth's the same profile leaves cloud too wet aloft.\claim{H18}\claim{W02} As a frequency
it is written into SWAN as the 1~Hz knee above which the code reduces its linear wind input, a value its source
comments call mostly arbitrary, and because the knee stays at 1~Hz while gravity changes, stock SWAN misses a
sixfold gravity-similarity test by up to 5.44\% in significant wave height; with that linear input switched on, SWAN's
sea at any gravity other than Earth's, the ancient seas of Mars among them, departs through that constant from the
scaled image of an Earth sea wherever its frequency band reaches past the lower of the two knees,
$g/g_\mathrm{E}$~Hz under weaker gravity and 1~Hz under stronger.\claim{H04} The other constants have the scale of
Earth's air, storms, composition and rotation. WRF-ELEC caps a breakdown field scaled with the air's density
\autocite{dwyer2003fundamental} at 180~kV\,m$^{-1}$, a cap that binds on Earth only below about 4.5~km, beneath
where flashes start, and in lunar air below about 36~km, where it lowers the field by 44\% at the ground, and it
declares a ground strike when a channel reaches air warmer than $-7$\,\textdegree C, about 5~km above Earth's
ground and 27--34~km above the Moon's;\claim{H03} SWAN fixes the air's density at 1.28~kg\,m$^{-3}$, against a mean
of about 1.40 in CM1's air over the equatorial seas;\claim{H05} and RRTMG takes carbon dioxide from the calendar
year, with Earth's trace gases and ozone, while CM1's beta plane derives its gradient from Earth's rotation and
radius.\claim{H06}

Earth's day enters as a unit of time in the calendars. ExoPlaSim's year of 360 days in 17,280 half-hour steps
holds 12.08 lunar model days of 1,430 steps, so output kept by model time slips 120 steps a year against the
model day, and with it against the released code's Sun.\claim{H08} CM1's Sun follows Earth's 24-hour day,
Earth's calendar and an obliquity of 23.5\textdegree, and its five soil layers, 1--16~cm thick, resolve Earth's
diurnal thermal wave, which under the month-long day reaches about 0.6~m, below the deepest of
them.\claim{H09}

PlaSim and RRTMG treat each column as a stack of flat layers lit only while the Sun stands above that column's
horizon, a geometry that suits air a tenth of a percent of the radius tall. Against the same air in flat layers,
with a pseudo-spherical direct beam for the sunlight, the test world's air in curved shells absorbs
10.3~W\,m$^{-2}$ more sunlight and sends 16.2~W\,m$^{-2}$ more infrared to space, a net loss of
5.8~W\,m$^{-2}$, the sum of a 6.5~W\,m$^{-2}$ loss through the widening of the shells and a 0.7~W\,m$^{-2}$ gain
from the curved light and escape; the ground receives 1.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ of twilight while the Sun is below the
horizon.\claim{H11} PlaSim stretches the low Sun's water path by Earth's horizon magnification,
$35/\sqrt{1+1224\mu^2}$ with $\mu$ the cosine of the solar zenith angle
\autocite{lacis1974parameterization,rodgers1967use}, where the Moon's factor is 13.5 for water vapour and 7.1
for well-mixed air (Figure~\ref{fig:slant}).\claim{H12} The dynamical cores use the same flat or thin geometry,
PlaSim's a thin shell and CM1's a flat slab, both with gravity constant with height, while the reference models
compute with $g = GM/r^2$; lunar gravity is 5.4\% lower at the air's mass-mean height and 15.3\% lower at CM1's
150~km top.\claim{H13} PlaSim's ten $\sigma$ levels are set at fractions of the surface pressure chosen for
Earth's air, and in the design case they put the top three layers above 107~km, at 81--107~km and at 63--81~km,
each spanning about 25~K, with ice saturation changing 55-fold across the second, and leave two layers for the
lowest 6.5~km, too few to resolve the boundary layer's day--night shape.\claim{H33}

The radiation schemes were fitted to Earth's columns and Earth's Sun, and the test world's columns lie far
outside both. PlaSim's fits erred first in the column's totals, then in its split of sunlight, then in the
heights at which it absorbs and cools. In the totals, ExoPlaSim normalises its Rayleigh coefficient to the
ultraviolet of a 5772~K blackbody, which for the shield-filtered Sun gives a coefficient too low, and PlaSim's
water continuum over-absorbs at long paths; uncalibrated, the first design run, with 25\% seas under the
undimmed shield, passed 310~K and was still warming at 315.5~K in model year 12.\claim{H15} In the split, Lacis
and Hansen's near-infrared water absorptivity, fitted to Earth's water paths over a stated range that ends at
10~cm of precipitable water \autocite{lacis1974parameterization}, levels off toward half the sunlight; over the
test world's paths, from 17~cm upward, it left the air absorbing 7.7--8.2 percentage points too little of the
arriving sunlight on eleven columns.\claim{H14}\claim{S08} By height, PlaSim's two shortwave bands omit carbon
dioxide and oxygen, whose lines absorb sunlight high in a column 7.25 times Earth's, so once a water multiplier
was calibrated on the column's total, the water absorbed their share lower down; and PlaSim's longwave,
Sasamori's broadband absorptivities \autocite{sasamori1968radiative} with a water continuum that the first
calibration fitted to the outgoing flux, cools the lowest two layers 9.7--11.9~W\,m$^{-2}$ too much against the
reference model's correlated-$k$ emulation and the air aloft up to 12~W\,m$^{-2}$ too little.\claim{H16}\claim{H17}
PlaSim treats all cloud as liquid, giving it liquid water's optics and reckoning saturation over liquid at every
temperature, so in the design case the high cloud in its second layer from the top, at 180--204~K, forms only at
136--149\% of ice saturation; it forms cloud in each layer above a relative-humidity threshold set for Earth,
0.983 in the lowest layer.\claim{H19}\claim{G16} In CM1, RRTMG's reference pressures begin at 1,053.63~hPa, and
the lowest 7.5~km of air over the lunar sea, at up to 1,216~hPa, lie beyond them and are extrapolated in
$\ln p$; its ice optics end at 140~\textmu m, a size that slowly falling lunar ice outgrows, and the electrified
box stopped at day 6.45 when its microphysics passed 157~\textmu m to the radiation.\claim{H20}

Below the clouds, the laws of particles, seas and charge were fitted to Earth's data. The fall-speed laws $V =
aD^b$ of the Morrison and NSSL schemes, and their caps, were fitted at Earth's gravity; since gravity's part in
each fall speed follows from the balance of weight and drag, a scaling law extends them to another
gravity.\claim{H01} Morrison's scheme as implemented in CM1 \autocite{morrison2005new} also bounds the mean
diameter of rain at 2.8~mm and begins breakup in rain's self-collection above a mean of 0.3~mm, after
\textcite{verlinde1993fitting}, values set for Earth's rain, while the largest drop that survives its own fall
scales with the capillary length $\sqrt{\sigma/\rho_w g}$, 2.46 times longer at lunar gravity.\claim{H21} The wave
and storm laws are Earth's calibrations as well: Wu's drag \autocite{wu1982wind}, Komen's growth and whitecapping
\autocite{komen1984existence}, the AGROW coefficient \autocite{cavaleri1981wind}, a breaker index of 0.73
\autocite{battjes1985calibration}, SWASH's breaking thresholds of 0.6 and 0.3, and WRF-ELEC's laboratory charging
laws \autocite{saunders1998laboratory,takahashi1978riming}, between which the lunar storms' arrangement of charge
turned over in two windows of an earlier run.\claim{H22} One simplification holds for storms of a few hours:
WRF-ELEC conducts no charge through clear air, its screening option acting only at cloud edges, and in a month-long
lunar run the charge that evaporating cloud handed to the small ions persisted for weeks and held the field at the
ground near 20~kV\,m$^{-1}$ across the whole domain.\claim{H10}

Five defects in the released codes contradict their own inputs, units or intent. Three of them, all in
ExoPlaSim, produce little or no error at Earth's values and an error that grows as a world departs from them,
two through units of time and one through a constant. In a non-synchronous run on its default solar geometry,
PlaSim's solar-position routine turns the Sun at the planet's rotation rate and restarts its clock every model
day, so each day ends with the Sun skipped back by the excess of the model day over the rotation period: none at
ExoPlaSim's default rotation of one day, about 1\textdegree\ for a rotation near Earth's set away from exactly one
day, 10\textdegree\ for a ten-day rotation, 16\textdegree\ from 12 to 20 days and 33\textdegree\ for any slower
rotation, for which ExoPlaSim's wrapper sets twelve model days to the year. On the Moon the skip of
32.5\textdegree\ left sunlight along the equator 8.3\% short from 270\textdegree\ to 57\textdegree~E and up to
17\% over near 164\textdegree~E (Figure~\ref{fig:sunclock}).\claim{H23} Slingo's
convective cover, $0.245 + 0.125\ln P$ in the form PlaSim's manual gives it
\autocite{slingo1991response,lunkeit2012plasim}, with $P$ the convective rain in millimetres per day, converts
the rain with the model's solar day, a conversion ExoPlaSim's developers left in place when they replaced the
solar day by 86,400~s in its unit conversions \autocite{paradise2022exoplasim}; with a model day of 29.8 Earth
days the formula reads lunar rain as about 30 times heavier than it is and adds $0.125\ln 29.8 = 0.42$ to the
cover, up to its cap of 0.8, and on any world it adds $0.125\ln(\text{day}/1\,\text{d})$.\claim{H07} The third
puts an Earth constant in place of a configured value: ExoPlaSim's default orbit setting silently replaces the
configured obliquity with that of Earth's orbit for the model's starting year from Berger's series
\autocite{berger1978long}, 23.7\textdegree, which on the Moon put 149~W\,m$^{-2}$ of sunlight on 86\textdegree\ of
latitude against the Moon's 29 and brought Earth's seasons and summer rain to 60\textdegree, while on Earth it
moves the tilt by a fraction of a degree.\claim{H24} The Moon's slow rotation and small tilt made all three
large. The errors of the other two are independent of the world. SWAN reads its breaker parameter uninitialised
whenever depth-induced breaking is off, and divides by its square root in the fraction that sets the spectrum's
limiter, so repeated Earth runs differed by about 2.5\% in wave height; its usual configuration, with breaking on,
assigns the parameter before the read.\claim{H25} In the lunar boxes, runs of CM1 on several threads part from
one another after 3,000--7,400~s, first in the domain's total vertical momentum, in the plain Morrison build as
in the electrified one; since every patch in a threaded region writes only its own points, the race lies in
CM1's own threaded loops, where it remains unlocated.\claim{H26} A sixth fault, of uncertain cause, makes PlaSim's
lowest model level read up to 20~K too cold in 0.8\% of three-day windows, most likely through the spectral
transform over steep terrain.\claim{H27}
