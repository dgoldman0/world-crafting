\textbf{What each repair changed.} The first calibration moved the climate furthest: the uncalibrated run
passed 310~K and was still warming at 315.5~K in model year 12, while the same configuration, calibrated,
settled at 295.5~K, at least 20~K cooler.\claim{H15}\claim{G14} Figure~\ref{fig:repairs} sets the later
repairs' effects on the global-mean surface temperature beside the sensitivity tests, each pair of runs
averaged over set years and several of them still drifting. On the first calibrated run, counting cloud water
by pressure warmed the Moon by 2.3~K over the years averaged and about 2.5~K as the run settled, and counting
convective rain per Earth day warmed it by a further 1.8~K, about 1.6~K settled.\claim{G01} From the
pre-correction run, the Sun clock, the Moon's own tilt and the recalibrated split of sunlight warmed the Moon
by only 0.6~K, yet cut its rain from 2.91 to 2.24~mm per day and raised the share of land receiving under
0.5~mm per day from 9 to 42\%.\claim{G02} The curved air then cooled it by 0.65~K, its clouds offsetting in
the first year 3.5 of the 5.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ that the geometry removed from the clear sky, and the carbon dioxide
and oxygen bands cooled it by a further 2.2~K, mostly through PlaSim's high cloud, since a cloud-free column
moves its ground only 0.57~K: the high deck fell from 16 to 12\% of the sky, and the top layer's cirrus from
2.0 to 0.05\% as that layer warmed by 7~K.\claim{G03} With the ledger's corrected terms and the dimming eased
from 5.0 to 3.0\%, the design case settles at 295.0~K, with 2.31~mm of rain a day.\claim{G04}

The Earth fits that remain in PlaSim's clouds and longwave move the Moon further than any general repair did.
Two of PlaSim's Earth-tuned cloud settings, varied as tests from the Sun-and-tilt run, move it as far as the
general repairs do or further: thinner convective cloud warms it by 1.7~K over the years averaged and about
2.2~K as the runs settle, and more low cloud cools it by 5.0~K and about 5.9~K, while cloud water at its
full-column bound cools the first calibrated run by 3.2~K and about 3.5~K. The longwave correction with the
ice optics, the one change that moves the cooling by height, warms the design case by 11.5~K over years
10--19, toward a balance near 307~K with twice the water vapour, 348 against 170~kg\,m$^{-2}$, and cloud over
38\% of the sky in place of 20\%, most of the gain in high cloud.\claim{G05} Given the reference's
spectroscopy, the case's climate is therefore least certain in its cloud amount and in the height at which its
longwave cools the air, both set by Earth fits that cloud-resolving runs, the reference model and an
independent GCM can constrain. The 307~K state is a sensitivity, resting on a correction used beyond the 295~K
at which it was fitted.

\textbf{The gap between the models.} CM1 and the GCM disagree near the ground, and forcing CM1 from the GCM
allowed the disagreement to be taken apart. On the 45\textdegree\,N ring forced from the design case, the
lowest 1.8~km of CM1 ran 5.9~K warmer than the GCM over the second lunar day; imposing the GCM's transport
across the circle, about 15~W\,m$^{-2}$ of heat carried out of the air below 8~km, cut the offset to 3.7~K
(Figure~\ref{fig:gap}).\claim{G06} PlaSim's longwave over-cooling of its own lowest layers is estimated at
about 1.5~K of the rest, from a 6~W\,m$^{-2}$ difference at the ring's 4~W\,m$^{-2}$ per kelvin, and the
remainder, a degree or two, is attributed by elimination to the two models' upward transport of the ground's
heat: CM1 mixes its lower air 14--19~km deep by day, where the GCM, with two layers in its lowest 6.5~km, stays
stable. The ring's starting-sounding ice above 107~km adds a term still unquantified. At the site where the
rings cross the equator at 0\textdegree~E, forced from the pre-correction run, which brought 12\% too little
sunlight there, three other suspects were tested and cleared: a three-dimensional box ran warmer still, the
land surface moved the air by at most 0.3~\textdegree C, and rain falling at Earth's speeds warmed the air by a
further 1.0~\textdegree C; at 45\textdegree\,N the dry-ground land test has yet to run.\claim{G07} The warmth
and humidity of the air near the ground, where people and plants would live, are therefore best given as a
range between the two models, 3.7~K wide in the lowest 1.8~km at 45\textdegree\,N with the transport imposed.

Rain shows a larger disagreement. A small box forced from the pre-correction run brought 34~mm to the ground
per lunar day, where the GCM that forced it rained 129~mm and the Sun-and-tilt run 180~mm; at lunar gravity 85\%
of the water falling from the box's clouds evaporated on the way down, against 64\% when the same drops fell at
Earth's speeds.\claim{G08} The rain of each model rests on Earth fits of its own, a convection scheme in the
GCM and Earth's drop limits in CM1, and rain at a place in the rings changes by a factor of several with the
slice's direction, while temperature, humidity and wind agree within about a degree and 0.3~m\,s$^{-1}$
between crossing rings.\claim{G15}

\textbf{Storms and lightning.} At lunar gravity the 0\,\textdegree C and $-40$\,\textdegree C levels stand
five to six times higher than in Earth's tropics, near 25 and 60~km, and cloud tops reach 55--80~km.\claim{G09}
The main electrified run, two lunar days of a box at 0\textdegree~E forced from the pre-correction run, made
530 flashes, a density over the box of 0.022 per square kilometre per year, about a hundredth of Earth's
average. Up to a fifth were negative ground strikes, counted under WRF-ELEC's rule with the leader's crossing
as an upper bound, and none was positive. Each ground strike lowered a median 149~C, where Earth's lower
5--30~C, while in-cloud flashes neutralised a median 108~C. The charge takes Earth's arrangement at five to six
times its height: flashes start a median 35.8~km up, and the main negative charge lies at 40--42~km, at $-13$
to $-14$\,\textdegree C, just above the $-7$\,\textdegree C level, as on Earth. Hail, which rimes faster than
the lunar graupel, does 87\% of the non-inductive charging.\claim{G10}

Two of the storm results depend on choices that a measurement or a theory could settle. Under Takahashi's
charging law \autocite{takahashi1978riming} in place of Saunders and Peck's \autocite{saunders1998laboratory}
the storms separate as much charge with their polarity inverted, so the sign of the lightning depends on
laboratory laws that disagree on the sign rimed ice takes in the little cloud water of the lunar storms; and
with the NSSL microphysics in place of Morrison's the same box runs 1.0~\textdegree C warmer at 2~m and its
night fog at the ground covers 29\% of the box in place of 56\%, so the lunar night fog depends on the
microphysics by about a factor of two.\claim{G11}

\textbf{Seas.} Forced by sixty days of the Sun-and-tilt run's surface stress, SWAN raises big, slow waves under
the light lunar winds: the same wind, fetch and duration build waves 4--5 times as high as on Earth, with
periods five times as long. Over the nearside sea, 5.29 million~km$^2$, the mean significant wave height is
1.39~m, 68\% of area and time stands at or above 1~m, and the largest sea reaches 4.9~m with a 19.3~s mean
period. Lunar waves keep Earth's steepness and travel at 0.41 of the speed of an Earth wave of the same length,
and SWASH runs them up today's steep slopes to a 98th-percentile run-up of 2.1~m on the exposed western face of
the eastern Smythii headland.\claim{G12} These wave results come with Earth's laws for wind input, whitecapping
and breaking scaled to lunar gravity by similarity.
