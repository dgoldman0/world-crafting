\section{Results on the test world}

Run with these repairs, the codes gave results of two kinds on the test world: sensitivities, the change in its
climate as Earth values were removed one repair at a time or varied where they remain, and the climate that CM1, SWAN and SWASH compute when
forced from the GCM. Table~\ref{tab:states} lists the GCM states behind both, by the names used below, and the
state that forced each CM1 and wave result.

\textbf{What each repair changed.} The first calibration cooled the climate more than any later change. In
the first design runs, with 25\% seas under the undimmed shield and with PlaSim's Sun clock and Earth's orbit, the
calibrated configuration settled at 295.5~K, at least 20~K below the uncalibrated run, which was still warming at
315.5~K in model year 12.\claim{H15}\claim{G14} The later repairs' effects on the
global-mean surface temperature are plotted beside the sensitivity tests in Figure~\ref{fig:repairs}, each pair of
runs averaged over the years of Table~\ref{tab:states}, several of them still drifting. On the first calibrated
run, still in Earth's seasons, counting cloud water by pressure warmed the test world by 2.3~K over the years
averaged and about 2.5~K as the run settled, and counting convective rain per Earth day warmed it by a further
1.8~K, about 1.6~K settled.\claim{G01} From the Earth-orbit state, run with both cloud repairs, four changes
were made in one step: the synodic
clock, a general repair; a year of whole model days, an avoidance in the output's bookkeeping; the Moon's own tilt,
an avoidance by configuration; and the recalibrated split of sunlight, a calibration. Together they warmed the test
world by 0.6~K and cut its rain from 2.91 to 2.24~mm per day, and the land receiving under 0.5~mm per day grew from
9 to 42\%, and to 36\% with the Sun and tilt alone in a two-year test, since with Earth's 23.7\textdegree\ tilt
summer rain had reached 60\textdegree.\claim{G02} The Sun and tilt changed the global means through the clouds: in a
two-year test of the two alone, with the Earth-orbit state's split of sunlight, the clouds' effect in sunlight
weakened from $-15.7$ to $-13.3$~W\,m$^{-2}$ and the planetary albedo fell from 0.293 to 0.285, so that the test
world ran 0.9~K warmer over the two years while still gaining 1.9~W\,m$^{-2}$ at the top of its air.\claim{G17}
From the Sun-and-tilt state, the curved air, on the table's first channels, cooled it by 0.65~K, its clouds
offsetting in the first year 3.5 of the clear sky's 5.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ loss under those terms. On the same channels
the carbon dioxide and oxygen bands cooled it by a further 2.2~K over years 10--19, still cooling 0.3~K a decade,
mostly through PlaSim's high cloud: computed cloud-free, the column's ground temperature changes by 0.57~K with the bands, while the
high deck fell from 16 to 12\% of the sky and the top layer's cirrus from 2.0 to 0.05\% as that layer warmed by
7~K.\claim{G03} The corrected terms have been run only together with the bands and the eased dimming, in the 295~K design: to hold
its design temperature on them, the dimming was eased from 5.0 to 3.0\%, and at that dimming the state settles at
295.0~K, with 2.31~mm of rain a day.\claim{G04} In the refined state the GCM's convection, levels, surface
exchange, rain evaporation and cold cloud were changed together, the longwave correction was refitted near 301~K and
the design temperature was set at 301~K, near which the state settles at a dimming of 6.6\%, with 300.8~K and
2.00~mm of rain a day over years 10--19.\claim{G18}
Three of its changes were measured singly, each on a baseline of its own: the cold cloud's factors cooled the
climate by 2.8~K and cut its rain by 19\%, the clear share of the curved air's terms cooled it by about 1~K, and
Beljaars's gust in PlaSim's transfer would settle it about 0.7~K warmer.\claim{G19} Turned by its seas and lakes, at
the same sunlight, the test world runs 0.80~K warmer, mainly because its clouds cool it less, while its global rain
and the share of its land in drylands hold, the near side's north growing wetter and the southern highlands drier;
with the longwave correction refitted on that climate and the dimming increased to 7.6\%, the design case settles at
301.2~K over years 10--49, with 1.97~mm of rain a day.\claim{G24}

Changing two of PlaSim's Earth-tuned cloud settings from the Sun-and-tilt run, each to an end chosen to test an
explanation of the gap between the models, moves the global mean as far as the general repairs do or further:
counting convective cover per 2,400~s, which holds the convective cloud near its floor, warms the test world by
1.7~K over the years averaged and about 2.2~K as the run settles, and lowering the lowest layer's cloud threshold
from 0.983 to 0.6 cools it by 5.0~K and about 5.9~K, while cloud water at its full-column bound cools the first
calibrated run by 3.2~K and about 3.5~K.\claim{G05}\claim{G16} Ranked by form, then, the largest effects on the test world were those of the fits: the first calibration of the radiation's fits, used beyond the range they were
fitted over, moved the global mean by at least 20~K, the cloud's Earth-tuned settings, pushed to the ends of
these tests, by up to about 6~K, and the cold cloud's factors, a calibration of the cloud's fits, by 2.8~K, while the two general repairs of units and constants measured singly moved it by
1.6--2.5~K and the geometry's curved-air terms, on the table's first channels, by 0.65~K. A change to the cooling by height moves the global mean more than any cloud setting: the longwave correction with the ice optics, a
calibration and a general repair switched on together and the one change fitted to the cooling by height, warms
the 295~K design by 11.5~K over years 10--19, toward a balance near 307~K, a sensitivity reached with a correction
fitted at 295~K, with the warmer run's water vapour over years 15--19 twice its baseline's, 348 against
170~kg\,m$^{-2}$, and its cloud covering 38\% of the sky in place of 20\%, most of the gain in high
cloud.\claim{G05} Given the reference's spectroscopy and these tests, the test world's climate is least certain
in its convection, its cloud amount and the height at which its longwave cools the air, all set by schemes and fits
open to constraint by cloud-resolving runs, the reference model and an independent GCM.

The fits' effects are of the order of the spreads measured in intercomparisons of exoplanet models. The radiation
codes themselves disagree on moist air: five band codes used in GCMs and two line-by-line codes, on clear-sky
moist adiabats with surface temperatures of 250--360~K, differ by up to 60~W\,m$^{-2}$ in the shortwave and, in
outgoing longwave, by amounts that grow with the surface temperature to 10--20~W\,m$^{-2}$
\autocite{yang2016differences}. Among whole GCMs the spread widens when the cases include water and cloud: four GCMs on the same
cases of TRAPPIST-1e spread in global-mean surface temperature by 7 and 6~K in dry air dominated by nitrogen and by
carbon dioxide \autocite{turbet2022thai}, and by 14 and 24~K with water and cloud \autocite{sergeev2022thai},
while standard GCMs diverge by 20--30~K on a tidally locked planet under an M star, against 8~K or less on a
rapidly rotating one under a G star, differences their authors trace to cloud, radiative transfer and their
interplay with the dynamics \autocite{yang2019simulations}. Parameterized and resolved cloud have been set side by
side on other worlds. \textcite{yang2023cloud} compared the cover of two GCMs that parameterize it, as PlaSim does,
with resolved cloud on two tidally locked planets, TRAPPIST-1e and K2-72e: CAM3's cloud covered 94.8 and 81.7\% of the sky and ExoCAM's 90.4 and 97.4\%, their cover computed from relative humidity, stability and large-scale ascent, against
26.9 and 41.1\% in a quasi-global cloud-permitting model, which counts cloud by a condensate threshold and ran over
sea surfaces fixed from a coarser run of the same model. The planetary albedos were closer, however, 22.1--29.1 and
39.0--47.0\% in the GCMs against 18.1 and 37.4\%, and the authors judged the three models' climates broadly
similar.

\textbf{The gap between the models.} CM1 and the GCM disagree in their rain and their upper air, a disagreement
measured in boxes forced from the refined state's years 10--19 through the whole column against a test set before
they ran: at each box the rain within 10\% of the GCM's over a lunar day, at most 15\% of it in a different
quarter of the lunar day, the processes agreeing as the test's rules set them, and every change made to either
model to close a difference justified by evidence apart from the comparison.\claim{G20} Over the Near-side Sea,
over the land at 0\textdegree~E and over the semi-arid inland of the near side's south-east, the boxes rain
82--84\% of the GCM's rain, so none passes. Over the land CM1 rains at the GCM's hours, whereas over the sea
22--41\% of its rain falls in a different quarter of the lunar day, its evening rain short, and at every site its
air at 40--60~km runs 3.5--10~K colder than the GCM's.\claim{G21} Carrying the GCM's own weather within each lunar
day, the sea box follows the GCM's lunar days, with a correlation of 0.91, and still rains 17.5\% less, so the
shortfall lies in the two models' physics; there CM1 evaporates 0.69 of its falling rain and the GCM
0.51.\claim{G22} The colder upper air comes mostly from the mixing in CM1's updrafts, which dilute at about
$2\times10^{-4}$ per metre where Earth's thermal structure, translated to the Moon's scales, implies
0.25--0.66$\times10^{-4}$, and which of the two free tropospheres the Moon would have stays open.\claim{G23}

CM1's rain depends on the slice as well: in the rings forced from the Sun-and-tilt state the direction of the slice
alone changes the rain at a place by a factor of several, while temperature and wind at that place agree between
crossing rings within about a degree and 0.3~m\,s$^{-1}$.\claim{G15} Those rings, like the boxes behind the storm
results below, were held toward the GCM from 8~km and ran with CM1's density fault, so their results are
conditional on both. \textcite{song2022idealized}, who ran a cloud-resolving model in two dimensions across the day
and night sides of a tidally locked aquaplanet, likewise found it far from a GCM in cloud water path and cloud width,
though qualitatively consistent with it in the Walker circulation and the substellar clouds.

\textbf{Storms and lightning.} Over the second lunar day of six Morrison runs, three boxes at 0\textdegree~E forced
from the Earth-orbit state, one of them with Earth's fall speeds, and three rings forced from the Sun-and-tilt
state, the 0\,\textdegree C and $-40$\,\textdegree C levels stand near 25 and 60~km, five to six times their
heights in Earth's tropics, and cloud tops reach 55--80~km.\claim{G09} In the main electrified run, two lunar days
of a box at 0\textdegree~E forced from the same Earth-orbit state, the charge takes Earth's arrangement at five to
six times its height: flashes start a median 35.8~km up, and the main negative charge lies at 40--42~km, at $-13$
to $-14$\,\textdegree C, just above the $-7$\,\textdegree C level, as on Earth, while hail, which rimes faster than
the lunar graupel, does 87\% of the non-inductive charging. Its 530 flashes come to 0.022 per square kilometre per
year over the box, about a hundredth of Earth's average, 44 flashes a second \autocite{christian2003global} or about 2.7 per square
kilometre per year;
in-cloud flashes neutralised a median 108~C, and the ground strikes, all negative and at most a fifth of the
flashes under the condition for ground strikes, lowered a median 149~C. Part of the difference from Earth may result from the grid, since the box's 6~km columns correspond to about 1~km at Earth's scale and on the Earth benchmark the storm made fewer, larger flashes on a coarser grid.\claim{G10} Where the lunar runs separate charge and compute each flash, two exoplanet studies estimate lightning with laws fitted to Earth's storms, whose tops stand far below the lunar runs' 55--80~km: the flash-rate laws with which
\textcite{braam2022lightning} estimated lightning on Proxima Centauri~b with nitrogen--oxygen air, and
\textcite{sergeev2025lightning} on TRAPPIST-1e with oxygen-free nitrogen air at 0.25--10~bar, set the rate from the
cloud-top height \autocite{price1992simple} or, in Sergeev et al.'s storm-resolving runs, from the upward flux of
graupel at $-15$\,\textdegree C and the column's ice water path \autocite{mccaul2009forecasting}.

Two of the storm results depend on the choice of a law or a scheme. In two windows of an earlier electrified run,
days 10.5--12 and 40.5--42, made before the ventilation repair and with leakage off, the storms under Takahashi's
charging law \autocite{takahashi1978riming} held the arrangement of their charge inverted, the main negative charge
above the positive, against the normal arrangement under Saunders and Peck's \autocite{saunders1998laboratory}, in
WRF-ELEC's default form with Brooks et al.'s critical rime accretion rate above $-15$\,\textdegree C
\autocite{mansell2026wrfelec}. Even so, they separated about as much charge, 30 against 16--26~kC in the first window and
140 against 138--145~kC in the second, differing from the paired run by less than the Saunders and Peck runs differ
among themselves; which arrangement the storms take therefore depends on laboratory laws that disagree on the sign
rimed ice takes in the little cloud water of the lunar storms. The second result depends on the scheme: with the
NSSL microphysics in place of Morrison's, the main electrified run over its second lunar day is 1.0~\textdegree C warmer at 2~m than Morrison's box at the
same site and forcing, one realisation against another, and its night fog at the ground covers 29\% of the box in
place of 56\%, so the lunar night fog depends on the microphysics by about a factor of two.\claim{G11}

\textbf{Seas.} In the Sun-and-tilt run the GCM's surface stress over the seas corresponds to light winds, 2.6~m\,s$^{-1}$ at 10~m at the
median over the equatorial seas. Light as these winds are, under Earth's laws for wind input, whitecapping and
breaking, scaled to lunar gravity by similarity, and with Wu's drag law at both gravities, the same 10~m wind, blowing over the same fetch for the same duration, builds waves 4--5 times as high as on Earth, with periods five times as long, whereas with
Charnock's roughness at each gravity the lunar drag would be larger and the ratio higher, by about a tenth to a
half. Forced by sixty days of the
Sun-and-tilt run's surface stress, the first lunar cycle a spin-up and the second measured, SWAN computes a mean
significant wave height of 1.39~m over the nearside sea, 5.29 million~km$^2$, with 68\% of its area and time at or
above 1~m, and its largest sea reaches 4.9~m with a 19.3~s mean period. The waves are as steep as Earth's. At the
shore, computed by SWASH from SWAN's spectra, they run up today's steep slopes to a 98th-percentile run-up of 2.1~m on
the exposed western face of the eastern Smythii headland.\claim{G12}
