\section{Results on the test world}

Run with these repairs, the codes gave results of two kinds on the test world: sensitivities, the change in its
climate as Earth values were removed one repair at a time or varied where they remain, and the climate that CM1, SWAN and SWASH compute when
forced from the GCM. Table~\ref{tab:states} lists the GCM states behind both, by the names used below, and the
state that forced each CM1 and wave result.

\textbf{What each repair changed.} Fitting the first calibration cooled the climate more than any later change. In
the first design runs, with 25\% seas under the undimmed shield and with PlaSim's Sun clock and Earth's orbit, the
uncalibrated run passed 310~K and was still warming at 315.5~K in model year 12, while the same configuration,
calibrated, settled at 295.5~K, at least 20~K cooler.\claim{H15}\claim{G14} The later repairs' effects on the
global-mean surface temperature are plotted beside the sensitivity tests in Figure~\ref{fig:repairs}, each pair of
runs averaged over the years of Table~\ref{tab:states}, several of them still drifting. On the first calibrated
run, still in Earth's seasons, counting cloud water by pressure warmed the test world by 2.3~K over the years
averaged and about 2.5~K as the run settled, and counting convective rain per Earth day warmed it by a further
1.8~K, about 1.6~K settled.\claim{G01} From the Earth-orbit state, which holds both cloud repairs, four changes
were made in one step: the synodic
clock, a general repair; a year of whole model days, an avoidance in the output's bookkeeping; the Moon's own tilt,
an avoidance by configuration; and the recalibrated split of sunlight, a calibration. Together they warmed the test
world by 0.6~K and cut its rain from 2.91 to 2.24~mm per day, and the land receiving under 0.5~mm per day grew from
9 to 42\%, and to 36\% with the Sun and tilt alone in a two-year test, since with Earth's 23.7\textdegree\ tilt
summer rain had reached 60\textdegree.\claim{G02} The Sun and tilt changed the global means through the clouds: in a
two-year test of the two alone, with the Earth-orbit state's split of sunlight, the clouds' effect in sunlight
weakened from $-15.7$ to $-13.3$~W\,m$^{-2}$ and the planetary albedo fell from 0.293 to 0.285, so that the test
world ran 0.9~K warmer over the two years while still gaining 1.9~W\,m$^{-2}$ at the top of its air.\claim{G17}
From the Sun-and-tilt state, the curved air, on the table's first channels, cooled it by 0.65~K, its clouds
offsetting in the first year 3.5 of the clear sky's 5.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ loss under those terms. On the same channels
the carbon dioxide and oxygen bands cooled it by a further 2.2~K over years 10--19, still cooling 0.3~K a decade,
mostly through PlaSim's high cloud: computed cloud-free, the column's ground moves 0.57~K with the bands, while the
high deck fell from 16 to 12\% of the sky and the top layer's cirrus from 2.0 to 0.05\% as that layer warmed by
7~K.\claim{G03} The corrected terms have run only together with the bands and the eased dimming, in the design
case: holding the design temperature on them took easing the dimming from 5.0 to 3.0\%, and at that dimming the
design case settles at 295.0~K, with 2.31~mm of rain a day.\claim{G04}

Changing two of PlaSim's Earth-tuned cloud settings from the Sun-and-tilt run, each to an end chosen to test an
explanation of the gap between the models, moves the global mean as far as the general repairs do or further:
counting convective cover per 2,400~s, which holds the convective cloud near its floor, warms the test world by
1.7~K over the years averaged and about 2.2~K as the run settles, and lowering the lowest layer's cloud threshold
from 0.983 to 0.6 cools it by 5.0~K and about 5.9~K, while cloud water at its full-column bound cools the first
calibrated run by 3.2~K and about 3.5~K.\claim{G05}\claim{G16} Ranked by form, then, the largest effects on the
test world came from the fits: the first calibration of the radiation's fits, used beyond the range they were
fitted over, moved the global mean by at least 20~K, and the cloud's Earth-tuned settings, pushed to the ends of
these tests, by up to about 6~K, while the two general repairs of units and constants measured singly moved it by
1.6--2.5~K and the geometry's curved-air terms, on the table's first channels, by 0.65~K. Parameterized and resolved
cloud have been set side by side on other worlds. \textcite{yang2023cloud} compared the cover of two GCMs that
parameterize it, as PlaSim does, with resolved cloud on two tidally locked planets, TRAPPIST-1e and K2-72e: CAM3
covered 94.8 and 81.7\% of the sky and ExoCAM 90.4 and 97.4\%, their cover following relative humidity, stability
and large-scale ascent, against 26.9 and 41.1\% in a quasi-global cloud-permitting model, which counts cloud by a
condensate threshold and ran over sea surfaces fixed from a coarser run of the same model. The planetary albedos
lay closer, however, 22.1--29.1 and 39.0--47.0\% in the GCMs against 18.1 and 37.4\%, and the authors judged the
three models' climates broadly similar. A test of the cooling by height moves the global mean more than any cloud
setting: the longwave correction with the ice optics, a calibration and a general repair switched on together and
the one change that moves the cooling by height, warms the design case by 11.5~K over years 10--19, toward a
balance near 307~K, a sensitivity reached with a correction fitted at 295~K, with the warmer run's water vapour over
years 15--19 twice the design case's, 348 against 170~kg\,m$^{-2}$, and its cloud covering 38\% of the sky in place
of 20\%, most of the gain in high cloud.\claim{G05} Given the reference's spectroscopy and these tests, the test world's climate is least certain in its cloud amount and in the
height at which its longwave cools the air, both set by Earth fits open to constraint by cloud-resolving runs, the
reference model and an independent GCM.

\textbf{The gap between the models.} CM1 and the GCM disagree near the ground, and with CM1 forced from the GCM
the disagreement was taken apart term by term. On the 45\textdegree\,N ring forced from the design case, the
lowest 1.8~km of CM1 ran 5.9~K warmer than the GCM over the second lunar day; imposing the GCM's transport across
the circle, about 15~W\,m$^{-2}$ of heat carried out of the air below 8~km, cut the offset to 3.7~K
(Figure~\ref{fig:gap}).\claim{G06} Two further terms are radiative. Against the correlated-$k$ model, PlaSim's
longwave cools its lowest layer 10.6~W\,m$^{-2}$ too much on cloud-free columns at 47\textdegree\,N, and RRTMG
cools CM1's lowest 1.8~km 4.5~W\,m$^{-2}$ too much on the ring, so the GCM sheds about 6~W\,m$^{-2}$ more than
CM1, worth about 1.5~K of the remaining 3.7~K at the ring's estimated sensitivity of 4~W\,m$^{-2}$ per kelvin.
A third term, from the ice that the ring's starting sounding left above 107~km, remains unquantified. Three other
explanations were tested at the site where the rings cross the equator at 0\textdegree~E, in boxes forced from
the Earth-orbit state, which brought 12\% too little sunlight there, and run on several threads, so each pair
compares two realisations: the ring's two dimensions, since a three-dimensional box ran warmer still; the land
surface, which moved the air by at most 0.3~\textdegree C; and the slow fall of lunar rain, since rain falling at
Earth's speeds warmed the air by a further 1.0~\textdegree C. At 45\textdegree\,N, however, the dry-ground land
test has yet to run.\claim{G07} The remainder, a degree or two, is attributed by elimination to the two models' upward
transport of the ground's heat: CM1 mixes its lower air 14--19~km deep by day, where the GCM, with two layers in
its lowest 6.5~km, stays stable. Under the design-case forcing, with the longwave correction off, the two models
bracket the warmth of the air where people and plants would live, the lowest 1.8~km at 45\textdegree\,N, within
3.7~K once the transport is imposed; about 1.5~K of the bracket is the GCM's over-cooling beyond CM1's, so the bracket
measures the models' disagreement in that one GCM state.

Beyond the temperature near the ground, the models differ on rain, by a factor of about four. A small box forced
from the Earth-orbit state brought 34~mm to the ground per lunar day, where the GCM that forced it rained 129~mm and
the Sun-and-tilt run 180~mm, with 85\% of the water falling from the box's clouds evaporating on the way down at
lunar gravity, against 64\% when the same drops fell at Earth's speeds.\claim{G08} Each model, moreover, computes
its rain from Earth fits of its own, a convection scheme in the GCM and Morrison's Earth-fitted limits on raindrop
size and breakup in CM1. CM1's rain depends on the slice as well: in the rings forced from the Sun-and-tilt state
the direction of the slice alone changes the rain at a place by a factor of several, while temperature and wind at
that place agree between crossing rings within about a degree and 0.3~m\,s$^{-1}$.\claim{G15}

\textbf{Storms and lightning.} Over the second lunar day of six Morrison runs, three boxes at 0\textdegree~E forced
from the Earth-orbit state, one of them with Earth's fall speeds, and three rings forced from the Sun-and-tilt
state, the 0\,\textdegree C and $-40$\,\textdegree C levels stand near 25 and 60~km, five to six times their
heights in Earth's tropics, and cloud tops reach 55--80~km.\claim{G09} In the main electrified run, two lunar days
of a box at 0\textdegree~E forced from the same Earth-orbit state, the charge takes Earth's arrangement at five to
six times its height: flashes start a median 35.8~km up, and the main negative charge lies at 40--42~km, at $-13$
to $-14$\,\textdegree C, just above the $-7$\,\textdegree C level, as on Earth, while hail, which rimes faster than
the lunar graupel, does 87\% of the non-inductive charging. Its 530 flashes come to 0.022 per square kilometre per
year over the box, about a hundredth of Earth's average of 44 flashes a second \autocite{christian2003global};
in-cloud flashes neutralised a median 108~C, and the ground strikes, all negative and at most a fifth of the
flashes under the condition for ground strikes, lowered a median 149~C. Part of the difference from Earth may come
from the grid, since the box's 6~km columns correspond to about 1~km at Earth's scale and on the Earth benchmark a
coarser grid gave fewer, larger flashes.\claim{G10} Exoplanet estimates of lightning use laws fitted to Earth's
storms, whose tops stand far below the lunar runs' 55--80~km: the flash-rate laws with which
\textcite{braam2022lightning} estimated lightning on Proxima Centauri~b with nitrogen--oxygen air, and
\textcite{sergeev2025lightning} on TRAPPIST-1e with oxygen-free nitrogen air at 0.25--10~bar, set the rate from the
cloud-top height \autocite{price1992simple} or, in Sergeev et al.'s storm-resolving runs, from the upward flux of
graupel at $-15$\,\textdegree C and the column's ice water path \autocite{mccaul2009forecasting}.

Two of the storm results depend on the choice of a law or a scheme. In two windows of an earlier electrified run,
days 10.5--12 and 40.5--42, made before the ventilation repair and with leakage off, the storms under Takahashi's
charging law \autocite{takahashi1978riming} held the arrangement of their charge inverted, the main negative charge
above the positive, against the normal arrangement under Saunders and Peck's \autocite{saunders1998laboratory}, in
WRF-ELEC's default form with Brooks et al.'s critical rime accretion rate above $-15$\,\textdegree C
\autocite{mansell2026wrfelec}. Even so, they separated about as much charge, 30 against 16--26~kC in the first window and
140 against 138--145~kC in the second, differing from the paired run by less than the Saunders and Peck runs differ
among themselves; which arrangement the storms take therefore depends on laboratory laws that disagree on the sign
rimed ice takes in the little cloud water of the lunar storms. The second result depends on the scheme: with the
NSSL microphysics in place of Morrison's, the main electrified run over its second lunar day is 1.0~\textdegree C warmer at 2~m than Morrison's box at the
same site and forcing, one realisation against another, and its night fog at the ground covers 29\% of the box in
place of 56\%, so the lunar night fog depends on the microphysics by about a factor of two.\claim{G11}

\textbf{Seas.} The GCM's surface stress over the seas corresponds to light winds, 2.6~m\,s$^{-1}$ at 10~m at the
median over the equatorial seas. Light as these winds are, under Earth's laws for wind input, whitecapping and
breaking, scaled to lunar gravity by similarity, and with Wu's drag law at both gravities, the same 10~m wind,
fetch and duration build waves 4--5 times as high as on Earth, with periods five times as long, whereas with
Charnock's roughness at each gravity the lunar drag would be larger and the ratio higher, by about a tenth to a
half. Forced by sixty days of the
Sun-and-tilt run's surface stress, the first lunar cycle a spin-up and the second measured, SWAN computes a mean
significant wave height of 1.39~m over the nearside sea, 5.29 million~km$^2$, with 68\% of its area and time at or
above 1~m, and its largest sea reaches 4.9~m with a 19.3~s mean period. The waves keep Earth's steepness. At the
shore, computed by SWASH from SWAN's spectra, they run up today's steep slopes to a 98th-percentile run-up of 2.1~m on
the exposed western face of the eastern Smythii headland.\claim{G12}
