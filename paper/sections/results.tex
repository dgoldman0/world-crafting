\section{What the adapted models have given}

The repaired models are instruments for the lunar climate, and their first products are sensitivities: how
far each Earth assumption, once removed, moves the climate, and where the uncertainty that remains now sits.
Every climate number below is a conditional model result, holding given the scenario and the Earth-fitted
parts each model keeps.

\textbf{What each repair moved.} Figure~\ref{fig:repairs} sets each repair's effect on the global-mean
surface temperature beside the effects of cloud settings that remain Earth's. Counting cloud water by
pressure warmed the Moon by 2.3~K, and counting convective rain per Earth day warmed it by a further
1.8~K.\claim{G01} The Sun clock, the Moon's own tilt and the recalibrated split of sunlight warmed it by
only 0.6~K, yet cut its rain from 2.91 to 2.24~mm per day and raised the share of land receiving under
0.5~mm per day from 9 to 42\%.\claim{G02} The curved air cooled it by 0.65~K, its clouds giving back 3.5
of the 5.4~W\,m$^{-2}$ that the geometry took from the clear sky, and the carbon dioxide and oxygen bands
cooled it by a further 2.2~K, mostly through PlaSim's high cloud, which fell from 2.0 to 0.05\% of the sky
in the top layer as that layer warmed by 7~K.\claim{G03} Held at the design temperature by dimming the
sunlight 3.0\% in place of the earlier 5\%, the corrected Moon settles at 295.0~K, with 2.31~mm of rain a
day.\claim{G04}

The climate is most sensitive where the models remain Earth's. Two of PlaSim's Earth-tuned cloud settings
move the Moon as far as the repairs do: thinner convective cloud warms it by 1.7~K, and more low cloud cools
it by 5.0~K, farther than any repair but one; cloud water at its full-column bound cools it by 3.2~K. The
one repair that moves the cooling by height, the longwave correction with ice optics, warms the Moon by
11.5~K, to a state near 307~K with twice the water vapour, 348 against 170~kg\,m$^{-2}$, and high cloud over 40\% of the sky in
place of 20\%.\claim{G05} Cloud amount and the vertical placement of the longwave cooling therefore carry
the lunar climate's largest open uncertainty, and both rest on fits that only cloud-resolving runs and the
reference model can constrain.

\textbf{The gap between the models.} The cloud-resolving model and the GCM disagree near the ground, and the
forcing methods let the disagreement be taken apart. On the 45\textdegree\,N ring the lowest 1.8~km of CM1
ran 5.9~K warmer than the GCM over the second lunar day; imposing the GCM's transport across the circle,
about 15~W\,m$^{-2}$ of heat carried out of the air below 8~km, cut the offset to 3.7~K
(Figure~\ref{fig:gap}).\claim{G06} PlaSim's longwave over-cooling of its own lowest layers accounts for
about 1.5~K of the rest; what remains lies in how the two models carry the ground's heat upward, CM1 mixing
its lower air 14--19~km deep by day where the GCM's stays stable. Three other suspects were tested and
cleared: a three-dimensional box ran warmer still, the land surface moved the air by at most
0.3~\textdegree C, and rain falling at Earth's speeds warmed the air by a further
1.0~\textdegree C.\claim{G07} Rain shows the larger disagreement. On the GCM's forcing before its corrections, a small box brought 34~mm to the ground
per lunar day where the GCM rained 180~mm, and at lunar gravity 85\% of the water falling from its clouds
evaporated on the way down, against 64\% when the same drops fell at Earth's speeds.\claim{G08}

\textbf{Storms and lightning.} At lunar gravity the storms stand five to six times taller than Earth's: in
every run the 0\,\textdegree C level lies near 25~km and the $-40$\,\textdegree C level near 60~km, and
cloud tops reach 55--80~km.\claim{G09} The lightning they make is rarer and larger
than Earth's. The main electrified run, two lunar days of a box at 0\textdegree~E, made 530 flashes, 0.022
per square kilometre per year, about a hundredth of Earth's average; a fifth were negative ground strikes and
none positive, and each ground strike lowered a median 149~C where Earth's lower 5--30~C, while in-cloud
flashes neutralised a median 108~C. The charge keeps Earth's arrangement at five to six times its height:
flashes start a median 35.8~km up, hail does 87\% of the non-inductive charging, and the main negative charge
sits at 40--42~km, at $-13$ to $-14$\,\textdegree C, just above the $-7$\,\textdegree C level as on
Earth.\claim{G10}

Two of the storm results hang on choices that only measurement could settle. Under Takahashi's charging law
in place of Saunders and Peck's the storms separate as much charge with their polarity inverted, so the lightning's sign rests on laboratory
laws measured at Earth's impact speeds; and with the NSSL microphysics in place of Morrison's the same box
runs 1.0~\textdegree C warmer at 2~m and its night fog at the ground covers 29\% of the box in place of 56\%,
so the lunar night fog depends on the microphysics by about a factor of two.\claim{G11}

\textbf{Seas.} Forced by sixty days of the GCM's surface stress, SWAN finds the light lunar winds raising
big, slow waves: the same wind, fetch and duration build waves 4--5 times as high as on Earth, with periods
five times as long. Over the nearside sea, 5.29 million~km$^2$, the mean significant wave height is 1.39~m,
68\% of area and time stands at or above 1~m, and the largest sea reaches 4.9~m with a 19.3~s mean period.
Lunar waves keep Earth's steepness and travel at 0.41 of the speed of an Earth wave of the same length.
SWASH runs them up today's steep slopes to a 98th-percentile run-up of 2.1~m on the exposed face of the
eastern Smythii headland.\claim{G12} These wave results rest on the corrected design climate as it stood
before the curved air and the gas bands, and on Earth's laws for wind input, whitecapping and breaking.

\textbf{What stays conditional.} The conditions under these numbers differ in weight. The design
temperature of 295~K is a target held by dimming the sunlight; the 307~K state of the longwave
test is a sensitivity resting on a correction used beyond its fit. The warmth and humidity of the air near
the ground, the air people would live in, stand best as a range between the models, 3.7~K wide in the lowest 1.8~km at 45\textdegree\,N.
The lunar rain depends on a convection scheme fitted to Earth in the GCM and on Earth's drop limits in
CM1, the lightning's polarity on a laboratory charging law, and the seas on Earth's wave physics carried to
lunar gravity by similarity.
