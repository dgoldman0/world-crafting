# Prose passes: A breathable Moon as a modelling regime (`sections/regime.tex`)

Reviewer: Claude Opus 5.5, the drafting assistant (self-review). Date: 2026-10-17. Silent reading only.
Line numbers refer to the version before the passes' revisions.

## 1. Bead-string / equal-weight declaratives

- P1 (L3–13) lists the scenario's conditions: an intentional parameter catalogue (G1 allows it), landing on
  "Every climate number below holds given these choices". Recorded as catalogic; kept.
- **L63–64** "The tilt of 1.54° leaves the poles a grazing Sun all year." A bare declarative ending the
  paragraph, unconnected to any strain. **Revised:** "...all year, a regime that ExoPlaSim's orbit default
  would replace with Earth's seasons", which ties the last ratio to the model part it strains, as the
  paragraph's other sentences do.
- P4 (L66–73) pairs each question with the model that answers it, a deliberate catalogue in a repeated
  colon form. Kept; its last sentence revised under pass 7.

## 2. Inheritance

- P2: "ratios" → "air over each square metre" → "scale height" → "The same height ... also strains the
  dynamics", each sentence taking the previous one's quantity. Kept.
- P3 opens "Gravity and the calendar strain everything else", inheriting P2's "strains". P4 opens on "these
  strains". Kept.

## 3. Reorderability

- P3 runs gravity's consequences (heights, fall speeds, roughness, wave speed), then the convective
  similarity that follows from them, then the calendar (day, rotation, tilt). Moving the similarity
  sentence ahead of the gravity sentence loses its premise. Kept.

## 4. Verb and exact action

- **L49** "The same height that tilts the radiation": the height curves the radiation's paths, it tilts
  nothing. **Revised:** "The same height that curves the radiation's paths".
- **L56** "lengthens the roughness of a wind-driven sea ... sixfold": the quantity is the roughness length.
  **Revised:** "sets the roughness length of a wind-driven sea six times longer for the same friction
  velocity".
- **L60** "The 29.53-day solar day reaches the soil's thermal wave ... and every formula that counts its
  quantity per day": "reaches" named no action. **Revised:** "deepens the soil's thermal wave to about 0.6 m
  ... and multiplies nearly thirtyfold whatever a formula counts per day".
- Table 1, L26: "RRTMG's tables, which end at 1.05 bar" was ambiguous about direction. **Revised:** "whose
  highest pressure is 1.05 bar", matching the hidden-Earth section's "begin at 1,053.63 hPa".

## 5. Human and living presence

- **L66** The questions named the air near the ground without saying whose air. **Revised:** "the air near
  the ground, where people and plants would live". The design's purpose (Terluna's living environment)
  enters once, where the questions are set.

## 6. Subject-generated transitions

- No stock transitions; each paragraph opens on a term from its predecessor. Kept.

## 7. Defensive structure and affirmative development

- **L72** "The line-by-line model answers none of these": a negative framing of a model's role.
  **Revised:** "The line-by-line model stands behind them all, holding the radiation every model must
  reproduce."
- L12–13 "they are a design, and other designs would move the numbers": the qualification attaches to the
  claim it bounds (G4). Kept.

## 8. Redundancy and expert-audience deletion

- The text repeats Table 1's column mass, scale height and horizon air mass; each repetition adds the
  consequence (absorber paths, flat-column picture, PlaSim's 35), so kept. The introduction's brief preview
  of the same ratios is kept as motivation.

## 9. Cadence (silent)

- **L45–49** One sentence carried the scale height, the codes' geometry in apposition, the verdict and three
  measured consequences. **Revised** into two sentences: the verdict on the codes' geometry, then the three
  measured consequences. Audible review pending.

## 10. Final-page arc

- The section ends on the questions each model owns, which the results section answers in the same order
  (GCM, CM1, electrified CM1, waves). Consistent.
