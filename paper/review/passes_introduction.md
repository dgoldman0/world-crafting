## 1. Bead-string / equal-weight declaratives

- **L16–18** "Every height in pressure terms stands six times higher, raindrops fall about three times
  slower, and the Sun returns once in 29.5 Earth days." Three coordinated consequences with no cause; the
  first two share a cause (gravity) that the sentence left out. **Revised:** "A sixth of Earth's gravity
  sets every pressure level six times higher and slows falling rain to about a third of its Earth speed, and
  the Sun returns once in 29.5 Earth days." The cause now governs the first two clauses; the calendar stays
  coordinate as the separate ratio it is.
- **P6 (L51–56)** provenance, conditionality, two kinds of agreement, strongest test: a short series of
  methodological statements, kept; it is the disclosure the charter allows here (G2), and it builds to the
  last sentence's test.

## 2. Sentence and paragraph inheritance

- P1: "asks" → "Only numerical models can answer" → "Each lunar number therefore rests on an earlier
  question": each sentence takes the predecessor's term. Kept.
- P2 opens on "that question"; P4 opens on "these models"; P5 on "Earth models have travelled before",
  inheriting "carrying these models". Kept.
- **P3 opening (L22)** "Three model families carry the work" turned from P2's last clause (Earth's own air)
  without taking anything from it. **Revised** to "Three model families divide the work, each answering where
  the previous one stops", which keeps the turn deliberate and states the sequence relation that orders the
  paragraph.
