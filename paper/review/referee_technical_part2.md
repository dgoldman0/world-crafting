- **Method.** Every `\claim` was read against its sources in `claims.json`, and every unmarked technical statement was
  checked against the record. Terluna was read at 186b294 with `git show`: `climate/gcm/README.md` (1,240 lines),
  `climate/crm/README.md` (2,528 lines), the waves records, `atmosphere/electricity/README.md` and

  the data extracts and the pristine upstream sources (ExoPlaSim 3.4.2 `__init__.py` and `plasim.f90`; CM1 r22.0
  `init_surface.F`) were also read. Where a note and Terluna's record disagree, Terluna's record is taken and the row
  says so.
- **Checks made in this pass.**
  - Recomputed all nine `repairs.json` rows from the runs' `progress.json`. They agree to 0.001 K, and the source hashes
    match.
  - Recomputed the cloud-cover means of `A28_corrected_970` and `A28_corrected_970_lw_ice`, and the rms of the
    11-column split from `radiation_check_A28_dim5_moon.json`.
  - Re-hashed the seven identity restart pairs. All are identical and match the record.
  - Byte-compared the `A_kill` and `A_ref` year-1 outputs.
  - Hashed the Fortran (`*.F`) of nine deployed CM1 build folders and compared the builds with each other.
  - Recomputed the scale heights, column masses and Chapman factors in `worlds.json`, and counted Terluna's commits.

  re-applied to pristine source; the deployed builds were compared with each other instead. The netCDF variables were
  not read, so the 39-variable comparison and the 348 kg/m² vapour reading were not repeated. The issue URL behind N02
  was not opened. Two points in F24 and F43 rest on general knowledge outside the record, and the rows say so.

## Most consequential problems

1. **Two numbers that contradict Terluna's record.**
   - F1: at 0° E the line-by-line air takes 31.4%, the same share as the GCM. The paper's 31.1% exists only in this
     repository's verification record.
   - F2: the "high cloud over 40% of the sky in place of 20%" is total cloud cover, 19.8% → 38.3%.

   Both errors also sit in the claims register (V07, G05) and in the notes, so they would propagate.
2. **The deciding charging measurement is misidentified (F3, F4).**
   - next.tex asks for graupel at 1.3–1.5 m s⁻¹, a figure from the Morrison runs, which had no hail.
   - Terluna's record names hail, which does 87% of the charging, striking at 4–8 m s⁻¹ in little cloud water. That
     condition Earth's laboratories can reach.
   - Laboratory data near the graupel speeds already exist and lean towards the inverted sign.
3. **Reproduction is stated more strongly than it was shown (F5, F6, F7, F11).**
   - The identity runs match the build without each patch, not the released model.
   - Five ExoPlaSim patches lack runtime identity runs, not two.
   - The series does not rebuild every deployed CM1 source: the Earth-gravity benchmark builds and the MPI builds
     predate parts of it.
   - The conclusions and abstract say every repair defaults to the released code, contradicting repairs.tex's own five
     exceptions.
4. **The reach section's Sun-clock rule and slow-rotator claim (F8, F9).**
   - ExoPlaSim rounds the rotation count to a multiple of 12 and never below 12. The skip is therefore 33° for every
     rotation slower than 20 days, not 21–60.
   - The model day is 12/11 of the rotation period, which is not the solar day for most slow rotators. "Need only the
     general repairs" is untested.
   - Table 5 also misstates the soil depth (1.55 m, not 80 cm), and the "any world", "any O2" and "other shields" claims
     overreach.
5. **Missing conditions on the CM1 results (F13, F15, F16, F21, F22).**
   - The suspects called "cleared" for the 45° N gap were all tested at the equatorial crossing, on pre-correction
     forcing.
   - The 180 mm GCM rain belongs to the corrected GCM, not the one that forced the box.
   - The main electrified run is on pre-correction forcing.
   - The 3.7 K model range is measured against the GCM before its longwave correction.
6. **Window means of drifting runs reported as effects (F17–F20).** The paper's 2.3/1.8 K split and the 1.7/5.0/3.2 K
   cloud-setting values differ from Terluna's settled values: 2.5/1.6 K and +2.2/−5.9/−3.5 K. The figure mixes four
   baselines without saying so, and it draws a longwave test that is not part of the design case as a repair.
